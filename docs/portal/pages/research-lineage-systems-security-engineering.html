Systems Security Engineering

Systems Security Engineering relates a protected consequence and threat model to actual authority, enforcement, evidence, recovery and residual exposure.

Current scientific findings

The current 60-property account distinguishes whole-system security, reference validation, policy models, capability delegation, supply-chain controls and trustworthy restoration. A credential or checklist cannot establish a security claim without its protected object, enforcing mechanism and relevant adversarial conditions.

Questions the study addresses

  • What exact resource and effect does the real grant authorize?
  • Which claim does a proof, signature, inventory or observation actually establish?
  • Can a bounded recovery close known hostile authority while retaining explicit unknowns?

Identity and authority

Authentication supplies identity information to policy; it does not grant an arbitrary export. A correctly scoped bearer capability can authorize an action without naming a person. A deputy must bind the caller’s requested effect to the authority it actually spends; a filename does not justify unrelated ambient write power.

Adequacy and comparative economy

The r3 P07 correction preserves that one authorized actor with appropriate logging can be adequate in a bounded setting. Reversibility or low consequence alone does not establish that it is cheaper or generally preferable. Transition, delay, privacy, maintenance and recovery costs belong to the same relevant horizon.

Evidence ceilings

A proof can establish a logical invariant while deployment correspondence is unknown. A signature can identify an authorized issuer for a digest while the artifact remains defective. Provenance, inventory, exploitability, severity and installation-specific loss are different claims. Multiple tools sharing one oracle do not supply independent error paths.

Recovery and retirement

Closure of known material grants can support restoration without proving that unknown credentials or persistent compromise are absent. Uptime is not trustworthy restoration. Executable authority, verification material, decryption capability and recovery data have distinct roles; indiscriminate deletion can destroy useful evidence without addressing the real threat.

A supported example and its conditions

An account can be disabled while an offline signed token remains usable inside its permitted expiry window. That consumer-specific fact can matter without requiring an online revocation mechanism for every use. A bounded stateless rebuild with new grants can also close its known domain while broader persistence remains unknown.

Negative findings, uncertainty and limits

Earlier P36/P59 and P07 adverse findings remain attached to earlier subjects. The unresolved S069 empirical aggregate rate is withheld rather than invented. Five optional configurations and four rejected novelty proposals preserve simpler adequate paths; this account supplies no universal security or deployed effectiveness claim.

Existing engineering responsibilities

The receiving record names R002E / #198 as the existing Security/Privacy owner. Its existing evidence, identity, independent-challenge and acceptance routes remain the consumers. A later implementation question should compare the actual grant with the protected effect, configuration and recovery evidence. One complete bounded check can be sufficient without a new security subsystem.

These are preliminary routes for a future exact consumer. Research acceptance does not establish a current native defect, appoint an owner or require implementation. Adequate controls, smaller methods and no change remain legitimate outcomes.

Genealogy

  1. Early whole-system security reports and trusted-system research treated policy, people, physical conditions, communications, hardware and software as one protection problem. Reference-monitor and security-kernel work emphasised always-invoked enforcement, tamper resistance and a small analysable trusted base.
  2. Protection domains, capability systems, confidentiality, integrity and commercial-transaction models, and later role- and attribute-based control supplied different authority mechanisms. They address different objectives; authentication is not authorisation and the models are not stages on one ladder.
  3. Cryptographic systems contributed open design, confidentiality and authenticity mechanisms, and key or signing authority. Mathematical strength remains bounded by endpoints, issuers, key custody, authorisation, build state and recovery.
  4. Software security, secure-development lifecycles and assurance traditions integrated architecture, coding, analysis, fuzzing, formal proof, adversarial challenge, release and response. Each technique covers particular claims and failure hypotheses rather than certifying the whole system.
  5. Vulnerability management and supply-chain security added affectedness, exploitation context, dependency identity, source/build/release provenance, signing and update safety. An inventory, severity score, attestation or signature remains an input, not a trust conclusion.
  6. Identity-centred and zero-trust architectures removed implicit authorisation from network location, while cyber-resilience work extended prevention towards detection, containment, revocation, trustworthy reconstitution and adaptation. Both expose new common-mode dependence on identity, policy, telemetry and recovery planes.

What survives after criticism

Trust boundaries matter only while current authorisation governs the exact effect, and secure failure means choosing a bounded failure direction rather than assuming that denial is always harmless.

The mature form starts with the protected confidentiality, integrity, availability, authenticity, authority, privacy, safety or mission consequence and a bounded account of the attacker or misuse pressure. It then exposes trust assumptions and crossings, minimises authority by scope, duration and parameters, mediates consequential paths using current identity and authorisation, and chooses the smallest set of sufficiently independent evidence and controls that can discriminate the claim.

  • Minimise privilege, common mechanisms, attack surface and blast radius while preserving usable and tested recovery paths.
  • Bind delegated action to the exact resource, parameters, audience, purpose and expiry; capability and identity do not equal permission.
  • Treat secrets, keys and certificates as an authority lifecycle with known consumers, revocation and compromise recovery.
  • Make security continuous wherever a consequential assumption can be introduced or invalidated, with a cheap deterministic path for low-risk changes.
  • Bind assurance to the exact source, build, artefact, configuration, environment, time and threat assumptions; expire it after relevant change.
  • Prioritise vulnerabilities by affected state, exposure, reachability, exploitation evidence, privilege and consequence rather than severity or finding count alone.
  • Prepare to detect, contain, revoke, recover from trusted material and re-establish invariants before reconnecting.

Criticism shifted the tradition from perimeter, vulnerability and compliance programmes towards lifecycle trustworthiness. Perimeters still help containment, but location cannot grant authority. Patching remains necessary, but severity does not equal current risk. Compliance makes obligations auditable, but cannot establish current adversarial effectiveness. Prevention remains essential, but incident evidence shows that identity, signing, build, update and recovery planes can themselves be compromised or become fleet-wide failure modes.

The lineage contains unavoidable tensions. Fail-closed behaviour may prevent unauthorised action or cause unsafe denial. Central policy improves consistency or creates a common mode. Compartmentalisation limits blast radius or multiplies complexity. Rapid patching reduces exposure or destabilises essential service. Telemetry aids detection or harms privacy. Recovery restores availability or reconnects compromised trust. Each tension requires a named consequence, failure direction, bounded hybrid and residual-risk owner.

How IMPLEMENTAUDIT translates it

IMPLEMENTAUDIT translates the security residue into bounded authority and evidence rather than a separate security mode. Repository content is treated as data, not permission. Consequential mutation requires current scope and authorisation; identity, capability and permission stay distinct; external effects require structured post-state readback; stale state refuses; destructive paths require exact targets and recovery or containment; evidence remains bound to the source, package, installed, deployed or public layer it actually observed.

The accepted original v0.4 crosswalk records seven bounded v0.4 changes through the security integration owner: an explicit adversary and threat boundary; visible trust boundaries; attack-surface minimisation; keys and secrets treated as expiring authority; security architecture and design review; secure-by-design and secure-default responsibility; and explicit adjudication where security conflicts with privacy, safety, availability or usability. These compose with existing planning, repository-state, mutation-integrity, independent-review and closure controls. They do not create host-wide mediation, a universal threat worksheet or a claim of system security.

What was already native

IMPLEMENTAUDIT already required explicit scope for destructive work, least necessary authority, exact repository and artefact identity, current evidence, safe failure, rollback or compensation, independent challenge for consequential claims, refusal of stale handoffs, and separation of command success from the intended world effect. It also already kept local validation, package proof, installation, deployment and public readback as distinct claims.

The recorded original v0.4 architecture crosswalk preserves further convergences: small specialised components cannot acquire lifecycle authority; the package remains one identifiable unit with an exact member list; a dependent conclusion is made only after all required current inputs arrive and only one place writes the authoritative result; specialist work receives only its stated scope and authority; correction and recovery remain under the top-level workflow; and model or tool output remains evidence rather than consequential authority.

What was deliberately not imported

No universal security mode, control catalogue, threat-model template, zero-trust programme, fixed two-person rule, annual penetration-test certificate, SBOM mandate, CVSS-as-risk queue, scanner-count target, trust score, training quota or extra audit object was added. Encryption, a signature, an attestation, a clean scanner, a passed audit and a restored service do not become broader conclusions than their evidence supports.

Least privilege does not mean unusable work, complete mediation does not require elaborate policy for public immutable data, and defence in depth does not mean stacking products with the same failure mode. Low-exposure reversible work may need only a named protected consequence, a brief abuse/trust-boundary check, established secure defaults and one high-signal deterministic check.

Tensions, assumptions and evidence limits

Security guidance and mechanism-specific evidence are stronger than whole-programme comparative evidence. Threat models remain subjective and can go stale. Scanners and static analyses have class-specific blind spots and correlated noise. Formal proof is conditional on its specification and trusted base. SBOM tools may disagree, and component presence is not affectedness. Exploit predictions, reachability and telemetry can be wrong or stale. Incident reports expose mechanisms but do not yield universal rates or optimal architectures.

This route does not establish certification, complete mediation outside governed helpers, resistance to a privileged same-principal process, supply-chain provenance for unobserved builders, trustworthy recovery after unknown-depth compromise, universal host isolation or deployment-predictive AI/agent security. Security, privacy, safety, availability and usability remain context-dependent consequences, not one scalar score.

P01 — Explicit protected consequence and security objective
Property id

P01

Property name

Explicit protected consequence and security objective

Historical origin

Ware-era whole-system security analysis and later requirements/risk traditions; broadened beyond confidentiality labels by systems-security engineering.

Lineage class

HYBRID

Original form

State which information, function or mission must be protected and which confidentiality, integrity or availability condition must hold.

Problem it addressed

Controls cannot be judged when the protected object and unacceptable consequence are vague or replaced by generic 'cybersecurity'.

Security objective or protected consequence

Prevention or bounded acceptance of specified mission, service, safety, privacy, financial, legal or human harms caused through loss of required trustworthiness.

Adversary or threat assumption

Any relevant intentional actor or compromised component capable of producing the specified consequence; capability and access must be bounded separately.

Failure mode it tries to prevent

Control accumulation that does not change a consequential adversarial path; protection of the wrong asset; technically secure behaviour that still permits unacceptable mission harm.

Mechanism

Translate stakeholder consequences into security objectives, invariants, misuse consequences, unacceptable states and decision thresholds; trace controls and evidence back to those claims.

Trigger or context

Always for consequential systems, new exposed capabilities, material trust-boundary changes, or when risk acceptance is requested.

Non trigger or cheap path

For a low-exposure, reversible change, record the protected consequence and a brief invariant/abuse check rather than producing a full control catalogue.

Dependencies or preconditions

Named decision owner; system/mission context; asset and dependency inventory sufficient to connect technical state to consequence.

Trust or identity preconditions

Identity claims are relevant only insofar as they affect the protected consequence; anonymous or environmental threats must not be excluded by default.

Authority preconditions

Someone must have authority to declare objectives, tolerances and residual-risk acceptance; implementers cannot silently define them by available tooling.

Configuration or provenance preconditions

The objective must bind to the actual architecture, deployment, data flows and dependency versions to which it applies.

Observability or detection preconditions

Evidence must reveal whether the protected invariant is being approached or violated; otherwise objectives remain aspirational.

Recovery preconditions

Where consequence includes service continuity, recovery criteria and trust-restoration conditions must be stated with the objective.

Expected payoff asserted at this source scope

Prevents security work from optimising findings, controls or scores that do not reduce the harm decision-makers care about.

Decision or consumer

System owner, product owner, security architect, risk acceptor, operator and assessor.

Known failure modes

Assets are listed without consequence; CIA labels are copied mechanically; objectives conflict; risk acceptance has no owner; non-security dependencies are omitted.

Important criticisms

CIA is useful but incomplete as a universal ontology; objectives can be unknowable, contested or dynamic, and overly broad objectives can justify disproportionate surveillance/control.

How the property evolved

From data-classification and access-control policy to explicit mission/business/service consequences, security invariants and lifecycle trustworthiness claims.

Evolved form in this source

A small, current set of consequence-linked objectives with stated tolerance, decision owner, adversary assumptions, measurable evidence and recovery condition.

Ceremony vs property

A risk register, policy statement or control matrix is optional; the property is an actionable, current consequence-to-claim chain.

P02 — Bounded adversary and threat model
Property id

P02

Property name

Bounded adversary and threat model

Historical origin

Early trusted-system work explicitly assumed malicious users and bounded physical, communications and hardware conditions; later attack-tree, misuse-case and threat-modelling traditions operationalised the idea.

Lineage class

THREAT_MODELLING_LINEAGE

Original form

State hostile capabilities, access, objectives and excluded conditions before arguing that a protection mechanism is sufficient.

Problem it addressed

Security evidence is meaningless if the attacker is silently weaker than the real environment or if a named threat is mistaken for a bounded model.

Security objective or protected consequence

Confidence that required objectives remain protected against specified intentional, adaptive and persistent behaviours, including insider and supply-chain positions where relevant.

Adversary or threat assumption

Explicit attacker classes, initial access, privileges, knowledge, resources, persistence, collusion, physical proximity and dependency compromise; exclusions are visible assumptions, not impossibilities.

Failure mode it tries to prevent

Designs that are secure only because a decisive attacker path was omitted; assurance that tests a toy adversary; inability to explain residual risk.

Mechanism

Represent attacker goals and capabilities, trust assumptions, abuse paths and out-of-scope conditions; use the model to choose architecture, controls and assurance.

Trigger or context

When a consequential decision depends on what an attacker can do, at new trust boundaries, after major change, or when relying on a control with known bypass conditions.

Non trigger or cheap path

For low-consequence changes, a short trust-boundary and plausible-abuse check can be sufficient; do not instantiate a full template if it cannot alter a decision.

Dependencies or preconditions

Knowledge of system exposure, dependencies and likely consequence; access to design and operational evidence; an owner for model currency.

Trust or identity preconditions

Compromised identity providers, operators, workloads and suppliers must be considered where within scope; 'authenticated user' is not synonymous with benign user.

Authority preconditions

Authority is required to accept exclusions and residual risk; threat intelligence may inform capability but cannot dictate policy.

Configuration or provenance preconditions

The model identifies the exact configuration, deployment and dependency state being analysed and records change invalidators.

Observability or detection preconditions

Telemetry and incident data should test model assumptions; absence of detection cannot validate absence of attacker capability.

Recovery preconditions

Recovery assumptions must include whether the adversary persists in identities, management planes, update paths or recovery media.

Expected payoff asserted at this source scope

Concentrates engineering depth where credible adversarial paths threaten consequence and exposes assurance claims that depend on hidden exclusions.

Decision or consumer

Architects, threat modellers, assessors, operators, product/security owners and risk acceptors.

Known failure modes

Template taxonomies substitute for analysis; model goes stale; only external attackers are considered; capabilities are unbounded; assumptions cannot be tested; model produces no design change.

Important criticisms

Completeness is unattainable; analyst subjectivity and knowledge gaps are material; formal overhead can cause abandonment; intelligence is biased toward observed campaigns.

How the property evolved

From static lists and one-time diagrams to current, change-aware models that bind assumptions to trust boundaries, decisions, evidence and incident feedback.

Evolved form in this source

A decision-linked, revision-triggered adversary model with explicit exclusions, uncertainty, supplier/identity compromise cases and a lightweight path when consequence is low.

Ceremony vs property

A threat-model document or STRIDE worksheet is not required; a current, reviewable assumption-to-decision relation is.

P03 — Explicit trust assumptions and trust boundaries
Property id

P03

Property name

Explicit trust assumptions and trust boundaries

Historical origin

Trusted-system/reference-monitor research, protection-domain and capability traditions, then distributed security architecture and zero-trust translations.

Lineage class

TRUSTED_SYSTEMS_LINEAGE

Original form

Identify where untrusted subjects cross into protected state and which components, people and environments must behave correctly for enforcement to hold.

Problem it addressed

Implicit trust in network location, administrative domain, process separation, supplier or management plane permits authority to cross without adequate mediation or containment.

Security objective or protected consequence

Integrity, confidentiality, availability and accountable control remain bounded when components, tenants, suppliers or users are hostile or compromised.

Adversary or threat assumption

Adversaries may occupy any untrusted domain and, where modelled, a trusted component; trust is a dependency claim, not a moral judgement.

Failure mode it tries to prevent

An apparently separate component is not an independent boundary; transitive trust expands silently; compromise of a shared control plane defeats many controls.

Mechanism

Map subjects, resources, data flows, authority and enforcement points across process, host, network, tenant, supplier, build and human domains; state what each boundary guarantees.

Trigger or context

New data/authority flow, tenant or supplier relationship, remote administration, agent/tool integration, or concentration of control in a shared service.

Non trigger or cheap path

For simple single-user/offline/reversible work, record the few consequential crossings and rely on existing platform isolation where its assumptions are acceptable.

Dependencies or preconditions

Accurate architecture/data-flow view; enforcement mechanisms whose behaviour is understood; ownership of cross-boundary contracts.

Trust or identity preconditions

Identity assertions are accepted only from stated issuers under bounded proofing/authentication assumptions; identities crossing domains are reinterpreted explicitly.

Authority preconditions

Each boundary specifies which authority may cross, who can change policy and what delegated authority remains downstream.

Configuration or provenance preconditions

Claims bind to deployed topology, runtime configuration, firmware/platform state and supplier provenance; diagrams alone are insufficient.

Observability or detection preconditions

Boundary crossings and policy decisions need enough logging/measurement to reveal misuse or enforcement failure.

Recovery preconditions

Containment and recovery plans identify which boundaries can be re-established and which trust roots/recovery sources remain clean.

Expected payoff asserted at this source scope

Reduces implicit authority, reveals concentration/common-mode risk and makes containment and assurance scopes tractable.

Decision or consumer

Architect, platform/security engineer, service owner, supplier manager, assessor and incident commander.

Known failure modes

Trust diagrams become decorative; labels such as 'internal', 'container' or 'trusted service' replace guarantees; encrypted channels are mistaken for trustworthy endpoints.

Important criticisms

Boundary enumeration can become unbounded; microservice splits may increase attack surface and policy complexity; central identity or policy systems can become catastrophic shared dependencies.

How the property evolved

From perimeter and machine boundaries to resource-, identity-, workload-, supply-chain-, build-, agent- and management-plane boundaries with continuous evidence.

Evolved form in this source

A current authority/data-flow map whose boundaries have explicit guarantees, dependencies, observability, common-mode analysis and reconstitution plan.

Ceremony vs property

A data-flow or zero-trust diagram is optional; the property is explicit, enforced and testable trust partitioning.

P04 — Least privilege and authority minimisation
Property id

P04

Property name

Least privilege and authority minimisation

Historical origin

Saltzer–Schroeder least privilege, capability/protection-domain work, access-control models and modern short-lived/contextual authorization.

Lineage class

ACCESS_CONTROL_LINEAGE

Original form

Give each subject only the privileges needed for the task and only while needed.

Problem it addressed

Compromise or misuse of an over-privileged subject converts one foothold or error into broad consequential control.

Security objective or protected consequence

Reduced unauthorised disclosure, modification, execution, administration and lateral consequence; smaller blast radius after credential or component compromise.

Adversary or threat assumption

Any principal, process, workload, agent or delegated service may be malicious or compromised while holding legitimate credentials.

Failure mode it tries to prevent

Standing or excessive authority permits actions beyond current intent; stale entitlement survives role/task change; shared admin rights defeat attribution.

Mechanism

Minimise scope, resource set, operations, parameters, duration and delegation; separate privilege domains; use just-in-time elevation and explicit exceptions where justified.

Trigger or context

High-consequence actions, multi-tenant/shared systems, automation/agents, remote administration, production access and broadly reusable credentials.

Non trigger or cheap path

For isolated low-consequence work, platform defaults and a coarse role may be cheaper than elaborate fine-grained policy; still avoid unnecessary administrator/root authority.

Dependencies or preconditions

Complete task/authority model; usable elevation and recovery paths; policy enforcement that cannot be bypassed by alternate interfaces.

Trust or identity preconditions

Current subject/workload identity, assurance level and session context must support the granularity claimed.

Authority preconditions

Entitlement grantors and policy administrators are themselves bounded; delegated authority cannot exceed intended scope.

Configuration or provenance preconditions

Entitlements must reflect actual resources, APIs, parameters and deployed policy; reviews compare desired with effective authority.

Observability or detection preconditions

Use, denial, elevation and abnormal privilege paths need visible evidence; stale/unexercised privilege should be discoverable.

Recovery preconditions

Break-glass and account-recovery mechanisms must preserve availability without silently recreating permanent broad privilege.

Expected payoff asserted at this source scope

Limits consequence after compromise and makes authorization reasoning, review and revocation tractable.

Decision or consumer

Resource owner, access administrator, service owner, developer/platform engineer, assessor and incident responder.

Known failure modes

Policies become too granular to administer; users share accounts or build shadow systems; stale roles accumulate; nominal rights differ from effective transitive authority; emergency privilege becomes permanent.

Important criticisms

Least privilege is not 'remove every permission'; excessive friction can damage availability, safety and accountability. Empirical outcome evidence for specific entitlement-review programmes is limited.

How the property evolved

From static role restrictions to task-, parameter-, context- and time-bounded authority with automated expiry, workload identity and usable elevation.

Evolved form in this source

Minimise consequential authority across scope, duration, delegation and parameters while preserving a tested path for legitimate work and emergency recovery.

Ceremony vs property

Periodic access-review completion is not the property; current effective authority and bounded use are.
