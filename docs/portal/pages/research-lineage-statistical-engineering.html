Statistical Engineering

The useful inheritance is disciplined decision-making under measurement error, variation, dependence and uncertainty—not a universal significance threshold, a Six Sigma programme or a quota of repeated green runs.

Two research subjects: the historical public corpus below contains 65 statistical properties and its bounded v0.4 engineering translation. The current statistical lane keeps its later scientific qualification separate. The CSS family package supplies the family links and four bounded receiving outlines.

Genealogy

Statistical engineering has a plural genealogy. At Bell Labs, Shewhart developed statistical control and economic action around manufacturing variation, while Dodge and Romig developed acceptance sampling for bounded lot decisions. In agricultural experimentation, Fisher established randomisation, replication, blocking and factorial designs. Neyman–Pearson and Wald developed error-controlled and sequential decision theory. Metrology separately refined the measurand, calibration, traceability, accuracy, precision and uncertainty; reliability and robust-design traditions addressed lifetime, stress, degradation and nuisance factors.

These strands interacted, but they did not become one fixed toolkit. Quality institutions and management programmes translated parts of them into industrial practice. Six Sigma, emerging later, recombined inherited statistical tools with projects, roles, training and managerial routines; it was not the origin of control charts, experimental design, metrology or reliability. From the 2000s, a broader professional use of “statistical engineering” described the integration of statistical and domain knowledge for complex, unstructured problems. Adaptive digital evaluation, reusable holdouts, dataset-shift studies and online inference are modern translations or convergences, not evidence of one uninterrupted industrial lineage.

Criticism repeatedly separated useful mechanisms from recipes. Autocorrelation, nonstationarity and mixtures can invalidate classical control limits; measurement error can conceal change; large samples can remain biased; repeated observations can be pseudoreplication; a non-significant result is not equivalence; optional stopping and multiplicity can manufacture confidence; and optimised metrics can cease to represent the protected outcome. Taguchi's robustness objective survived criticism of universal signal-to-noise ratios, while Six Sigma's coordination contribution can be considered without importing belt hierarchies or tollgates.

Surviving engineering properties

The starting boundary is measurement-system fitness for the actual decision; the mature discipline also makes proxy resistance explicit rather than trusting a precise but gameable measure.

  • Define the quantity before governing by it. State the decision-relevant property, population, unit, operating conditions, consumer and admissible interpretation before accepting, rejecting or optimising from a metric.
  • Test the evaluator, not only the candidate. Accuracy and bias are different from precision; repeatability is different from reproducibility and both are different from validity. Resolution, drift and uncertainty matter only relative to the decision margin.
  • Model variation and dependence enough to avoid false action. Distinguish routine variation from evidence of change, keep control limits separate from specifications, and do not count correlated reruns, seeds, batches or shared evaluators as independent corroboration.
  • Design the smallest informative contrast. Use representative sampling, randomisation, blocking, replication and factorial structure when they reduce a live ambiguity. Use a direct authoritative check when it already settles the matter.
  • Plan analysis and stopping before the result tempts revision. Report magnitude, uncertainty and engineering consequence; control the real family of repeated looks and alternatives; stop collecting when further information cannot change a capable consumer's action.
  • Protect evaluator integrity. Preserve data and preprocessing lineage, version evaluators and thresholds, detect drift and contamination, and require independent prospective evidence before changing a failed test, fixture, expected answer or decision rule.
  • Keep claims separate. Mean performance, variability, tails, reliability over time, robustness across context and causal transfer are different propositions with different evidence burdens.

Formal statistics is not required when a cheap, current and authoritative deterministic discriminator already answers the question. Nor may a convenient deterministic-looking score take that shortcut if it is an unvalidated proxy, stale oracle or single observation from a stochastic population.

When both candidate and evaluator change

A new pass after a failed candidate and its test both change leaves an attribution question. The historical ledger's P052 preserves the failure and requires independent justification for changing the evaluator. P053 distinguishes product variation from measurement variation. Stable reference cases, an old/new candidate and evaluator comparison, or an authoritative independent instrument can resolve the ambiguity. A direct proof of a deterministic defect may settle it without an experiment.

Other predicates remain separate: P030 concerns assignment and analysis consistent with the actual design; P032 concerns independent experimental units; P036 concerns magnitude, uncertainty and decision margins; P049 concerns the context boundary between held-out performance and deployment validity. Several repeated runs, reviewers or green labels do not automatically satisfy these four different requirements. These examples are grounded in the public statistical ledger.

What it deliberately did not import

A branded programme, artefact count or mathematical guarantee under assumptions is not live evidence that the assumptions hold.

Tensions, boundaries and evidence limits

Measurement investment can delay action and itself alter, destroy or contaminate what is measured. Monitoring can create false alarms, surveillance and over-adjustment. Independence is often hidden by shared code, models, data, prompts, environments or evaluators. Representative sampling and external validity remain difficult when populations drift. Rare-event and tail claims are data-hungry, while accelerated tests depend on the stress-to-use mechanism. Proxy safeguards can themselves become targets.

The frozen evidence is strong for many formal results under stated assumptions, historical transmission, metrology distinctions and mechanism-level cautions. It is weaker for whether a target population satisfies those assumptions, programme-wide Six Sigma effects, universal transfer from manufacturing or reliability to software and agents, and calibrated probabilities for evolving evaluators. Repository fixtures prove only their encoded classifications and mutations. This page does not claim causal improvement, universal statistical power, representative deployment populations or evaluator validity across models and hosts.

P001 — Define the decision-relevant measurand or construct before quantification

Historical origin:

Metrology’s measurand discipline, experimental operationalisation, and later construct-validity/invariance work.

Original form:

State what quantity or property is intended, under which conditions, before assigning a number.

Development:

Evolved from physical quantity definition toward explicit construct, context and decision-use validation.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

No metric governs until its intended property, scope, consumer, and admissible interpretation are fixed enough to be challenged.

Problem:

A precise, reproducible number can answer the wrong question or represent an unstable proxy.

Mechanism:

Bind each metric to a stated measurand/construct, unit of analysis, operating conditions, intended use, and disconfirming observations.

Trigger and context:

Any measurement used to accept, reject, optimise, compare, or monitor an engineering state.

Preconditions:

Domain definition, decision owner, scope, and an explicit mapping from observations to the property of interest.

Measurement preconditions:

The operational definition must preserve the distinctions the decision actually requires; an evaluator must not collapse relevant states.

Assumptions:

The construct is sufficiently coherent for the intended decision and its operationalisation does not change materially across samples.

Decision or consumer:

Design authority, test owner, regulator, operator, or other consumer of the decision.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

When an authoritative deterministic fact directly answers the decision, record that fact rather than inventing a surrogate score.

Disposition at the historical freeze:

STRONGLY_RETAINED

Failure modes:

Ambiguous labels; changing definitions between baseline and candidate; composite scores whose weights hide trade-offs.

Criticism:

Metrology can define a measurand precisely while still leaving broader validity contested; formal latent constructs add model dependence.

Contrary evidence:

Construct-validity evidence is domain-dependent; no universal statistical test establishes that a metric means what stakeholders intend.

Unresolved questions:

How should construct adequacy be stress-tested when the desired property is partly normative or changes with deployment context?

Property versus ceremony:

A template or ontology is optional; the property is the explicit decision-linked definition.

P002 — Establish measurement-system fitness before metric governance

Historical origin:

Eisenhart/NBS calibration-system evaluation, measurement assurance, gauge R&R, GUM and laboratory-quality systems.

Original form:

Characterise the measurement process before treating its outputs as facts about the production process.

Development:

Evolved from instrument calibration and variance decomposition to decision-specific evaluator capability and versioned validation.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Demonstrate enough discrimination for the intended decision, not abstract “good measurement,” and recheck after material evaluator change.

Problem:

Observed variation may be dominated by instrument, operator, environment, fixture, algorithm, or evaluator variation.

Mechanism:

Estimate bias, repeatability, reproducibility, resolution, stability and uncertainty relative to the decision margin; block use when discrimination is inadequate.

Trigger and context:

Metrics near thresholds, comparisons whose expected effect is small, multi-operator/evaluator systems, or costly decisions.

Preconditions:

Defined measurand, representative items/conditions, reference or comparison basis, and a stated decision margin.

Measurement preconditions:

The study must span the real operating range and relevant operators, instruments, environments and preprocessing paths.

Assumptions:

Measurement error is reasonably estimable over the intended range and the study itself has not selected unusually easy samples.

Decision or consumer:

Anyone relying on a metric, threshold, trend or experiment outcome.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

For a deterministic binary check with an authoritative oracle and negligible ambiguity, verify the oracle and use it directly rather than perform full MSA.

Contrary evidence:

P003 — Calibration and metrological traceability where decision-relevant

Historical origin:

Physical metrology, calibration hierarchies, VIM/GUM and ISO/IEC 17025.

Original form:

Development:

Problem:

Use suitable standards/reference methods, record calibration state and uncertainty contributions, and maintain traceability at the result level.

Trigger and context:

Assumptions:

Decision or consumer:

Laboratory, manufacturer, customer, regulator and downstream analysts.

P004 — Keep bias/accuracy distinct from precision

Historical origin:

Classical measurement error, NBS measurement evaluation, ISO 5725 and VIM.

Original form:

Separate systematic displacement from random dispersion and estimate each against a reference/use case.

Development:

Modern practice reports trueness/bias, precision and uncertainty rather than one undifferentiated “accuracy” score.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Decision-specific decomposition of systematic and random error, with correction only when validated.

Problem:

Highly repeatable measurements can be systematically wrong; averaging reduces random error but not shared bias.

Mechanism:

Compare to suitable references/alternatives for bias while estimating dispersion under repeatability and reproducibility conditions.

Trigger and context:

Whenever magnitude or direction relative to truth/standard matters, especially near limits.

Preconditions:

Reference value or defensible triangulation, representative operating range and stable calibration/evaluator state.

Measurement preconditions:

Enough resolution and coverage to detect both offset and dispersion; uncertainty in the reference included.

Assumptions:

Bias is reasonably stable or modelled over range/context; reference is more informative than the system under test.

Decision or consumer:

Measurement owner and decision consumer.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

If the decision is invariant to a known constant offset and only ranks within one stable system, bias correction may be unnecessary—but must be explicit.

P005 — Distinguish repeatability, reproducibility and validity

Historical origin:

Interlaboratory measurement science, ISO 5725, gauge R&R, rater reliability and reproducibility debates.

Original form:

Estimate same-condition variation separately from operator/site/instrument/context variation.

Development:

Evolved from instrument precision to interlaboratory, computational and evaluator-version reproducibility, while retaining validity as a separate burden.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Claim only the level of stability actually tested, and never infer validity from repeatability alone.

Problem:

A result may repeat under one setup but fail across competent operators or contexts; both may repeat an invalid construct.

Mechanism:

Design crossed/nested comparisons over relevant operators, instruments, sites, times and methods; assess validity separately.

Trigger and context:

Multi-operator, multi-site, automated evaluator, stochastic test or claimed general result.

Preconditions:

Representative factors and levels, randomisation/order control, clear agreement target and adequate item range.

Measurement preconditions:

Measurement outputs are comparable and evaluator versions are fixed/documented during the study.

Assumptions:

Factor effects and interactions can be estimated; repeated conditions approximate intended routine use.

Decision or consumer:

Measurement owner, experimenter and anyone claiming portability.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

For one-use, one-instrument local control with no transfer claim and a wide margin, a limited stability check may suffice.

Fisher’s local control/blocking, factorial design and modern stratified/randomised experiments.

Original form:

Group or balance experimental units on nuisance factors before randomisation/analysis to reduce unexplained variance and confounding.

Development:

Modern practice combines blocking with regression/hierarchical adjustment but retains design-stage protection.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Block what is known and costly, randomise what remains, and report context interactions rather than only an adjusted average.

Problem:

Batch, time, operator, hardware, site or baseline severity can obscure or mimic treatment effects.

Mechanism:

Select a few consequential pre-treatment factors, block/stratify randomisation and estimate block/treatment interactions when relevant.

Trigger and context:

Heterogeneous units or expensive experiments where nuisance variation is large relative to target effect.

Preconditions:

Known pre-treatment covariates, enough units per block or matched-pair structure, and stable measurement.

Measurement preconditions:

Blocking variables are measured before intervention without candidate-induced bias.

Assumptions:

Within-block comparisons remain meaningful; block definitions are not chosen after outcomes.

Decision or consumer:

Experimenter and engineering decision owner.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

Do not block on post-treatment variables or add complexity when units are already homogeneous and the effect is obvious.

P032 — Require independent replication at the experimental-unit level; treat technical repeats as measurement information

Historical origin:

Fisherian replication, hierarchical experimental design and Hurlbert’s pseudoreplication critique.

Original form:

Repeat treatments on genuinely independent units so variability relevant to generalisation is observed.

Development:

Evolved into multilevel models, cluster-robust inference, multisite replication and explicit technical/biological/system-level replication labels.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Count independent decision-relevant units, disclose dependence, and stop treating correlated reruns as corroboration.

Problem:

Many outputs, seeds, subsamples or repeated readings from one unit can look like a large experiment while providing one treatment assignment.

Mechanism:

Define unit/cluster, allocate multiple independent units per condition, model nesting/repeated measures and label technical replication explicitly.

Trigger and context:

Any claim intended to generalise beyond one specimen, run, model instance, batch, site or environment.

Preconditions:

Multiple independent units, provenance showing common sources, and a stable treatment/evaluator protocol.

Measurement preconditions:

Repeated measurement error is separated from between-unit variation; evaluator exposures do not couple units.

Assumptions:

Units are exchangeable/representative enough for intended scope; clusters and interference are modelled.

Decision or consumer:

Experimenter, reviewer and engineering decision authority.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

For deterministic proof or exact exhaustive checks, replication is unnecessary; for measurement precision, technical repeats remain useful but answer a different question.

Trigger and context:

Assumptions:

P063 — More samples, more reruns or repeated green tests automatically mean more truth

Historical origin:

A recurring statistical caricature contradicted by sampling, measurement error, dependence, missingness, sequential and adaptive-data theory.

Original form:

Treat raw observation count or repeated pass count as monotonically increasing confidence regardless of source/dependence/bias.

Development:

Modern practice distinguishes technical repeatability, independent replication, effective sample size, sequential validity and value of information.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Another observation earns weight only through identifiable independent/conditional information and positive decision value.

Problem:

Averaging can reduce independent random error, but cannot remove shared bias, selection, invalid constructs or common-mode dependence.

Mechanism:

Count independent information, model dependence/bias, protect stopping and ask whether the next observation can change the decision.

Trigger and context:

No universal trigger; more evidence helps only when it is valid, sufficiently independent and decision-relevant.

Preconditions:

Valid measurement, representative sampling, independent/new units or valid dependence/sequential model.

Measurement preconditions:

Measurement bias and evaluator drift must be bounded; repeated runs are provenance-distinct.

Assumptions:

Information increments satisfy the planning model and no outcome-contingent selection/stopping invalidates inference.

Decision or consumer:

Experiment/measurement owner.
