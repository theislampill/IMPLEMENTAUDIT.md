Agile

Agile: useful feedback, working behavior and deliberate adaptation

Agile approaches use observation and working increments to make consequential decisions under uncertainty. The current study separates that function from the frequency of meetings or releases. It preserves twenty-eight historical property identities and adds conditional current claims for feedback, integration, architecture, exposure, learning, assurance and continuing responsibility. None is a universal executable rule or a requirement to adopt a particular branded method.

Feedback needs a decision and a usable action window

Frequent feedback can be uninformative or arrive where nobody can act on it. A slower representative observation can be more useful. Evidence about integrated behavior does not by itself establish user value or safety, and an easily measured short-term proxy does not become a delayed outcome without a warranted connection.

Technical readiness, deployment and permitted exposure are separate states. A candidate can be ready while its actual exposure remains inappropriate. Recovery also has to cover the relevant consequence: reverting code does not necessarily reverse a disclosure, injury or persistent-state change. Existing adequate evidence, a bounded nonproduction prototype and justified continuation remain legitimate alternatives.

The serial-chain correction

Regression checks, integration and changeability can support one another without a compulsory global order. An isolated module can be refactored and checked before its first integration. A verified first release need not follow refactoring. The accepted correction therefore retains their mutual contributions while rejecting a universal chain in which every step must precede the next.

A useful batch example makes another limit concrete. With the stipulated cost function C(q)=100/q+q, batches of 1, 10 and 20 cost 101, 20 and 25 illustrative units. The example refutes a universal batch-of-one optimum under those premises. It does not calibrate a real project or establish that larger batches are generally better.

Six configurations, with adequate alternatives

The study examines small reversible work, an XP-style evolving product, Scrum-managed delivery with appropriate engineering controls, flow-oriented service work, assurance-constrained adaptation and multi-team product or platform work. Their functions can be supplied by different carriers. A useful library or competent handover may be sufficient where a permanent platform organization would add unnecessary provider and migration costs. Assurance-constrained work may iterate in simulation or verified increments when live exposure is unsuitable.

What the evidence does and does not settle

Iteration and evolving working subsets predate the 2001 Manifesto. The genealogy keeps early Scrum, XP, DSDM, Crystal, FDD, ASD and later delivery or operations relationships distinct. Similar vocabulary is not a transmission proof.

Empirical evidence is heterogeneous and often observational. Criticism-focused evidence does not estimate population prevalence, and an included primary study is not an extra independent dataset. Three novelty candidates—separate feedback clocks, authority-qualified adaptation and proxy-normalization hazards—were subsumed by existing properties or guards. The accepted study retains advisories about broad Scrum-displacement wording and an open-question locator. It establishes no universal framework superiority or current compliance determination.

The surviving contribution is a set of adaptive engineering mechanisms, not iteration alone and not mandatory Scrum ceremony.

Genealogy

  1. Iterative/incremental development, test-first work, evolutionary delivery, prototyping, and risk-driven spiral predate the 2001 Manifesto.
  2. Scrum, XP, DSDM, Crystal, FDD, ASD, and neighbouring methods formed a plural field. Scrum supplied empirical management; XP supplied a more explicit technical changeability system.
  3. The Manifesto and principles formed a thin declaration rather than a complete lifecycle or causal study.
  4. Lean software, software Kanban, Continuous Delivery, and DevOps hybridised with or reacted to Agile practice.
  5. SRE, safety, security, product discovery, evolutionary architecture, platform engineering, and systems assurance also converged from partly independent traditions.

Beyond the word iteration

Agile's strongest surviving properties include integrated working evidence, early risk retirement, automated regression, frequent real integration, technical changeability, release-ready state with exposure decoupled, direct problem/environment feedback, authorised process adaptation, sustainable pace and sustainable recovery capacity, and operational feedback after delivery.

A calendar iteration without engineering evidence, adaptation authority, technical quality, or operational learning does not establish those properties.

Rejected caricatures and ceremonies

Agile is not Scrum, and Scrum is not a complete engineering system. Shorter feedback, a two-week sprint, fixed iteration, release trains, continuous deployment, daily status meetings, user-story syntax, story points, velocity, TDD sequence, pair programming, one Product Owner, or a scaling framework are not universal prescriptions.

The retained property must be tested directly: coordination rather than stand-up attendance; learning rather than retrospective occurrence; regression evidence rather than a TDD label; knowledge resilience rather than pair count; and outcome evidence rather than velocity.
