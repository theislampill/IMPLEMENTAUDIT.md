Formal Methods & Verification Engineering

Mechanical checking can make evidence unusually strong for one precisely stated claim. It does not choose the right claim, supply missing assumptions, or prove an unconstrained real system correct.

Genealogy

Formal methods did not descend from one notation or theorem prover. Floyd’s assertions, Hoare’s preconditions and postconditions, and Dijkstra’s weakest-precondition calculus formed a program-logic lineage: turn an informal correctness idea into obligations attached to program structure. Specification and refinement traditions—including abstract data types, VDM, Z, B and Event-B—developed explicit states, operations, invariants and relations between abstract and concrete designs.

Temporal logic and process algebras addressed ongoing and concurrent behaviour. They distinguished properties such as “nothing bad happens” from progress claims such as “something good eventually happens”, while exposing scheduler, fairness, communication and memory-model assumptions. Model checking then made systematic finite-state exploration and counterexamples practical; symbolic, bounded, partial-order and abstraction-refinement techniques expanded the reachable scale without removing the model and bound.

Other branches pursued different evidence. Abstract interpretation and static analysis traded precision for scalable, sound approximation of selected error classes. SAT/SMT solving and symbolic execution automated encoded theories and paths while adding solver, encoding and path-coverage boundaries. Type systems and dependent types carried selected properties compositionally. Theorem provers made derivations mechanically replayable through a trusted kernel. Runtime verification observed finite executions, and proof-carrying code, translation validation, verified compilers and kernels moved checkable evidence across selected toolchain boundaries.

Industrial and research practice did not converge on “prove everything”. Focused modelling, executable contracts, bounded model finding, unit proofs, verified components, testing, fuzzing and runtime monitoring became hybrids chosen by claim shape and consequence. Large proofs are maintained software artefacts: their libraries, automation, tool versions and assumptions change. AI-assisted formalisation is the newest translation, and it intensifies an old problem—a type-correct proof can still certify a mistranslated requirement.

What survives as engineering practice

The first obligation is a precise property whose claim is precise enough to be false. Its variables, units, initial states, exceptional cases, timing, observer and decision consumer must reflect the engineering question. Positive examples, known bad cases and boundary cases challenge the translation before proof effort begins. A mathematically precise surrogate is still the wrong specification if it does not represent the stakeholder or world phenomenon at issue.

  • Carry explicit assumptions, preconditions and an environment model with every result. Enforce or monitor assumptions where possible; otherwise keep them as named residual risk.
  • Match the method to the property: invariants, temporal properties, contracts, refinement relations, type judgements, finite-state searches, certificates and runtime monitors answer different questions.
  • Make proof currentness part of the claim by binding the property, model, toolchain, inputs, configuration and replay evidence to the candidate actually checked.
  • Separate safety from liveness. A proof that no forbidden state occurs says nothing about useful progress; an unbounded eventuality says little about service-level time.
  • State abstraction direction and correspondence. Over-approximation can support bounded absence claims; under-approximation can find witnesses. Neither automatically connects a model to source, binary, configuration, hardware or deployment.
  • Challenge vacuity, unreachable initial states, inconsistent assumptions and weakened specifications with witnesses, anti-witnesses and mutation sensitivity.
  • Replay mechanical evidence against exact theorem, model, code, dependency and tool identities. Review theorem and assumption changes rather than repairing a proof until it becomes green.
  • Use proof together with independent testing, fuzzing, review and runtime evidence for assumptions, integration, environment and properties outside the formal perimeter.
  • Choose the cheapest sound discriminator. A typed assertion, truth table, finite model or deterministic checker can be the mature answer when whole-system theorem proving would not change the decision.

Persistent tensions remain. Stronger abstraction may scale while producing more spurious results; precise concrete models may become intractable. A small trusted kernel reduces the trust base but cannot validate the requirement or every parser, axiom, generator and hardware path. Deep proof can repay reuse and high failure cost, while rapid semantic change can make maintenance dominate. Runtime monitors see only observed finite traces, whereas static proof remains bounded by the world it modelled.

How the tradition changed, and what v0.4 translates

The tradition evolved through narrowing and hybridisation rather than a march towards total proof. Monolithic specifications gave way to modules, contracts and assume/guarantee reasoning. Explicit-state exploration gained symbolic representation, bounded checking and reduction. Solvers increasingly emit certificates or have results reconstructed in a separate checker. Verified compilers coexist with per-run translation validation. Proof artefacts are now treated as versioned dependencies, and proof plus testing is recognised as a division of evidence rather than a failure of formality.

IMPLEMENTAUDIT translates this into claim and evaluator discipline. It asks what property a check bears on, binds deterministic results to exact inputs and identities, retains negative and boundary witnesses, and refuses to treat a green tool result as broader than its oracle. When both a candidate and its evaluator change, agreement between them is not independent authentication; retained witnesses and old/new candidate-by-evaluator comparisons provide a distinct challenge. Exact release verification concerns the named candidate identity, not merely a similar tree or matching version text.

The accepted v0.4 mapping also preserves bounded formal-methods relationships in the composed architecture: each internal capability has a defined contract without gaining central decision authority; selective loading is an economy mechanism rather than a proof omission; package and version evidence describe one atomic product; parallel work joins through one current writer; and nested planning, interruption, execution and audit loops remain separately checkable controls. Verification of those relationships on the exact candidate is not a theorem that IMPLEMENTAUDIT as a whole satisfies the full formal-methods property set.

What was already native

IMPLEMENTAUDIT already used explicit invariants, acceptance criteria, terminal markers, exact hashes, deterministic package checks, negative evidence, held-out cases, independent review, and currentness rules. Its distinction between an attempted command, a resulting effect and verified closure already mirrors the formal distinction between a derivation, its claim and its real-world correspondence. Many contracts, replay and evidence-scoping properties therefore map to existing native controls or compositions.

The mapping is deliberately selective. It reuses an executable invariant when that cheaply excludes a recurring illegal state and escalates only when consequence, finite structure, reuse or failure cost justifies deeper formalisation.

Rejected caricatures, universals and ceremonies

The lineage rejects “formally verified” as an unqualified whole-system claim; model-checker silence as exhaustive real-world coverage; theorem proving as a replacement for testing; type safety as functional correctness; a proof assistant or solver as infallible; verified compilation as verification of the source programme and deployment; proof-line, obligation or document counts as assurance; and AI-generated, type-checked proof as evidence that the source requirement was faithfully translated.

No universal formalisation, theorem-prover requirement, proof-count target, certification ceremony or notation-specific workflow was imported. Lightweight methods are not treated as failed full verification, and a model is not required where a direct authoritative discriminator is cheaper and sufficient. Formal appearance cannot rescue the wrong property.

Evidence and generalisation limits

The frozen corpus examined 50 properties across 113 source records. Flagship cases demonstrate feasibility under particular scopes; they do not establish universal cost, adoption or whole-system assurance. Standards establish obligations or accepted evidence forms, not field effectiveness. State and path explosion, model-world correspondence, trusted tools, proof maintenance and organisational expertise remain live burdens. Recent AI and repository-scale benchmark evidence is promising but too narrow for mature industrial generalisation.

The current crosswalk records partial rather than complete native coverage for precise-property formulation, safety/liveness treatment, sound abstraction and stakeholder/world validation; exhaustive finite-state challenge remains context-bound rather than generally implemented. This page does not claim that IMPLEMENTAUDIT is formally verified, that its environment model is complete, that every safety or liveness property has a mechanical proof, or that toolchain and external-host behaviour are discharged. Exact-candidate architecture verification proves only the named relationships and checks.
