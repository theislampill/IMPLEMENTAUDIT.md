Reliability & Maintainability Engineering

Reliability begins with a required function under stated conditions; maintainability asks whether that function can be diagnosed and restored in the real support system. Neither is proved by a healthy component, a mean, a worksheet or a backup.

Genealogy

Reliability and maintainability engineering did not arise as one method. Life-data, survival and renewal theory asked how failures and repairs are distributed for stated populations. Wartime and post-war electronics programmes joined mission requirements, parts, environment, prediction, screening, test and field data. These statistical and institutional lineages supplied conditional evidence, but did not define the required function by themselves.

Failure analysis developed along different paths. Failure-mode and effects analysis worked upwards from ways items can fail; fault trees worked backwards from a defined top event. Reliability growth and failure reporting, analysis and corrective action linked observed failures to design change and recurrence monitoring. Maintainability and supportability became distinct design concerns: diagnosis, access, replaceability, tools, skills, spares, configuration preservation and return-to-service validation contribute to operational downtime, not merely hands-on repair time.

Fault-tolerant computing asked whether a function could continue or recover after activated faults. Redundant logic, voting, recovery blocks, diversity, checkpointing and rollback made coverage and state restoration explicit. Experiments with independently developed software versions later showed that separate teams do not guarantee independent failures. The resulting lesson was not “redundancy never works”, but that common cause, diagnostic coverage, transfer state and spare capacity belong in the claim.

Software reliability added operational input profiles because software does not wear out like a physical component; particular states and inputs activate faults. Dependability taxonomies later related reliability, availability, safety, integrity and maintainability while preserving their differences. Site reliability engineering, service-level objectives, chaos engineering, prognostics and AI-assisted operations are modern translations or hybrids. They add useful mechanisms, but do not form the inevitable final stage of the older disciplines.

What survives as engineering practice

The tradition begins by naming the function that matters, who consumes it, the operating mode and environment, the interval or number of demands, acceptable degradation and the consequence of failure. Reliability is avoiding a defined failure over that exposure. Availability concerns whether the function is capable at a point or over time. Maintainability and supportability concern restoration capability and resources. Durability is another claim again. Collapsing them into one “health” number loses the decision.

  • Define complete, partial, intermittent, latent and degraded states only as finely as their consequences require. A binary health bit is sufficient only when the end function is genuinely binary and directly observed.
  • Challenge how faults propagate, where single points and hidden dependencies lie, and which prevention, detection, containment, recovery or accepted-risk control addresses each important path. A focused “what can fail and what happens next?” review is often cheaper than a full worksheet.
  • Credit redundancy only for a declared tolerated fault set with credible independence, detection coverage, switchover, state compatibility and capacity. When independence cannot be supported, treat copies as one failure domain for the decision.
  • Separate detection, diagnosis and recovery. An alarm can be wrong, ambiguous or late; a component can report healthy while the user journey is stale, slow or broken.
  • Allow degraded operation only through a tested contract naming protected and sacrificed functions, consumer impact, observability, duration and exit criteria. Stop safely when continuation has no defensible envelope.
  • Close recovery on authoritative state, dependencies and an end-to-end functional postcondition. Restore sources must be accessible, integral, compatible and exercised under representative conditions.
  • Design maintainability into access, isolation, replacement, configuration/state preservation, validation and support logistics. Replacement or graceful discard may be the better cheap path for low-cost, short-life items.
  • Bind demonstration, field data and inherited evidence to population, operating profile, configuration, assumptions and uncertainty. Material change invalidates only the dependent evidence, which must then be requalified.
  • Call corrective action effective only after the mechanism-targeted fix is deployed and recurrence is assessed against comparable exposure. A closed ticket or falling incident count is not a causal reliability result.

The properties pull against one another. Redundancy can tolerate independent faults while adding control, maintenance and common-cause paths. Fast failover can reduce interruption while transferring stale state or overload to a weak standby. Preventive maintenance can reduce age-related failures or induce new ones when the mechanism is not age-related. Rich diagnosis can improve isolation while creating false actions and operator burden. Higher availability can conflict with forensic preservation or safe stopping after an unknown corruption.

How the tradition changed, and what v0.4 translates

The mature field moved from component means towards mission and user-journey semantics; from assumed independence towards common-cause evidence; from prevention alone towards detection, containment, bounded degradation, repair and validated recovery; from universal scheduled maintenance towards mechanism- and consequence-based policy; and from handbook predictions towards calibrated test and field evidence. Service objectives and chaos experiments survive only as scoped decision instruments. Predictive maintenance survives only where a precursor is observable, uncertainty is calibrated, lead time is actionable and simpler policies are compared.

IMPLEMENTAUDIT translates only the parts that fit audit-governed repository work. Its accepted v0.4 architecture preserves atomic configuration/package identity, dependency-correct parallel work, one writer at joins, nested loops for action and recurrence, and a separation between model judgement and deterministic authority. Those mechanisms can support configuration freshness, correlated-dependency reasoning, corrective-action closure and bounded automation.

They do not amount to a general reliability-and-maintainability contract. The research family would require explicit mission states, common-cause analysis, diagnostic coverage, degradation criteria and restoration behaviour before those claims could be made. The current mapping intentionally retains those gaps rather than treating architectural similarity as closure.

What was already native

IMPLEMENTAUDIT already retained failures and contrary evidence, required owner and source, recorded rollback and reopen conditions, stopped on abnormality, bound packages and releases to exact identities, and verified post-action state. These controls supply useful failure, currentness and recovery ingredients. Its recurrence loop also asks whether a repair addressed the mechanism instead of merely suppressing one symptom.

Native overlap remains bounded. A rollback plan is not tested disaster recovery; a deterministic package check is not a field-reliability demonstration; repeated green runs do not establish independence or a failure probability; and post-action readback does not by itself measure maintainability or service durability.

Rejected caricatures, universals and ceremonies

The lineage rejects mean time between failures as the universal meaning of reliability; “five nines” without an indicator, consumer, denominator, window and exclusions; the bathtub curve as a law for every population; handbook prediction as field truth; FMEA/FMECA completion as proof of controlled modes; redundancy diagrams as independence evidence; green component health as end-to-end availability; backup existence as recovery; one failover or chaos exercise as general resilience; incident counts or fitted growth curves as causal improvement; and zero observed failures as zero failure probability.

No reliability score, uptime target, mandatory worksheet, redundancy quota, permanent maintenance programme, predictive-maintenance requirement or AI auto-remediation mode was imported. Metrics and artefacts may answer a scoped question, but their presence cannot substitute for required-function evidence.

Evidence and generalisation limits

The frozen corpus examined 55 properties across 85 exact source records. Mathematical models are conditional on failure processes, distributions and dependence assumptions. Tests are conditional on sample, profile, duration and configuration. Incident reports establish mechanisms, not prevalence. Service and chaos guidance records practices and obligations, not automatic effectiveness. Current prognostics and AI/autonomy evidence remains use-case-specific, with transfer, drift, false action and authority still open.

The accepted crosswalk is especially conservative here. Required-function semantics and end-to-end journey checks retain deterministic fixture gaps; failure-mode analysis, failure-state semantics, common-cause reasoning, diagnostic coverage and maintainability retain source-level proof gaps; redundancy, graceful degradation and recovery retain behavioural proof gaps. Many other properties remain bounded with no new proof claim. This route therefore reports no measured reliability, availability, maintainability, supportability, diagnostic coverage, recovery time, service durability or probability of failure. Verification of selected v0.4 architecture relations is not evidence that those domain obligations are implemented or effective.
