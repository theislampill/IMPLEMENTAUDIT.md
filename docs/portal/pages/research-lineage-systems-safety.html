Systems Safety

The useful inheritance is consequence-sensitive control of hazardous system states across hardware, software, people, organisations and environments—not a universal risk matrix, worksheet or claim of absolute safety.

Genealogy

Systems safety is a braid, not a single chain. Early industrial accident prevention concerned injury causation, supervision, guarding and training. Military reliability introduced structured component failure analysis; fault trees represented combinations leading to a defined top event; process industries developed HAZOP to challenge deviations from design intent. Military and aerospace programmes then established lifecycle hazard analysis, design controls and explicit risk-acceptance authority. Nuclear defence in depth, functional safety, aviation development assurance, medical-device risk and other regulated domains added distinct technical and legal regimes.

Later traditions corrected important blind spots. Organisational safety research examined tight coupling, production pressure, communication and local rationality. Safety and assurance cases made claim–argument–evidence structures visible, while failures such as the Nimrod loss showed that a large accepted document can diverge catastrophically from the actual system. STAMP and STPA reformulated accidents around inadequate constraints, unsafe control actions, process models and feedback, making it explicit that locally correct components can interact unsafely. Resilience and living-assurance work added adaptation, degraded operation, current configuration and reassessment after change.

The mature result is complementarity, not replacement. FMECA, fault trees and HAZOP remain useful for the causal classes they represent; control- and interaction-oriented methods address unsafe timing, context, authority and feedback, including hazards with no component failure. Accident histories such as Ariane 5 and Therac-25 demonstrate mechanisms involving inherited assumptions, software, interfaces and system interaction. They do not provide universal intervention effect sizes. Domain certification levels and artefacts remain bounded to their applicable regimes.

Surviving engineering properties

Systems safety connects unacceptable loss and hazardous system state to enforceable safety constraints, control feedback, containment and validated recovery.

  • Name the unacceptable loss and hazardous state. A hazard is a system condition linked to loss, not merely a failed component. The boundary, exposure, stakeholders and decision authority remain explicit and revisable.
  • Establish enforceable constraints. A safety requirement matters only when it is allocated, enforceable, verified and paired with enough feedback to know whether the intended safe state was reached.
  • Analyse complementary causal classes. Search component failures, logical combinations, process deviations, unsafe interactions, timing, interfaces, software, human coordination and organisational control using the smallest adequate set of representations.
  • Do not call a score the risk. Preserve consequence, uncertainty, model assumptions, coupling, observability and recovery difficulty. Scale control depth to credible loss and reversibility rather than to a method label.
  • Bind assurance to identity and change. Claims, evidence and assumptions apply to a configuration and environment. Reassure the affected claims after change; reuse unaffected evidence only with a reason.
  • Test independence by information, not headcount. Redundant channels, tools, reviewers and evidence can share a cause or oracle. Stronger assurance comes from relevant and independently informative failure detection, not more signatures.
  • Confirm containment and recovery as states. Issuing a stop, rollback or fail-safe command is not containment. Restart follows evidence that relevant constraints and feedback are restored and that an authorised owner accepts residual uncertainty.

Low-consequence reversible work with an immediate authoritative result retains a cheap path: state the bounded consequence, perform the direct check and stop. High-consequence work deepens only where loss, coupling, delayed observation, irreversibility or difficult recovery require it.

How IMPLEMENTAUDIT translated the properties

IMPLEMENTAUDIT translated the lineage into consequence-control selection. Before adding process, it asks what unacceptable outcome is credible, what state would permit it, which constraint prevents it, who has authority to act or stop, what feedback establishes the achieved state, and how containment or recovery will be confirmed. If continuing and stopping can each cause harm, bounded degraded operation needs explicit limits and compensating controls; otherwise unknown control state preserves the stop.

Change and acceptance guidance keeps configuration, assumptions and evidence current. A component pass does not establish safe composition, a local green result does not expand its claim, and rollback is not recovery until the intended system state is independently observed. The discipline also distinguishes genuine intervention capability from a ceremonial human override.

The accepted crosswalk does not say that all 68 safety properties became runtime behaviour. It records property-by-property composition with existing owners, exact matches, out-of-scope items and stripped ceremonies; only admitted architecture relations with exact evidence are marked preserved. Internal integration ownership routes reconciliation; it does not create a new certification regime or universal safety case.

What was already native and where traditions converge

IMPLEMENTAUDIT already used consequence-aware stopping, bounded scope, rollback, negative evidence, independent review, post-effect verification, exact identity and claim ceilings. Those controls already prevented an attempted command, component result or received document from proving a broader system outcome. The lineage sharpened hazardous-state, constraint, degraded-operation and recovery semantics rather than adding a separate safety lifecycle.

Systems safety shares configuration, interfaces and lifecycle change with systems engineering; state, authority and workload with cognitive systems engineering; and uncertainty, dependence and measurement validity with statistical engineering. Its distinctive contribution is the explicit link from unacceptable loss to hazardous state, enforceable control, feedback, containment and authorised recovery. Domain safety levels, submissions and certification objectives do not automatically transfer with that mechanism.

What it deliberately did not import

IMPLEMENTAUDIT did not add a universal risk matrix, hazard worksheet, FMEA table, fault-tree diagram, HAZOP workshop, STPA control diagram, safety case notation, hazard log, review board, certification checklist, safety score, fixed review count or maximal process for every change. Any of these can instantiate a needed property; completing the artefact is not evidence that the loss is controlled.

It rejects risk-matrix colour as objective risk, compliance as proof of current safety, zero incidents as proof that controls work, “human error” as the terminal cause, redundancy as automatic independence, reviewer or document count as assurance strength, a complete case as proof of safety, STPA as a universal replacement, generic resilience language without a hazard and control, and zero risk as a general engineering target.

Tensions, boundaries and evidence limits

No practical method proves that every hazard in an open sociotechnical system has been found. Hazard boundaries and tolerability contain value judgements; rare-event probabilities may be structurally underidentified; formal proof depends on the model and its correspondence to the physical system; and continuous monitoring cannot guarantee discovery of unknown hazards. Added independence can slow integrated learning, while elaborate fail-operational and degraded modes can introduce new interaction and comprehension hazards.

Historical and domain-practice support is strong for many lifecycle mechanisms, and accident cases strongly establish the existence of unsafe interaction, stale-assumption, common-mode, organisational and assurance failures. Comparative evidence for exact methods, safety-case formats, STPA portfolios, resilience indicators and process prescriptions is uneven. Standards establish recognised or required practice in scope, not optimality. This page does not claim complete hazard analysis, calibrated loss probability, certified safety, universal transfer or safe operation in any external environment.
