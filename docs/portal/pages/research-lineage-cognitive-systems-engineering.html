What the property population contributes

ECSE-01–06 set the system boundary and account for how work changes when functions move. A joint system is the relevant unit when material dependencies cross the individual or device boundary; an individual analysis remains adequate when expansion contributes no relevant dependency. Work-domain models must support decisions and remain contestable by practitioners. External representations must help an actual consumer, and automation must be assessed over displaced and residual work, including exceptions. None of these mechanisms is fulfilled by simply drawing a larger diagram or assigning another role. CSE-006

ECSE-07–14 distinguish state knowledge, transitions, evidence freshness, command effects, authority, oversight, handoff and timed recovery. Seeing a label does not automatically supply understanding, but learned compact cues can be sufficient in a familiar setting. A protective intervention requires information, competence, practical control and permission in the relevant time window. The recovery criterion follows task dependencies through the required stable state; it cannot assume that all stages must be serial or that a command acknowledgment is the required physical effect. CSE-007

ECSE-15–33 treat skill, reliance, alerts, interruption, shared capacity, communication, explanation, operational expectations, learning and degradation as conditional mechanisms. Recognition is useful when cues are valid and learning provides relevant feedback. Trust should support appropriate reliance rather than maximize persuasion. Alerts require a capable recipient and feasible response; information, explanations and cross-checks consume the same attention that other tasks need. A combination of individually useful demands can therefore be jointly infeasible. Procedure and adaptation require a bounded authority relationship, and adaptation near a capacity limit must account for costs displaced onto other actors. CSE-008

Genealogy

Cognitive Systems Engineering grew from several overlapping traditions rather than one clean line of descent. Wartime and post-war human factors treated displays, controls, workspaces, selection, training and human performance as engineering concerns. Supervisory-control research then studied people who set goals and monitor computers carrying out lower-level control. By the early 1980s, this work had exposed a harder problem: automating a task does not remove the human contribution. It redistributes monitoring, diagnosis, exception handling, coordination and recovery, often leaving people responsible for rare interventions they have little opportunity to practise.

The 1983 Cognitive Systems Engineering programme shifted attention from an isolated operator or interface towards cognitive work performed with tools in a complex world. Cognitive Work Analysis and ecological interface design developed constraint- and work-domain representations; distributed cognition located memory and calculation across people, artefacts and time; common-ground and team-cognition research addressed commitments, mutual prediction and repair. Later joint-cognitive-system work made the coupled work system the primary unit of analysis. These traditions overlap, but the frozen research does not collapse them into one genealogy.

Criticism changed the tradition. Static lists assigning functions to “human” or “machine” were narrowed because they neglected coordination, residual work and changing capability. Mode-error and out-of-the-loop research showed that a visible mode name or manual switch need not give a person the state, time or skill required to intervene. Situation awareness remains a contested family of individual, team and distributed constructs, while full Cognitive Work Analysis and ecological interface programmes can be costly, analyst-dependent and difficult to maintain. Healthcare handoff and checklist studies also show why named bundles cannot be detached from implementation and setting. Modern human–AI evidence adds a sharper limit: combining a person with AI does not, by itself, establish performance better than the stronger solo component.

Surviving engineering properties

The durable contribution is joint cognitive work organised around decision-relevant state, with authority and capability aligned to the people, agents, tools and representations that can actually decide, act, verify, stop or recover.

  • Bound the joint work system. When a consequential outcome depends on several participants or artefacts, identify the people, automation, representations, information flows, timing and organisational supports that actually perform the work. Keep the boundary narrow enough that responsibility and action remain assignable.
  • Make consequential state and transitions legible. Show what is current, pending, committed, executing, completed, failed or degraded when those distinctions change action. A command being accepted is not evidence that the intended world state was achieved.
  • Align authority with capability. Whoever decides, acts, verifies, stops or recovers needs the relevant state, controls, competence and time. A nominal approver or override is not an effective control when intervention is infeasible.
  • Transfer state, authority and obligations explicitly. Handoffs distinguish what was transmitted, what the receiver accepted, what the receiver independently checked and which discrepancies or pending obligations remain.
  • Treat attention and workload as limited capacity. Alerts need an actionable condition, a capable recipient, useful timing, escalation and retirement. Peak load, transitions, interruption and reserve capacity matter more than one average workload score.
  • Evaluate the joint outcome. Test decision and action quality, coordination cost, recovery and failure under realistic conditions. Usability, trust, awareness or explanation scores may diagnose mechanisms, but they do not establish system value on their own.
  • Learn from coordination failures. Near misses, workarounds, automation surprises and handoff failures should be able to change architecture, representation, authority, staffing, procedure or training—not merely produce another reminder.

The direct path remains valid for low-consequence, single-actor work with an immediate, transparent and deterministic effect. That case needs a clear actor, action and result, not a model of an elaborate joint cognitive system.

Receiving a pending action

Consider an acknowledged remote command. If execution is queued or delayed, the acknowledgement can establish receipt while the intended effect remains unknown. The receiver may need current state, pending obligations, authority and enough time to decide whether to wait, investigate, stop or recover. The historical ledger separates transition visibility (ECSE-08), provenance and uncertainty (ECSE-09), observed world effect (ECSE-10), and feasible intervention (ECSE-11/12). A green display cannot substitute for those distinctions.

Handoff adds a further question: what did the receiver actually accept and independently verify? Transmitting a complete message does not by itself establish comprehension or readiness. For an atomic local reversible action with immediate authoritative feedback, the effect and a clear owner may be sufficient. This is an explanatory example grounded in the public cognitive ledger, ECSE-08–ECSE-14 and ECSE-34; it is not a new observed performance result.

Tensions, boundaries and evidence limits

The joint-system boundary can become so broad that nobody owns an action. Rich work-domain models can encode work as imagined, go stale and cost more than a simple state machine or decision table. Extra state and explanation can overload attention or persuade without improving detection. Dynamic allocation can create new mode and authority surprises. System explanations must reveal upstream mechanisms without dissolving evidence-based individual accountability.

Evidence is strongest for historical provenance, recurring mechanisms, selected laboratory and field findings, accident plausibility and established-domain practice. It is weaker for universal effect sizes, the cost-effectiveness of full CWA or ecological interface programmes, a unitary situation-awareness construct, long-term human–agent operations and transfer of domain bundles. This page therefore does not prove synergy, lower workload, calibrated reliance, complete common ground, universal cognitive fit or better live human-agent performance.

ECSE-01 — Joint cognitive work system as the unit of design

Historical origin:

Hollnagel and Woods (1983), Rasmussen et al. (1994), Hutchins (1995), Hollnagel and Woods (2005)

Original form:

Analyse cognition and control across the coupled human-machine-work system rather than treating the person as an isolated component.

Development:

From component human factors to cognitive engineering, distributed cognition and joint cognitive systems.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Define the work system boundary, cognitive functions, authority, information flows, dependencies, timing and adaptation; test joint performance, not merely component usability.

Problem:

Local component optimisation can degrade system-level coordination, conceal displaced work, and leave no owner for coupling failures.

Mechanism:

Model people, automation, procedures, representations and organisational supports as interdependent contributors to one work function; evaluate joint outcomes and coordination costs.

Trigger and context:

Consequential work in which decisions, representations, authority or control are distributed across two or more participants or artefacts.

Preconditions:

A stable-enough work boundary, access to work-as-done, and a named engineering consumer.

Authority preconditions:

Authority and accountability must remain assignable even when cognition is distributed.

State and mode preconditions:

Current shared and private state relevant to coordination must be identifiable.

Timing and attention preconditions:

Temporal coupling and decision deadlines must be represented.

Decision or consumer:

System architect, operations owner, safety/assurance owner, and the actors who perform the work.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

For a low-consequence, single-actor, transparent deterministic operation, document the actor, state and effect without constructing a full joint-system model.

ECSE-02 — Decision-relevant work-domain and constraint modelling

Historical origin:

Rasmussen's abstraction hierarchy; Rasmussen, Pejtersen and Goodstein (1994); Naikar's CWA development

Original form:

Represent purposes, values, functions, processes, physical resources and means-ends links independently of one prescribed task sequence.

Development:

From comprehensive work-domain analysis toward proportional constraint modelling and live maintenance.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Maintain an auditable, decision-consumed constraint model at the smallest useful granularity; link representations and procedures to it.

Problem:

Procedure-only descriptions hide invariant constraints and give operators little support when conditions depart from the nominal script.

Mechanism:

Elicit the constraints, boundaries, resources, trade-offs and means-ends relations that determine which actions remain viable; expose the minimum subset needed by live decisions.

Trigger and context:

Complex, variable or safety/consequence-sensitive work in which unanticipated states or multiple strategies are plausible.

Preconditions:

Domain expertise, access to actual work, a model owner and change-control linkage.

Authority preconditions:

The model must distinguish who may trade off which goals and who can cross or relax constraints.

State and mode preconditions:

The operational state variables and constraints represented must be measurable or inferable with known uncertainty.

Timing and attention preconditions:

Constraints needed under disturbance must be available before action deadlines.

Decision or consumer:

Interface designer, procedure designer, training owner, controller/operator and architecture reviewer.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

For stable routine work, a concise state-transition model, invariant list or decision table may preserve the property without a full CWA programme.

Criticism of Fitts-list allocation; MABA-MABA critique; joint-activity and coactive-design work

Original form:

Replace one-time assignment of functions with design of dependencies, communication, observability, predictability and recovery between performers.

Development:

Mechanism:

Trigger and context:

Decision or consumer:

ECSE-05 — Dynamic role, function and authority coordination

Historical origin:

Supervisory control, adaptive automation, mixed initiative and coactive design

Original form:

Allow roles and initiative to change with state, capability and workload instead of remaining fixed for every condition.

Development:

From adaptive automation keyed mainly to workload toward coactive, bounded and mutually observable role adaptation.

Evolved form in this historical corpus:

Use bounded, state-dependent mixed initiative with explicit transition contracts and post-change confirmation—not opaque adaptive reassignment.

Problem:

Fixed allocation becomes brittle when capabilities, workload, uncertainty or environment change, but unannounced adaptation creates authority surprise.

Mechanism:

Define allowed role states, triggers, negotiation/consent rules, transition annunciation, fallback and reversion; keep authority changes observable and challengeable.

Trigger and context:

Variable environments where relative human/automation capability changes materially and the cost of fixed allocation is high.

Preconditions:

Validated triggers, role-state model, transition feedback and stable fallback.

Authority preconditions:

Each transfer needs an authorised initiator, acceptance semantics and residual-responsibility rule.

State and mode preconditions:

Current role, next role, trigger and degraded state must be visible.

Timing and attention preconditions:

Transitions must complete within the time needed to reacquire state and act.

Decision or consumer:

Automation-policy owner, operators and governance authority.

Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:

Keep roles fixed when they are adequate, when transition risk exceeds benefit, or when authority changes cannot be made legible in time.
