## 2. The genealogy is plural and evidence-bounded

Queueing, inventory, wartime operational research, mathematical programming,
statistical decision theory and human choice studies have different historical
questions and evidence. Erlang's queueing work is not a generic proof of modern
agent workflow performance. Harris's lot-size reasoning carries the source's
own conditions. Theodore Harris in the Ford–Fulkerson history is not inventory
author Ford W. Harris. Kuhn's explicit references to König and Egerváry support
a documentary relationship that should not be replaced by vague resemblance.
[Genealogy, all 16 edges; report §2.]

G04 says that a particular direct influence is not established; it does not
prove a Tolstoi-specific absence. PERT and CPM are related but not identical
duration models. Bellman's R245 Introduction (1953) and P550 Theory (1954)
remain distinct entities after r2 removes the mixed citation. Online dates,
issue dates, preprint versions and conference/journal continuations are not
independent replications. Source dependence must be considered before using a

## 3. Objectives, flow and capacity

An operational loss must correspond to the valued consequence. A convenient
proxy such as spending can fail to represent need. Obermeyer's study supports
its actual cohort and design, not an invented randomized health-outcome
intervention. Decision rights need to be explicit without treating separate
persons as universally necessary. Hard constraints and noncompensable
consequences precede tradeoff; mathematical weights do not manufacture
legitimacy. [Report §§3–4; P005–P007.]

Flow identities and stochastic queue models have different assumptions.
Finite-window occupancy can equal the exact area 8 divided by window 6,
or 4/3, without establishing an M/M/1 model or a long-run stationary regime.
Equal mean arrival and service rates do not determine delay: the accepted
constructed cases contrast memoryless, deterministic and batched processes.
For a receiving delay formula, measurements must cover the actual population
and regime; a predicted mean delay cannot identify its generating process.
[Report §4; D02; composition R02.]

Parallelism is constrained by real completion resources. D03's shared acceptance
resource produces a total duration of 8, distinct from independent acceptance
at 5 and complete serialization at 10. A concurrency ceiling is not a target
that must always be filled. Idling can be rational when setup, synchronization
or a future critical task matters. Aggregate throughput does not settle
distributional fairness. A single explicit resource inequality may resolve
the conflict without a comprehensive enterprise model. [D03; R03; report §4.]

## 4. Optimization and uncertainty have distinct guarantees

A solver gap is a mathematical statement about a represented problem, not
world regret. Exact computation may not be worthwhile if a simpler near-optimal
policy has small relevant loss and much lower implementation or maintenance
burden. EOQ's local flat-cost example supports that stated model, not all
inventory heuristics. A forecasting loss can also select a different action
from the operational shortage/excess loss. [Report §5; D01, D07, D09.]

Uncertainty can be stochastic variation, incomplete knowledge, ambiguity or
model form. Coherent explicitly subjective priors can support a normative
decision analysis without being represented as calibrated frequencies.
Inventing a number does not become evidence by calling it a probability.
Robust, stochastic, chance-constrained and distributionally robust formulations
have different quantifiers. Feasibility after observing each state does not
prove that one pre-observation action works for all states; recourse requires
its actual information timing and authority. [P041–P044; report §5; D08.]

Protection has opportunity cost. An unsupported phantom state can drive a
conservative policy that sacrifices value in the credible states. A robust set
certificate does not establish the separate independent, symmetric, bounded
deviation assumptions needed for a probability bound. Tail criteria and vetoes
may be warranted, but choosing them is a substantive consequence and authority
decision. No complete new proof audit of all risk measures is claimed.
[Report §5; D08; source access register.]

Inventory, flexible investment and maintenance share a conditional economic
structure, not interchangeable formulas. Demand, lead time, service loss and
option feasibility matter. The maintenance examples retain both a memoryless
case favoring run-to-failure under its costs and an increasing-hazard finite-grid
example supporting replacement; the grid is not a global theorem and safety
constraints can change the decision. [Report §5; D07, D09, D11.]

## 5. Information, preferences and simulation

The accepted value-of-information example gives EVPI 14 and EVSI 8 under a
specified prior/payoff table; acquisition cost 3 leaves net value 5. These are
constructed calculations. More information can have zero decision value when
one action dominates regardless of observation. The XOR case shows why two
complementary inquiries can be jointly useful when each is useless alone; a
greedy next-test rule can stop prematurely. Deadlines, acquisition cost and
execution duties remain guards. Required research cannot be omitted and
retroactively relabeled optimal stopping. [Report §6; D04; M09; R04–R05.]

Weighted additive models can be valid with meaningful scales and authorized
compensation. Alternative-dependent normalization can create a rank reversal;
a veto can exclude the high-scoring option; a weighted sum cannot select every
unsupported Pareto point. These discriminate different mathematical and
preference claims, rather than rejecting all score tables. AHP criticism and
replies remain contested at the actual access scope; partial and complete
outranking results are not interchangeable. [P007, P052, P059; D05; R09.]

Simulation separates conceptual validity, implementation verification,
input-data adequacy and operational correspondence. Ten thousand samples from
a wrong mean can produce a narrow interval excluding truth. Additional runs
cannot automatically remove input uncertainty, structural misspecification or
rare-event omissions. Common random numbers can help a matched difference
when covariance has the helpful sign, and harm it when the sign is adverse.
A shared seed by itself proves neither variance reduction nor relevance.
[Report §6; D06; R07.]

## 6. Selection, learning, feedback and no-change alternatives

Choosing the largest noisy estimate can create post-selection optimism even
when individual estimates are unbiased. D07 yields expected selected estimate
.5 for two equally valuable alternatives whose true value is 0; perfectly
shared errors remove that specific optimism. This is a conditional analytical
result, not a field experiment. Decision-focused learning needs a loss and
feasible set corresponding to the actual decision, plus its own learning
premises. Low downstream training loss does not validate the chosen proxy.
[P017, P046, P054; report §7; R06.]

Deployment can change the population being predicted. Stability under repeated
retraining is distinct from optimality under a deployment-dependent objective.
Monitoring must have an actual response consumer: revalidate, revise, fall back
or retire. A material change can justify reconsidering a plan without
determining the best new plan. A gain of 2 with switching cost 4 can favor the
incumbent; an infeasible incumbent cannot be retained merely to avoid churn.
[D07, D10; R08, R10.]

Human reactions to algorithms are context-dependent: controlled aversion and
contrary appreciation findings do not establish a universal behavior. Recent
learned-ambiguity backtests, online scheduling results and decision-focused
papers retain their actual comparator, calibration and access limits. A future
conference programme at the research cutoff is an agenda, not completed
evidence. The correct response is to preserve useful bounded results and name
the next application discriminator, not declare general agent-work readiness.
[Report §7.]
