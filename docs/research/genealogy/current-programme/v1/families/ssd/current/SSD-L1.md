## 3. Intent, requirements and architecture have different predicates

Intent must concern actual stakeholders, mission conditions and a legitimate
decision authority. A technically successful solution can still optimize the
wrong objective. A boundary must include consequential excluded interactions,
but boundary completeness does not mean examining the whole world. Rights and
noncompensable constraints cannot be silently exchanged for a high weighted
score. A well-formed requirement can still be unauthorized, infeasible or
irrelevant; clear syntax does not settle those questions. [Report §§3–4.]

Traceability helps a particular consumer locate a relevant change or reason.
Link density alone does not establish correct semantics. The accepted
traceability experiment concerns 71 participants, two Java projects, supplied
checked traces and maintenance-task answers; it does not measure an enterprise
lifecycle after paying construction and maintenance costs. For a known local
change, a short linked note or direct inspection can be sufficient.
[Report §4; REL-11; source R016.]

Architecture records consequential choices, alternatives, constraints and
their rationale. Decomposition makes work manageable but creates a need to
examine shared power, bandwidth, clocks, facilities and human attention. A
clean API or separate device enclosure is not proof of independence. Alternatives
include no change, reuse, a service, manual work and simplifying the objective.
Keeping options has a carrying cost; sensitivity reveals dependence on values
or assumptions without making those values objective. P018's debt-specific
full-text ceiling and P055's product-family source limits remain explicit.
[Report §5; REL-02, REL-10.]

## 4. Interface meaning and joint behavior

The relevant interface includes units, reference frames, timing, states,
exceptions, resource use and authority. Documents, schemas, types and tests
are alternative or complementary carriers only insofar as they express the
required semantics and satisfy applicable obligations. The Mars Climate
Orbiter report already contained a metric requirement: the failure involved
actual numerical meaning and operational handoffs, so merely adding another
unit label would not establish compatible operation. [Report §6; REL-02.]

Ariane 501 shows the different danger of shared software and transferred
trajectory assumptions. Its inquiry does not require all real hardware in
every test; appropriate simulation remains a permitted means under justified
coverage. Apollo's positive engineering cases show that joint tests can support
continued use or improve an already acceptable margin without compelling
redesign. These are selected cases and mechanisms, not population failure rates
or an average economic effect. [Report §6; sources R011–R014.]

The direction matters. Requirements and architecture can locate an interaction
question, a contract can narrow admissible behavior, and a joint observation
can inform a particular configuration. A successful integration does not show
that every omitted scenario is harmless. An integration failure can instead
reopen an allocation or requirement. A focused hand calculation or joint test
can be cheaper than complete model federation. [REL-02, REL-04.]

## 5. Lifecycle, configuration and current evidence

The official ISO catalogue permits iterative, concurrent and recursive
application without prescribing one lifecycle. Its catalogue and early paper
abstract are the actual access ceiling; paid clauses and the complete early
paper are not claimed as read. Expensive fabrication, destructive tests,
contracts and limited windows can justify staged commitments. Iteration can
learn, but can also destabilize suppliers or consume resources without resolving
a material uncertainty. [Report §7.]

As-designed, as-built, as-tested and as-operated configurations can differ. A
matching hash does not establish calibration, runtime or physical
correspondence. Evidence reuse is claim-specific: a proved display-label-only
change can leave thermal evidence valid; changed power may invalidate it;
unknown dependency remains unknown. Unknown is neither permission to reuse nor
an instruction to discard every previous result. Maintaining the dependency
argument has a cost and a cheap direct retest can be preferable. [Report §8;
REL-03.]

Verification, intended-use validation, contractual acceptance and regulatory
qualification establish different propositions. A certificate can attest its
defined compliance result without proving a wider mission result. An early
prototype is useful only if it addresses a consequential uncertainty; it may
consume the same scarce test rig as final qualification. CASE-05 preserves
positive prospective information value even when the realized observation
leaves the chosen action unchanged. Competent challenge requires relevant
access and different error pathways; two signatures can share one wrong
premise. [Report §9; REL-04, REL-09.]

## 6. Models, systems of systems and human work

A geometric model, integration model, monitoring model and qualification model
have different consumers and credibility burdens. Calibration can overfit;
input pedigree and use domain remain premises. The 2026 NASA handbook's
official page and internally DRAFT-marked PDF disagree on status, and both
observations remain recorded. SysML version metadata establishes neither tool
correctness nor organizational benefit. [Report §10.]

The S117 repair is material: its transformed workflow comparison reports
modeled schedule gains from a qualitative case and stated assumptions, not
measured gains after deployed digital transformation. Its arithmetic can be
correct while the original evidence-class inference is wrong. Reviews of MBSE
benefits, adoption cases and sociotechnical barriers have different designs;
they are not a pooled set of independent causal trials. A static record or
manual measurement remains an alternative to a continuously maintained twin.
[Report §§10, 12, 15; CASE-13.]

Independent constituent authority is a real systems-of-systems constraint.
An architect cannot compel another owner's upgrade by drawing a shared
diagram. Agreements, compatibility, incentives, data rights and resource access
condition feasible coordination. Human workload, skill, maintenance and
emergency response are part of operational allocation; an available human is
not automatically an effective monitor. These are grounded design concerns,
without invented numerical safety or productivity gains. [Report §11;
REL-06, REL-08.]

AI-generated requirements, diagrams and models can supply candidate material.
Syntax, model identity or reproducibility do not establish truth, authorization
or engineering adequacy. The current studies' limited tasks, overlaps,
single-run configurations and author scoring are preserved. Human drafting,
direct calculation or an existing simple tool remain live alternatives.
[Report §13; REL-12.]
