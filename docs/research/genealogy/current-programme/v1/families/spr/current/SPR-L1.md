## 1. The identification question and its plural genealogy

Identification asks what a particular body of information can establish about a stated target. The target might be a parameter combination, a predictive response, a behavioral machine, a causal intervention functional, a software relation or a decision that remains valid across several models. Naming that target is consequential: uniqueness of an optimizer, useful prediction and recovery of a unique generating mechanism are different claims. The canonical lineage retains 83 property rows, 189 attributes, 127 predicates and 73 profiles. Its 54-row and 65-row ancestral executions are preserved as ancestry; they are not added to the canonical denominator. [Canonical report](SOURCES.md#spr-l1) · [All current records](SPR-L1-CURRENT-PROPERTIES.md).

The history is plural. Classical estimation and dynamical-system identification ask how excitation, noise, feedback, realization and finite data support model recovery. Language identification and automata learning ask what target classes and information presentations permit identification. Behavioral software learning adds reset, oracle, time, data and observation contracts. Architecture recovery, process mining and inquiry into human practice introduce different correspondence problems: an artifact graph, event log and situated account are different observations. Nonlinear, sparse, weak-form, lifted, neural and physics-informed approaches change representation and numerical assumptions; they do not remove the question of what the evidence distinguishes.

The genealogy therefore separates documented ancestry from analytical connection. Gold’s positive-text limit, membership/equivalence-query learning and characteristic-data methods change different parts of the information problem. The finite ambiguity of two candidates is a useful witness, but it is not a substitute proof of Gold’s eventual-identification theorem. The contemporary name “Evolved System Identification” denotes the packet’s criticism-filtered reconciliation; it is not attributed retrospectively to every source community. ## 2. Information, precision and use are distinct predicates

CL01 asks whether the target functional is constant on observational equivalence classes. This can leave some parameter coordinates nonunique while a target combination or common decision is established. CL02 then asks a different question: even a structurally identifiable target can have unusably uncertain finite-data estimates. ## 4. Nonlinear models and the corrected complexity decision

The nonlinear and scientific-inference branch retains several independent distinctions. Moving noise through a nonlinear transformation can change the model. An observable latent representation need not identify physical hidden coordinates. A regularizer can select a stable solution without adding distinguishing observations. Predictive equivalence under an observed policy does not establish agreement on an intervention functional. Sparse recovery depends on coordinates, library, observed state and informative trajectories; weak-form methods reduce particular numerical burdens while retaining their quadrature, tuning and identifiability conditions.

The Koopman reconciliation separates finite snapshot consistency, exact finite observable closure and state reconstruction. Multiple isolated equilibria alone do not establish the rejected universal obstruction to finite smooth embeddings. Removing that overbroad obstruction does not prove arbitrary finite linearizability. Residual diagnostics need their own nonvacuity, fixed-dictionary and quadrature conditions. Neural initialization, free rollout, solver tolerances and architectural topology are separate from fit to short corrected segments. A finite physics-informed residual does not establish an equation throughout its domain or the physical truth of the chosen operator family.
