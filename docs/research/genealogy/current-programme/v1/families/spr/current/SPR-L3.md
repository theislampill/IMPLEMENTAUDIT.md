## 1. Meaning, obligation, evidence and acceptance

Requirements engineering asks how statements about an intended system acquire interpretable meaning, support and justified use in decisions. An accurate description need not be an obligation. An authorized obligation may be infeasible. A specification may be satisfied while a domain premise is false. A delivered behavior can satisfy the chosen specification while missing the intended need. Acceptance adds its own criterion, recipient and decision. These distinctions explain why a diagram, signed document, solver result or clean language template cannot supply every relevant warrant. They also explain why a short clear request with an appropriate authority and observable criterion can be sufficient.

## 2. Necessity, correspondence and responsibility

C0001 types and sources the meaning of a requirement. C0002 makes a necessity claim relative to a stated, nonempty alternative universe and a goal. If two admissible solutions meet that goal without any proposed feature in common, the universal claim that this model has known common necessary features fails. That does not prove every project lacks necessary features, or erase a binding commitment made on a different basis. Unknown alternatives are a limit on knowledge, not permission to invent a preferred ontology.

C0003’s `D ∧ S ⊨ R` is conditional on interpretation and domain assumptions, with consistency checked separately. A controller can meet a sensor-based specification while a stuck sensor breaks correspondence with the physical variable. The machine proof and the external requirement therefore have different truth conditions. A direct correspondence check may resolve the actual problem better than a larger proof over a false domain model. Formal sufficiency does not establish stakeholder legitimacy or empirical domain truth.

Refinement, assignability, operationalization and realizability remain separate. Children may entail a goal while being inconsistent; a consistent sufficient set may contain a removable child. A refined goal can still be assigned to an actor lacking required observations or control. C0004 preserves the exact source distinction: assignability uses existence of a compatible witness Δ with `RUN_Δ(ag) = HIST(G)`, whereas actual responsibility satisfaction uses `RUN_actual(ag) ⊆ HIST(G)`. An implementation can enforce stronger behavior and satisfy its responsibility. The correction to the genealogy synopsis must not restore equality as a requirement on the actual implementation. [C0002–C0005 and the full profiles](SPR-L3-CURRENT-PROPERTIES.md).

C0007’s operationalization retains persistence, framing, trigger and scheduling premises. Permission is not eventual action. C0008 distinguishes a satisfying behavior from a causal strategy against the permitted environment and information order. A trace that anticipates a future environmental move can be satisfiable yet unrealizable. C0009’s nonvacuity inquiry looks for a meaningful discriminating witness; it neither certifies all adequacy nor bans every redundancy. C0016 retains the actual control-sensitive conflict classification and its exclusions.

Technical contracts describe model relations. They do not themselves create social agreement or legal authority. Two mutual conditional promises may allow perpetual idleness without a progress premise. This counterexample does not refute valid circular proof rules with their actual causality conditions. Behavioral substitution additionally needs relevant history constraints; individual pre/postcondition checks are not a complete substitute. [C0010–C0011, C0017–C0018].

## 3. Obstacles, uncertainty and residual risk

C0006 preserves deterministic obstacle analysis and its probabilistic variant. A conditional probability needs the actual eligible or triggering population; an empty or zero-probability condition cannot justify a favorable result. Shared antecedents or logical implication do not establish stochastic independence. A high probability of material implication can hide poor performance when the trigger is rare. Joint obstruction needs a justified joint model when dependence matters.

Operational failure probability and uncertainty about that probability are different objects. One decision asks whether an operational quantity meets threshold `q`; another asks how much epistemic probability lies below `q`. The thresholds themselves are decisions, not consequences of probability algebra. P02 retains lower-tail mass, unmatched residual/failure bounds and observation assumptions. Model-qualified satisfaction does not supply external calibration, exhaustive hazard coverage or accepted residual risk. A qualitative obstacle argument or small joint table can be adequate for a narrower question.

The R020 source caution is deliberately narrow: a 50th quantile need not be a distribution’s mode. The retained finite counterexample and coincident-summary control establish that distinction. P02 does not depend on the mistaken equivalence, so the caution did not warrant a retention reversal. The documentary account preserves that proportionate consequence rather than turning a source wording defect into a new universal rejection. ## 5. Revision, contemporary domains and source-specific ceilings

Iteration and monitoring support learning without silently revising commitments. Changed requirements need their actual reason, relevant authority and affected dependencies. A later relaxed criterion does not retroactively satisfy an earlier stronger one. C0202 retains observation population, model version and time-window limits; PENDING and UNKNOWN remain legitimate. A harmless reversible clarification may need only the existing issue and a short explanation. A draft standard is not automatically a published or adopted replacement. [C0201–C0204, C0217–C0218].

Safety, security, privacy, accessibility and sustainability are not interchangeable quality labels. Hazard analysis does not prove exhaustive coverage. Security controls can leave privacy relationships and information use unresolved. Accessibility claims have a scope and whole-process obligations. An energy metric does not exhaust sustainability dimensions and horizons. Incident reports and surveys do not establish universal requirements failure rates. The current source record distinguishes catalogue identity, publication status and actual normative-text access rather than treating any official-looking page as a full clause appraisal. Data-dependent acceptance needs population, labels, loss and deployment conditions. ## 6. Decisions, artifact roles and alternatives

Prioritization makes judgments inspectable without making them accurate or authoritative. Ordinal ranking, ratio judgments and cardinal utility differ. A consistency ratio does not establish representative participation or future value. Release feasibility concerns the full declared hard constraint set; a solver cannot repair a missing obligation or decide unresolved normative scope. Exact optimization and valid decision inputs remain different claims. [C0301–C0302, C0305–C0306].

The finite alternatives expose concrete limits. For minimization points `(0,3)`, `(2,2)` and `(3,0)`, the middle point is nondominated yet selected by no positive weighted sum. A finite penalty can permit violation of a hard constraint when opposing benefit exceeds it. A sufficiently justified coefficient can work in a bounded model, but its gain bound must cover relevant infeasible alternatives, not only the hard-feasible subset. Rounding a continuous relaxation can violate discrete feasibility. These findings preserve qualified optimization and direct enumeration while rejecting universal automatic translations. [C0309–C0310 and their profiles].

Reuse and feature-model tests depend on current meanings, not just file identity. Pairwise-compatible feature constraints can be jointly inconsistent. A stable test can have a stale oracle after the validity relation changes. Proving reuse may cost more than rerunning a cheap check. C0307 distinguishes eleven artifact roles, including requirements specifications, change requests, work descriptions, objectives/responses, briefs, experimental protocols, verification/validation plans, acceptance records, assume-guarantee contracts and assurance arguments. They can be combined proportionately, but they are not synonyms and a schema-complete or signed artifact cannot supply false or missing premises. [C0303–C0304, C0307–C0312].
