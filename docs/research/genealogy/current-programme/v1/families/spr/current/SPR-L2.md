## 4. Analysis, transformation, compilation and execution

An intermediate representation has invariants for a consumer. A sound overapproximation can remain too imprecise to resolve a particular question. Rewrites need the actual observations, effects, numeric semantics and side conditions. Equality exploration and bounded extraction are different operations: a valid equality does not establish the optimality of the chosen expression, and a good extraction score cannot repair invalid semantics. Stale analysis metadata can defeat a rule whose abstract form is sound. [C0101–C0105].

Compiler assurance is similarly scoped. A preservation theorem needs exact source and target semantics, admitted behaviors and assumptions. Translation validation supplies its checker-defined instance result and must preserve timeout or inconclusive outcomes. Proof-carrying results are checked against an explicit consumer policy. A verified transformation can still depend on a runtime, linker, backend, hardware behavior or executable correspondence outside the proof. Reproducible builds and diverse double-compiling can supply their respective conditional evidence without becoming universal semantic certification. Finite testing and source-only review retain their useful scope and explicit negative limits. [C0106–C0109, C0116–C0117, C0303, C0308–C0309].

Modular and secure compilation require compatible contexts and linking relations. A theorem for ordinary functional behavior does not automatically cover adversarial contexts or omitted side channels. Concurrent transformations need the actual event and memory model; temporal guarantees add scheduling, fairness and environment premises. A single semantic model can serve several consumers, but each consumer still needs its own projection and proof obligation. Separate small models with justified interfaces can be preferable to one costly universal semantics. [C0110–C0113; CM04 and CM07].

Compatibility and reuse are relations across versions, consumers and observations. A harmless rename may preserve an argument while changed units or a changed assumption invalidate it without changing a downstream byte. Recomputing a cheap result can be preferable to maintaining a dependency cache. The claim requires complete relevant dependencies or a proved invariant change; the cache hit rate is not the criterion of correctness. [C0114–C0115; CM08].

## 5. Generation, synthesis, formalization and human evidence

Staging requires phase and binding preservation; deterministic generation alone does not guarantee generated behavior. A shared language definition can keep parser, editor and generator internally consistent while they share a wrong domain interpretation. DSLs and workbenches have maintenance costs, supported-fragment limits and consumer obligations. Direct APIs, ordinary formats and simple templates remain legitimate alternatives. [C0201–C0204; CM09].

Synthesis needs an explicit candidate class, specification and validating oracle. Finite example consistency can leave several general functions possible. A complete finite-domain check can decide its specified finite claim; it does not need an unbounded theorem to be useful. Conversely, unsuccessful heuristic search is not universal unsatisfiability, and a weak oracle does not supply missing intent. Refinement and termination restrictions belong to the exact synthesis claim. [C0205–C0206, C0217; CM10].

Declarative rules can derive model-relative conclusions while realizability and operational authority remain separate. A satisfying trace need not be a strategy available under the permitted information order. An obligation label does not grant a capability or permission to execute. Descriptive reporting can have no new effectful-action requirement at all. [C0207–C0209; CM11].

Formalization is an accountable interpretation. A proof can validate the wrong statement; a round-trip paraphrase can preserve a shared mistake. A justified reference, exact artifact binding and claim-appropriate validation matter. A generated artifact’s correctness, a benchmark result and total programming benefit have different evidence targets. Human-cost comparisons must preserve task, population, outcome, comparator and lifecycle burden. Formal type guarantees and empirical language studies cannot be averaged into a universal language ranking. A disposable script can rationally use a simpler interface for its narrower consequence. [C0210–C0218; CM06 and CM12].

The additional security, probabilistic and differentiation branches also resist false equivalence. Consumers of the same bytes can disagree about security-relevant meaning; source presentation is distinct from parsing and binding. Non-malleable serialization applies to its specified format subset. Probabilistic semantics requires normalization and observation conditions; those facts alone do not prove a derivative. Automatic differentiation needs its actual derivative and composition semantics, including nested use. Blanket character bans, a single checker or a universal canonical representation cannot settle all these claims. [C0301–C0309].

## 6. The accepted C0310 correction

C0310 is the material r2 scientific correction retained exactly in accepted r3. The former guard could classify a successful safeguard as inapplicable merely because it prevented any effect before validation. That confused the reason the obligation applies with the success of the mechanism satisfying it. The corrected bearer is a fixed tuple: semantic operation, contract, validation-unit interpretation and governed effect projection, with relevant versions bound. The service must include that effect and the contract must prohibit release attributable to an invalid unit. Then the actual mechanism is evaluated. [Complete correction report](SOURCES.md#spr-l2).

For each declared unit, invalidity implies no released effect attributable to it. Validation before commit or a proved equivalent isolation/rollback rule can justify that safety result. A protected operation can therefore have a true applicability guard and satisfy its predicate. An intrinsically effectless operation or an explicitly permissive contract can establish a real nontrigger. Unknown operation or contract identity leaves applicability unknown. Missing attribution or incomplete mediation does not become false applicability. A genuine in-scope invalid-unit release can refute the claim despite incomplete positive coverage.

The historical construction considers two frames, four Boolean validity assignments and three release policies. Eager release fails for invalid frames. Per-frame validation and stipulated isolated speculation preserve applicability and satisfy the finite scoped implication. A valid first frame followed by an invalid second frame distinguishes contracts: committing the first frame is allowed when each frame is a unit, but violates a contract treating the pair as one invalid atomic message. A whole-message buffer satisfies that changed finite model. This does not require buffering every unbounded stream.
