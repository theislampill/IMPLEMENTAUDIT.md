## ESS-001 — Explicit unacceptable-loss framing

### Current status

STRONGLY_RETAINED

### Mature or evolved form

A concise, current, decision-linked definition of unacceptable loss that bounds later analysis without pretending completeness.

### Mechanism

Name losses, affected stakeholders and decision authority; derive hazardous system states and constraints from them.

### Trigger or context

Any consequential system, material change, novel operation or unresolved safety decision.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Stakeholders and authority must be known; loss statements must be specific enough to discriminate decisions.

### Authority or control preconditions

A competent owner must be authorised to define tolerability and resolve conflicts among safety, mission and availability.

### Required feedback or observability

Evidence that the loss set still matches current use, users, environment and public exposure.

### Decision or consumer

Design authority, operator, regulator or risk-acceptance authority.

### Non trigger or cheap path

For trivial reversible work, state the bounded loss in one line and use the authoritative deterministic check; no elaborate taxonomy.

### Known failure modes

Losses framed too broadly become slogans; too narrowly omit public/mission harms; economic inconvenience can be mislabelled as safety.

### Important criticisms

Different domains define accident, mishap and harm differently; not every adverse outcome is a safety loss.

### Contrary evidence

No source establishes one universal loss vocabulary or acceptable threshold.

### Open questions

How should distributional, long-latency and intergenerational harms be represented without making every objective a safety loss?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is the decision boundary; a “loss list” document or STPA worksheet is optional.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A safety decision and its loss boundary

**Criterion:** The stated loss set distinguishes the harms relevant to this decision, their affected parties and the authority accepting tradeoffs; it does not claim exhaustive capture of every social objective.

**Criterion type:** CONDITIONAL_METHOD_OR_CONTROL_CRITERION

**Quantifier:** For each proposed application or assurance claim within the declared system boundary, conditions and decision horizon; not a claim that every real system satisfies it.

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A compliance-successful design still admits an explicitly unacceptable injury or service loss omitted from the boundary.

**Cheap path and reopening:** A concise loss statement suffices for a bounded reversible task; reopen on materially changed use, exposure or stakeholder loss.

## ESS-002 — Hazardous system-state framing distinct from failure

### Current status

STRONGLY_RETAINED

### Mature or evolved form

A current loss-linked hazardous-state model with explicit boundary, exposure and causal assumptions.

### Mechanism

Model hazards separately from causes; then search failures, interactions, environmental conditions and control flaws that can create them.

### Trigger or context

Systems with software, humans, adaptive functions, hazardous energy/material, changing environments or nontrivial interfaces.

### Dependencies or preconditions

A defined system boundary, environment and loss relation; hazards must be controllable enough to inform design or operation.

### Authority or control preconditions

Ownership of system boundary and authority to impose constraints across components/interfaces.

### Required feedback or observability

Observable indicators of hazardous state, exposure and boundary conditions—not only component health.

### Decision or consumer

System architect, safety engineer, operations authority and design teams.

### Non trigger or cheap path

A simple local task with a complete deterministic invariant may use that invariant directly rather than a separate hazard ontology.

### Known failure modes

Hazards become vague nouns, duplicate losses, or are listed without a consumer/control.

### Important criticisms

Hazard definitions differ across MIL-STD-882, IEC/ISO, process safety and STPA; terminology should not be forced.

### Contrary evidence

No practical method can prove that all hazards have been identified in an open sociotechnical system.

### Open questions

What evidence is sufficient to bound a hazard search in adaptive or open-world systems?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is the loss-capable state model; exact hazard-log columns are not required.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A hazardous system state in a declared environment

**Criterion:** Represent the state and contextual conditions capable of producing the loss, including unsafe interactions without a component fault, separately from failure labels.

## ESS-003 — Enforceable safety constraints

### Current status

STRONGLY_RETAINED

### Mature or evolved form

The minimum sufficient constraint is explicit, allocated, enforceable, verified and paired with feedback or bounded assurance.

### Mechanism

Derive constraints from hazards; allocate them to controllers/components/procedures; verify implementation and operational enforcement.

### Trigger or context

Whenever a credible hazard remains after elimination or redesign.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Constraint must be testable or otherwise assessable and tied to a controller with feasible authority.

### Authority or control preconditions

The allocated controller must possess real control authority and resources; responsibility without authority is insufficient.

### Required feedback or observability

State/feedback showing the constraint is maintained and alarms when it is violated or unverifiable.

### Decision or consumer

Design authority, controller owner, verifier, operator and acceptance authority.

### Non trigger or cheap path

Where a deterministic existing rule already proves the safe condition, record/reuse it rather than create duplicate safety requirements.

### Known failure modes

Vague “shall be safe” statements, contradictory constraints, unenforceable human instructions, and unmonitored assumptions.

### Important criticisms

Constraint language can become model bureaucracy; not all safety behaviour is reducible to static rules.

### Contrary evidence

Constraint satisfaction cannot prove safety if the hazard model or environment assumptions are wrong.

### Open questions

How should adaptive controllers revise constraints without invalidating their assurance basis?

### Ceremony vs property

A requirements database is optional; live allocation and enforcement are the property.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A selected hazard constraint and its enforcing control

**Criterion:** For the relevant state and horizon, identify an enforceable prevention or mitigation condition, an actor or mechanism able to enforce it, and feedback or other justified assurance of its effect.

**Criterion type:** CONDITIONAL_METHOD_OR_CONTROL_CRITERION

**Quantifier:** For each proposed application or assurance claim within the declared system boundary, conditions and decision horizon; not a claim that every real system satisfies it.

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A policy forbids an unsafe release but the only named actor cannot inhibit the release or observe its result.

**Cheap path and reopening:** A physical limit or complete deterministic check can provide the adequate control without an elaborate feedback workflow.

**Current scientific status:** STRONGLY_RETAINED

## ESS-004 — Lifecycle hazard identification with explicit completeness boundaries

### Current status

USEFUL_BUT_EASILY_BUREAUCRATISED

### Mature or evolved form

Claim bounded coverage over a declared population; never claim that all possible hazards are known.

### Mechanism

Define search scope, phases, methods, expertise, exclusions, assumptions and residual unknowns; revisit on triggers.

### Trigger or context

Novel/high-consequence systems; phase transitions; operational change; significant incidents; emerging evidence.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Access to lifecycle knowledge and representative operators/maintainers; explicit stopping/saturation criteria.

### Authority or control preconditions

A named owner must decide when coverage is sufficient and when reopening is required.

### Required feedback or observability

Coverage ledger, unresolved assumptions, operational signals and change triggers.

### Decision or consumer

Safety manager, system owner, change authority and risk acceptor.

### Non trigger or cheap path

Low-consequence reversible work needs only a bounded check of known hazards and the deterministic discriminator, not a comprehensive workshop.

### Known failure modes

Checklist completion, duplicate analyses, expertise gaps, search stopping by schedule, and stale hazard logs.

### Important criticisms

True completeness is unprovable for open systems; exhaustive process can consume effort without changing design.

### Contrary evidence

No comparative evidence establishes a universal optimal analysis depth or meeting format.

### Open questions

What stopping rules best balance unknown-hazard discovery against analysis cost for continuously changing systems?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is coverage and reopening discipline; a prescribed list of analysis reports is domain-specific.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** Lifecycle hazard search for a bounded system

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A maintenance or disposal state lies outside an otherwise thorough operational hazard list and has a credible loss path.

**Cheap path and reopening:** Reuse a justified unchanged scope or a small targeted search; reopen only the material unresolved or changed boundary.

## ESS-005 — Component failure-effects analysis as a bounded complement

### Current status

CONTEXT_DEPENDENT

### Mature or evolved form

Use FMEA for what it represents well; do not treat a completed FMEA as a complete system-safety analysis.

### Mechanism

Perform function/item-based FMEA/FMECA focused on safety-significant elements and feed results into system hazard/control analysis.

### Trigger or context

Hardware/component/process failure modes are material and interfaces are sufficiently defined.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Defined functions/boundaries, knowledgeable participants and connection to system consequences and controls.

### Authority or control preconditions

Owners must be able to change design, detection, maintenance or architecture based on findings.

### Required feedback or observability

Failure detection coverage, field failures, common-cause data and control effectiveness.

### Decision or consumer

Component designer, system safety analyst, reliability engineer and verifier.

### Non trigger or cheap path

A full worksheet is not generally required; use any adequate representation of the material failure-effects question. A non-safety-significant item or a conclusive direct discriminator may need little analysis. Cheap replacement alone does not establish that failure consequences, propagation and recovery delay are acceptably bounded.

### Known failure modes

Mechanical worksheet completion, arbitrary ranking, omission of interactions/software/operational context and stale failure libraries.

### Important criticisms

Bottom-up FMEA can miss unsafe interactions without component failure and is often undermined by weak occurrence data.

### Contrary evidence

Comparative evidence that FMEA improves safety outcomes is limited and confounded by implementation quality.

### Open questions

How can automated model-based FMEA preserve expert challenge rather than scale stale assumptions?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is structured failure search and effect propagation; the worksheet and RPN are not general requirements.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A component failure-effects study

**Criterion:** Use failure-mode analysis when its item/function decomposition can discriminate a material system consequence; an adequate representation need not be a full worksheet.

**Criterion type:** CONDITIONAL_METHOD_OR_CONTROL_CRITERION

**Quantifier:** For each proposed application or assurance claim within the declared system boundary, conditions and decision horizon; not a claim that every real system satisfies it.

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A cheap replaceable component causes harm before replacement in CASE-04; this concerns loss scope, not the necessity of a worksheet.

**Cheap path and reopening:** Use a simpler adequate representation or conclusive direct discriminator; replacement cost alone does not establish bounded failure consequences.

## ESS-006 — Top-down causal-path and logical analysis

### Current status

CONTEXT_DEPENDENT

### Mature or evolved form

Use top-down logic where the event structure is meaningful. Add other searches when material interactions or organizational causes remain outside the justified boundary; one adequate method can suffice for a bounded question.

### Mechanism

Construct qualitative logic first; quantify only with defensible data/dependence models; use event trees for post-initiator progression.

### Trigger or context

A well-defined top event, architecture and causal logic; quantitative use requires data and dependence modelling.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Stable boundary, meaningful decomposition and explicit common-cause/uncertainty assumptions.

### Authority or control preconditions

Decision owner can act on dominant cut sets, barriers or design alternatives.

### Required feedback or observability

Configuration currentness, event definitions, dependency/common-cause evidence and operational data.

### Decision or consumer

System safety/PRA analyst, architect, operations and risk authority.

### Non trigger or cheap path

For a simple deterministic invariant, use direct proof/test; do not construct a tree that merely restates the invariant.

### Known failure modes

Omitted events, false independence, boundary truncation, unjustified probabilities and treating tree completeness as world completeness.

### Important criticisms

Trees represent only modelled causes and may omit organisational/software interactions and adaptive behaviour.

### Contrary evidence

Sparse catastrophic data and unknown interactions limit quantitative calibration.

### Open questions

How should causal models represent adaptive software and organisational decisions without becoming unmaintainable?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is transparent causal decomposition; gate symbols and large diagrams are optional.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A top-down causal or event-logic model

**Criterion:** The modeled events, dependencies and cut sets must support the claimed qualitative or quantitative conclusion; supplement the search where material causes remain outside its representational scope.

**Criterion type:** CONDITIONAL_METHOD_OR_CONTROL_CRITERION

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A fault tree quantifies correlated basic events as independent or omits a credible organizational control path it is claimed to cover.

**Cheap path and reopening:** Use a direct proof for a complete finite invariant; one adequate search can suffice when its boundary is justified.

## ESS-007 — Structured process-deviation search

### Current status

CONTEXT_DEPENDENT

### Mature or evolved form

Structured deviation prompts plus empowered expertise and action closure; method depth proportional to hazard and change.

### Mechanism

Use guidewords or equivalent structured prompts at nodes/functions; record decisions, actions and unresolved assumptions.

### Trigger or context

Hazardous processes, complex flows/procedures or modifications where deviation search has decision value.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Accurate design intent, competent multidisciplinary team, bounded nodes and action follow-through.

### Authority or control preconditions

Facilitator/review team must be able to obtain design changes or escalate unresolved hazards.

### Required feedback or observability

Action completion, safeguard state, deviations/near misses and change currentness.

### Decision or consumer

Process design authority, operations, maintenance and hazard owner.

### Non trigger or cheap path

Do not convene a full HAZOP for a tiny reversible change with a complete change discriminator; use focused review.

### Known failure modes

Guideword ritual, excessive scope, fatigue, repeated low-value findings and action lists without closure.

### Important criticisms

Conventional HAZOP does not by itself estimate likelihood or establish complete causal coverage.

### Contrary evidence

No evidence supports HAZOP as a universal method outside matching process/deviation problems.

### Open questions

What automated assistance improves coverage without anchoring teams to generated but shallow deviations?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is disciplined deviation search; exact guideword tables and workshop duration are not universal.

**Bearer:** A process-deviation investigation

## ESS-008 — Unsafe interaction and unsafe-control analysis

### Current status

STRONGLY_RETAINED

### Mature or evolved form

Explicitly reason about unsafe composition and control context, using the cheapest adequate representation and complementary techniques.

### Mechanism

Model controllers, controlled processes, actions, context and feedback; identify unsafe combinations and causal scenarios.

### Trigger or context

Software-intensive, autonomous, tightly coupled, multi-controller or human-automation systems.

### Dependencies or preconditions

A usable control/interaction model, representative operational knowledge and explicit system boundary.

### Authority or control preconditions

Controllers must have clear authority and conflict-resolution rules; analysis findings need a design/operations consumer.

### Required feedback or observability

Actual action timing/order/duration, process state, controller modes, feedback quality and handoffs.

### Decision or consumer

System architect, software/control designer, human factors, operations and safety authority.

### Non trigger or cheap path

Where a simple component failure fully determines the hazard, use the cheaper failure analysis and direct control; do not force a control diagram.

### Known failure modes

Subjective models, analyst-dependent results, diagram growth, unvalidated completeness and failure to connect scenarios to controls.

### Important criticisms

STPA is not uniquely entitled to interaction reasoning; other system safety, SOTIF, HAZOP and scenario methods can contribute.

### Contrary evidence

Comparative evidence does not establish STPA as universally more effective or scalable than all alternatives.

### Open questions

Which interaction-analysis combinations provide reproducible coverage for adaptive AI-enabled systems?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is interaction/control reasoning; STPA terminology and diagrams are optional.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A control structure and unsafe-action scenario set

**Criterion:** Examine whether missing, incorrect, mistimed or prolonged control and flawed interactions can violate constraints within the declared system boundary, including failure of actuation after a safe command.

**Criterion type:** CONDITIONAL_METHOD_OR_CONTROL_CRITERION

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** The command is safe but a delivery or actuator defect prevents its intended control; searching unsafe commands alone misses the loss path.

**Cheap path and reopening:** A complete local invariant can replace a large control model for its narrow claim; reopen when causal pathways extend beyond it.

## ESS-009 — Closed-loop control and feedback integrity

### Current status

CONTROL_LOOP_PROPERTY

### Mature or evolved form

Every consequential control has evidence sufficient to establish intended state, or the system treats state as unknown and constrains operation.

### Mechanism

Specify required state feedback, freshness, validity and independent confirmation; design fallback for unobservable state.

### Trigger or context

Consequential control actions, remote/autonomous actuation, interlocks, shutdown, recovery and distributed control.

### Dependencies or preconditions

Sensor/feedback path coverage, semantics and timing must match the safety decision.

### Authority or control preconditions

Controller must be authorised to act on missing/invalid feedback and to enter a safe/degraded mode.

### Required feedback or observability

State-based confirmation, feedback freshness/integrity, actuator/process health and observability gaps.

### Decision or consumer

Controller, operator, safety monitor and restart authority.

### Non trigger or cheap path

For local directly observable reversible actions, immediate observation/test can serve as the feedback path without additional instrumentation.

### Known failure modes

Common-mode sensor errors, feedback derived from the command rather than process, alarm flooding and unhandled uncertainty.

### Important criticisms

Additional sensors can share failure modes; feedback can create complexity or false confidence.

### Contrary evidence

No feedback architecture eliminates epistemic uncertainty or common-mode failure.

### Open questions

How should systems express and govern “unknown safety state” without causing unsafe service loss?

### Ceremony vs property

The property is state confirmation and bounded response; a particular control diagram is not required.

### Current assessment

**Bearer:** A safety-relevant control loop

**Criterion:** The controller receives sufficiently accurate, timely and interpretable state evidence for its claimed decisions, with a justified response when that evidence is missing or stale.

**Horizon:** The exposure, operating mode and time horizon stated by the actual safety decision; no universal duration is inferred.

**Discriminating witness:** A stop command is acknowledged while the hazardous process continues unobserved.

**Cheap path and reopening:** Direct immediate observation or passive bounded assurance may suffice; unknown state warrants a context-specific constraint, not automatic shutdown in every system.

CONTROL_LOOP_PROPERTY

### Mechanism
