# How Cognitive Systems Engineering, Statistical Engineering and Systems Safety interact

**Current derived scientific account — CSS-S, normalized 13 November 2026.** This account preserves the selected accepted study and its original qualifications. The historical accounts and earlier disclosure records remain dated history. The scientific review of the source does not establish deployed effectiveness, native implementation, or acceptance of this new presentation.

The synthesis identifies conditional contributions among three distinct traditions. Cognitive Systems Engineering makes the actual work, people, tools, information and resources part of the question. Statistical Engineering clarifies what an observation or comparison supports, with what uncertainty and dependence. Systems Safety supplies the consequential states, loss paths, complete response requirements and legitimate constraints that make an answer useful.

Their productive combination depends on an actual shared object and a specific receiving purpose. A valid measurement can be too late; a successful command can leave a fault unresolved; several individually useful tasks can overfill the same response window. A contribution may constrain the receiving question without establishing its entire answer. The selected synthesis develops six directional families, nine configurations, six higher-order findings and explicit adverse and unresolved cases. None of its eight proposed novelty candidates warrants a new property identity at the declared scope.

The account is analytical and finite. The examples below discriminate claims within stated models; they are not observations from an actual plant, organization or human population. Existing local scientific qualification is reported at that scope, with the original-history gaps and review exposure preserved.

<a id="D-AB"></a>

## Cognitive Systems Engineering → Statistical Engineering


Cognitive work specifies what the measurement or comparison must concern. Individual knowledge, distributed team knowledge, subjective workload, complete recovery, current mode understanding and whole-task performance have different bearers. Adding individual scores is not automatically a distributed construct; measuring first movement is not measuring completed recovery; an automation's component accuracy does not by itself represent the joint task. These distinctions define the observational unit, outcome, task conditions and consequential error that statistical work must preserve.

Work also supplies the actual cost and feasibility of collecting or acting on evidence. A monitor that generates a response creates interruption, routing, resumption and shared-resource work. A proposed probe can interrupt the state it measures. A lot-inspection plan can create a real inspection task; a corrective process can create later feedback and effect-check work. These contributions require an actual work path. Merely proposing to measure a method, or naming a hypothetical consumer, does not establish one.

For comparison and prediction, cognitive work identifies relevant task mixtures, learning opportunities, participant or crew units, interference and transport questions. A forecast must target the complete outcome and the intended event mix. A factorial comparison needs actual factors and a consequential interaction; a local response model needs quantitative controls and a justified local response region. These are restrictions on a real study, not mandates for an experiment, a smooth human-performance surface or a named design in every task.

A communication or handoff purpose can independently determine which statistical meaning must remain distinguishable: observed value versus inference, natural band versus requirement, forecast versus present fact, lot disposition versus process restoration. Recipient uptake does not make the statement true. A shared factual message also differs from independently acquired exploratory information.

<a id="D-BA"></a>

## Statistical Engineering → Cognitive Systems Engineering


Statistical Engineering limits what cognitive measurements can justify. Construct definition, decision-specific discrimination, systematic and random error, uncertainty, evaluator disagreement, reference scope and transport conditions can narrow a score's meaning. A precise or repeatable score need not be valid. A workload rating does not establish human capability, safe allocation or physical control; a subjective scale is not automatically a lifetime model.

Physical reference information can nevertheless materially affect a human-work claim. If a claimed duration, physical exposure or response margin uses a measurement result, its actual reference, correction, range or uncertainty can change what that unchanged human observation warrants. The reference does not establish learning or skill. This preserves a physical component inside a broader human claim without converting physical calibration into a human scale. The r4 P003 inquiry retains 260 conditional directions and two bounded negatives at ECSE-23; the exception is not a rejection of every physical component of cognitive work.

Detection and prediction can influence reliance, alarm response and fallback, but only at their actual targets. A detector's false-alarm, miss and delay tradeoff may change work. A stable process band does not establish an engineering requirement. A capability index is not current state or a person's ability. An old event mixture can misstate future performance even if conditional performance remains unchanged. A chart has to have an adequately measured stream, useful signal and capable consumer; rejecting a universal chart mandate preserves this narrower use.

Statistical design constrains claims about cognitive interventions. Selection, assignment, blocking, actual experimental units, interaction aliases, multiplicity, stopping and evaluator change are relevant when the claim consumes them. They do not replace the construct or create a causal effect. A full factorial is unnecessary if a consequential rival is already excluded; a direct valid discriminator can settle a narrow question. Repeated observations may add conditional information without becoming independent replications.

<a id="D-BC"></a>

## Statistical Engineering → Systems Safety


A safety argument can consume a statistical input while leaving its control and authority premises open. Uncertainty and detection delay limit the state or warning time credited to sensing. Exposure, censoring, dependence and mission conditions limit reliability and event-rate claims. Version and evaluator changes can stale the evidence relied upon by an assurance claim. None of these establishes hazard completeness, current physical safety or permission to operate.

The bounded lot example is particularly important. P026 preserves a defined lot, selection and measurement conditions, prespecified operating risks and the meaning of the disposition. P027 separately retains chronological process feedback, defect provenance and justified corrective work. The accepted lot is relevant to a specified restart or control mechanism, but the same lot observation can coexist with restored and faulty process states. Even a complete census of that lot does not establish the different current process-effect claim.

A single acquisition can legitimately inform several consumers. Its reuse does not become several independent acquisitions. D346 makes the actual lot-origin correspondence material to the informational dependence question; it neither maps lot items onto physical redundant channels nor assigns a probability to human reviewers. D347 independently states the receiving independence claim's need for sufficient origin information.

Evidence value and methodological economy also matter to safety when a discretionary evidence choice exists. An expensive exact oracle can have lower net value than a cheaper adequate signal. Evidence already obtained may remain valid even if purchasing another observation would not be worthwhile. A realized unchanged action does not refute prospective information or learning value. These comparisons are constrained by actual duties and do not grant waiver authority.

<a id="D-CB"></a>

## Systems Safety → Statistical Engineering


Safety reasoning identifies the particular loss, hazardous condition, required state, mission, response horizon and consequential error that make a statistical result useful. This can define an estimand, failure event, specification or detection deadline. It does not make a probability distribution known, select a numerical risk tolerance by arithmetic, or turn a nominal requirement into an effective control.

The receiver's actual use can require a distinction that a statistical summary omits. A time-uniform guarantee about a specified average is different from current-state assurance. The complete response, including actuation and final feedback, may determine the latest useful evidence time. A safety requirement can constrain an adjustment policy while preserving justified direct correction, planned learning and adequate fixed policies.

Configuration and change scope can determine which data remain applicable. A supplier's actual role and interface can define what lot acceptance needs to discriminate and what system questions remain. Reporting incentives, missed observations and denominator changes can qualify an incident trend. A genuine authority, preservation or separation duty limits which acquisition or retirement alternatives are available; it does not supply metrological calibration or human skill.

## Cognitive Systems Engineering → Systems Safety


Cognitive work contributes the concrete means by which people or teams participate in control. State and mode representations identify what the actor can observe; feedback distinguishes a command, acknowledgement and achieved world effect. Allocation, retained capability, coordination, handoff, interruption and resumption constrain the actual complete response. A trained, nominally assigned or approving person is not automatically able, permitted, informed or available to perform it.

The joint-work boundary can expose human, tool and organizational dependencies relevant to hazard analysis; work-as-performed can expose causal rivals that a terminal “human error” label hides. Actual attention and maintenance requirements can make an assurance portfolio infeasible. The source supplies these inputs at their scope. It does not choose acceptable losses, establish a failure rate, confer authority or prove the effectiveness of the entire safeguard.
