## Explanation from the public predecessor

### What the public predecessor supports

The central unit is the bounded joint work system: the people, agents, automation, procedures and representations that actually contribute to a consequential outcome. A useful boundary makes dependencies visible while leaving responsibility and the next action assignable. Within that boundary, the historical ledger distinguishes state and mode legibility (ECSE-07), pending and committed transitions (ECSE-08), freshness and provenance (ECSE-09), and command-to-world closure (ECSE-10). Making one of these visible does not establish the others. [Cognitive ledger][c-ledger]

**Illustrative case: an acknowledged remote action.** A worker sends a command and receives “accepted.” For a queued consequential action, that acknowledgement can establish receipt while execution and the resulting state remain pending. The receiver needs enough transition, provenance and effect information to decide whether to wait, investigate, stop or recover. For an atomic local action with immediate reliable feedback, the effect itself can be sufficient. This is an illustration of ECSE-08/10's distinction and cheap path, not a report of a new evaluated system. [Cognitive ledger, ECSE-08 and ECSE-10][c-ledger]

Authority must be usable. ECSE-11/12 require state access, competence, attention, controls and enough time for the proposed intervention; a nominal approver does not supply those conditions. ECSE-13/14 separate a transmitted handoff from accepted obligations, live-state verification and re-entry readiness. A receiver who knows that rollback exists may still lack the information or authority to apply it safely. Conversely, simple same-context work can require only a brief handoff because the relevant state and responsibility remain visible. [Cognitive ledger, ECSE-11–ECSE-15][c-ledger]

Common ground and independent exploration have different uses. People coordinating an action need shared authoritative facts, hazards and constraints. When several plausible explanations remain and a deterministic discriminator is unavailable, conclusion-neutral first passes or different evidence searches can reduce common anchoring. The relevant question is whether the information differs in a way that could change the decision, not how many agents or reviewers were counted. [Cognitive ledger, ECSE-23–ECSE-25 and ECSE-38][c-ledger]

The historical public corpus explicitly retains criticism and refusal. ECSE-50 does not establish mature general evidence for workload-triggered authority transfer. ECSE-53–ECSE-62 reject static universal allocation rules, ceremonial oversight, trust maximisation, information monotonicity, a mode display as sufficient understanding, human-error endpoints, narrative reasoning as faithful transparency, maximal alerts, acknowledgement as comprehension and universal workload minimisation. These are substantive conclusions about transfer limits. They are not unimplemented features. [Cognitive ledger][c-ledger] · [Public intake limits][c-intake]

### ECSE-01 — Joint cognitive work system as the unit of design

**Historical origin:** Hollnagel and Woods (1983), Rasmussen et al. (1994), Hutchins (1995), Hollnagel and Woods (2005)

**Original form:** Analyse cognition and control across the coupled human-machine-work system rather than treating the person as an isolated component.

**Development:** From component human factors to cognitive engineering, distributed cognition and joint cognitive systems.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Define the work system boundary, cognitive functions, authority, information flows, dependencies, timing and adaptation; test joint performance, not merely component usability.

**Problem:** Local component optimisation can degrade system-level coordination, conceal displaced work, and leave no owner for coupling failures.

**Mechanism:** Model people, automation, procedures, representations and organisational supports as interdependent contributors to one work function; evaluate joint outcomes and coordination costs.

**Trigger and context:** Consequential work in which decisions, representations, authority or control are distributed across two or more participants or artefacts.

**Preconditions:** A stable-enough work boundary, access to work-as-done, and a named engineering consumer.

**Authority preconditions:** Authority and accountability must remain assignable even when cognition is distributed.

**State and mode preconditions:** Current shared and private state relevant to coordination must be identifiable.

**Timing and attention preconditions:** Temporal coupling and decision deadlines must be represented.

**Decision or consumer:** System architect, operations owner, safety/assurance owner, and the actors who perform the work.

**Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:** For a low-consequence, single-actor, transparent deterministic operation, document the actor, state and effect without constructing a full joint-system model.

**Disposition at the historical freeze:** JOINT_COGNITIVE_SYSTEM_PROPERTY

**Historical disposition group:** TRANSFERABLE_OR_CROSSWALK_WORTHY

**Failure modes:** The boundary can become so expansive that analysis loses actionability or responsibility becomes diffuse.; Over-application can add cognitive load, delay, false authority or bureaucracy when the trigger does not hold.

**Criticism:** The boundary can become so expansive that analysis loses actionability or responsibility becomes diffuse.

**Contrary evidence:** Human-AI combinations do not show synergy on average, so joint-system framing is a design obligation, not evidence that pairing is beneficial.

**Unresolved questions:** How should boundaries be kept actionable in highly networked organisations and multi-agent ecosystems?

**Property versus ceremony:** A system map, dashboard or workshop is not the property; the property is engineering the consequential couplings and evaluating joint outcomes.

**Evidence profile at the historical freeze:** ACCIDENT_OR_INCIDENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVITY: MODERATE; payoff depends on consequence, coupling, timing and implementation quality; CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; CONTRARY_EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: MODERATE; mainly limits universal implementations rather than the underlying property; DOMAIN_PRACTICE_STRENGTH: HIGH_IN_ESTABLISHED_DOMAINS; FIELD_EMPIRICAL_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; HISTORICAL_PROVENANCE_STRENGTH: HIGH; LAB_EXPERIMENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE; OVERALL: STRONG_WITH_CONTEXTUAL_IMPLEMENTATION_LIMITS; RATIONALE: Primary evidence: Cross-domain mechanism evidence and case evidence are strong; controlled comparative evidence for any single modelling notation is weaker. ### ECSE-02 — Decision-relevant work-domain and constraint modelling

**Historical origin:** Rasmussen's abstraction hierarchy; Rasmussen, Pejtersen and Goodstein (1994); Naikar's CWA development

**Original form:** Represent purposes, values, functions, processes, physical resources and means-ends links independently of one prescribed task sequence.

**Development:** From comprehensive work-domain analysis toward proportional constraint modelling and live maintenance.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Maintain an auditable, decision-consumed constraint model at the smallest useful granularity; link representations and procedures to it.

**Problem:** Procedure-only descriptions hide invariant constraints and give operators little support when conditions depart from the nominal script.

**Mechanism:** Elicit the constraints, boundaries, resources, trade-offs and means-ends relations that determine which actions remain viable; expose the minimum subset needed by live decisions.

**Trigger and context:** Complex, variable or safety/consequence-sensitive work in which unanticipated states or multiple strategies are plausible.

**Preconditions:** Domain expertise, access to actual work, a model owner and change-control linkage.

**Authority preconditions:** The model must distinguish who may trade off which goals and who can cross or relax constraints.

**State and mode preconditions:** The operational state variables and constraints represented must be measurable or inferable with known uncertainty.

**Timing and attention preconditions:** Constraints needed under disturbance must be available before action deadlines.

**Decision or consumer:** Interface designer, procedure designer, training owner, controller/operator and architecture reviewer.

**Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:** For stable routine work, a concise state-transition model, invariant list or decision table may preserve the property without a full CWA programme.

**Disposition at the historical freeze:** RETAINED_IN_EVOLVED_FORM

**Historical disposition group:** TRANSFERABLE_OR_CROSSWALK_WORTHY

**Failure modes:** Full CWA can be costly, analyst-dependent, hard to validate and liable to staleness as software and organisation change.; Over-application can add cognitive load, delay, false authority or bureaucracy when the trigger does not hold.

**Criticism:** Full CWA can be costly, analyst-dependent, hard to validate and liable to staleness as software and organisation change.

**Contrary evidence:** Simpler task analysis or state models may equal the payoff in stable domains; poor analyst choices can encode work-as-imagined.

**Unresolved questions:** What validation and maintenance regimes best detect when a work-domain model has ceased to match work?

**Property versus ceremony:** The multi-stage CWA artefact is optional; the maintained decision-relevant constraint representation is the property.

**Evidence profile at the historical freeze:** ACCIDENT_OR_INCIDENT_STRENGTH: LOW_TO_MODERATE_OR_INDIRECT; ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVITY: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; CONTRARY_EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: MODERATE; DOMAIN_PRACTICE_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH_IN_SOME_DOMAINS; FIELD_EMPIRICAL_STRENGTH: MODERATE; HISTORICAL_PROVENANCE_STRENGTH: HIGH; LAB_EXPERIMENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE; MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE_OR_NOT_CENTRAL; OVERALL: MODERATE_TO_STRONG; RATIONALE: Primary evidence: EID/CWA studies support design value in complex control domains but comparative field evidence and cost-effectiveness remain uneven. ### ECSE-03 — Distributed representations and external cognitive support

**Historical origin:** Hutchins' studies of navigation and cockpit speed representations; cognitive engineering research on tools

**Original form:** Treat representations, marks, displays, procedures and shared artefacts as functional parts of remembering, calculating and coordinating.

**Development:** From display/readability concerns to cognition distributed across people and artefacts.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Design external memory around live tasks, ownership, update semantics, freshness and recovery; test whether it reduces errors rather than merely adding documentation.

**Problem:** Design that assumes all relevant state is held in individual memory creates omission, reconstruction and coordination failures.

**Mechanism:** Place stable, inspectable and updateable state in shared artefacts; make transformations and dependencies visible and preserve provenance across handoffs.

**Trigger and context:** Work with multi-step obligations, interruptions, team coordination, long time spans or high memory demand.

**Preconditions:** Clear update authority, identity, version/freshness semantics and retirement rules.

**Authority preconditions:** Write/update/approve permissions must match responsibility.

**State and mode preconditions:** State stored externally must be distinguishable from inferred or unverified claims.

**Timing and attention preconditions:** The representation must be accessible at the point of decision and resumable after interruption.

**Decision or consumer:** Operators, coordinators, shift leads and downstream decision makers.

**Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:** Do not externalise trivial facts when the representation costs more attention than it saves; use a small status marker or no artefact.

**Disposition at the historical freeze:** STRONGLY_RETAINED

**Historical disposition group:** TRANSFERABLE_OR_CROSSWALK_WORTHY

**Failure modes:** External representations can be stale, inconsistent, overly authoritative, insecure or cognitively expensive.; Over-application can add cognitive load, delay, false authority or bureaucracy when the trigger does not hold.

**Criticism:** External representations can be stale, inconsistent, overly authoritative, insecure or cognitively expensive.

**Contrary evidence:** Shared artefacts may create false consensus or become a single stale source of error.

**Unresolved questions:** How can shared state remain authoritative without suppressing legitimate local dissent or privacy needs?

**Property versus ceremony:** A shared document is not sufficient; the property includes semantics, ownership, freshness and actual decision use.

**Evidence profile at the historical freeze:** ACCIDENT_OR_INCIDENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVITY: MODERATE; payoff depends on consequence, coupling, timing and implementation quality; CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; CONTRARY_EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: MODERATE; mainly limits universal implementations rather than the underlying property; DOMAIN_PRACTICE_STRENGTH: HIGH_IN_ESTABLISHED_DOMAINS; FIELD_EMPIRICAL_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; HISTORICAL_PROVENANCE_STRENGTH: HIGH; LAB_EXPERIMENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE; OVERALL: STRONG_WITH_CONTEXTUAL_IMPLEMENTATION_LIMITS; RATIONALE: Primary evidence: Strong observational and experimental mechanism support; effect sizes depend on representation quality and task coupling. ### ECSE-04 — Coordination design beyond function allocation

**Historical origin:** Criticism of Fitts-list allocation; MABA-MABA critique; joint-activity and coactive-design work

**Original form:** Replace one-time assignment of functions with design of dependencies, communication, observability, predictability and recovery between performers.

**Development:** Static allocation was preserved as descriptive scaffolding but superseded as the complete design problem.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Use function allocation as an input, then close the coordination contracts, transitions, feedback and recovery for the joint activity.

**Problem:** A function can be allocated correctly in isolation yet fail because prerequisites, transitions, exceptions and residual work are uncoordinated.

**Mechanism:** For each interdependent function, engineer who initiates, informs, confirms, monitors, challenges, completes and recovers it, including transition load.

**Trigger and context:** Any consequential split of work between humans, automation, teams or agents.

**Preconditions:** A task dependency model and explicit authority/feedback semantics.

**Authority preconditions:** Initiative, veto, escalation and final-decision rights must be explicit in operation.

**State and mode preconditions:** Each participant needs state sufficient to coordinate its contribution.

**Timing and attention preconditions:** Coordination must fit the pace and consequences of the work.

**Decision or consumer:** System designer, automation-policy owner and operational team.

**Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:** A fully encapsulated, deterministic service with verified input/output contracts may need only interface and failure semantics rather than rich teaming protocol.

**Disposition at the historical freeze:** JOINT_COGNITIVE_SYSTEM_PROPERTY

**Historical disposition group:** TRANSFERABLE_OR_CROSSWALK_WORTHY

**Failure modes:** Over-coordination can create latency, excessive confirmation and role ambiguity; dynamic roles can become unpredictable.; Over-application can add cognitive load, delay, false authority or bureaucracy when the trigger does not hold.

**Criticism:** Over-coordination can create latency, excessive confirmation and role ambiguity; dynamic roles can become unpredictable.

**Contrary evidence:** In tightly coupled high-speed control, excessive negotiation can be worse than stable pre-authorised roles.

**Unresolved questions:** Which coordination contracts can be verified formally without excluding adaptive work?

**Property versus ceremony:** A RACI chart or automation-level label is not enough; the property is an executable, timed coordination relationship.

**Evidence profile at the historical freeze:** ACCIDENT_OR_INCIDENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVITY: MODERATE; payoff depends on consequence, coupling, timing and implementation quality; CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; CONTRARY_EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: MODERATE; mainly limits universal implementations rather than the underlying property; DOMAIN_PRACTICE_STRENGTH: HIGH_IN_ESTABLISHED_DOMAINS; FIELD_EMPIRICAL_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; HISTORICAL_PROVENANCE_STRENGTH: HIGH; LAB_EXPERIMENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE; OVERALL: STRONG_WITH_CONTEXTUAL_IMPLEMENTATION_LIMITS; RATIONALE: Primary evidence: Historical, theoretical and field evidence strongly supports the failure mechanism; universal superiority of any one coordination framework is unproven. ### ECSE-05 — Dynamic role, function and authority coordination

**Historical origin:** Supervisory control, adaptive automation, mixed initiative and coactive design

**Original form:** Allow roles and initiative to change with state, capability and workload instead of remaining fixed for every condition.

**Development:** From adaptive automation keyed mainly to workload toward coactive, bounded and mutually observable role adaptation.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Use bounded, state-dependent mixed initiative with explicit transition contracts and post-change confirmation—not opaque adaptive reassignment.

**Problem:** Fixed allocation becomes brittle when capabilities, workload, uncertainty or environment change, but unannounced adaptation creates authority surprise.

**Mechanism:** Define allowed role states, triggers, negotiation/consent rules, transition annunciation, fallback and reversion; keep authority changes observable and challengeable.

**Trigger and context:** Variable environments where relative human/automation capability changes materially and the cost of fixed allocation is high.

**Preconditions:** Validated triggers, role-state model, transition feedback and stable fallback.

**Authority preconditions:** Each transfer needs an authorised initiator, acceptance semantics and residual-responsibility rule.

**State and mode preconditions:** Current role, next role, trigger and degraded state must be visible.

**Timing and attention preconditions:** Transitions must complete within the time needed to reacquire state and act.

**Decision or consumer:** Automation-policy owner, operators and governance authority.

**Nontrigger or cheaper adequate path:** Keep roles fixed when they are adequate, when transition risk exceeds benefit, or when authority changes cannot be made legible in time.

**Disposition at the historical freeze:** HUMAN_AUTOMATION_CONTEXT_PROPERTY

**Historical disposition group:** TRANSFERABLE_OR_CROSSWALK_WORTHY

**Failure modes:** Adaptive authority may be driven by noisy workload proxies, increase mode complexity and make accountability unstable.; Over-application can add cognitive load, delay, false authority or bureaucracy when the trigger does not hold.

**Criticism:** Adaptive authority may be driven by noisy workload proxies, increase mode complexity and make accountability unstable.

**Contrary evidence:** Stable allocations often outperform adaptive ones when triggers are unreliable or transition costs dominate.

**Unresolved questions:** Which trigger combinations are robust enough for consequential real-world authority adaptation?

**Property versus ceremony:** Adaptivity is not itself the property; bounded, legible and recoverable role change is.

**Evidence profile at the historical freeze:** ACCIDENT_OR_INCIDENT_STRENGTH: LOW_OR_INDIRECT; ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVITY: HIGH; CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: MODERATE; CONTRARY_EVIDENCE_STRENGTH: MODERATE_TO_HIGH; DOMAIN_PRACTICE_STRENGTH: MIXED; FIELD_EMPIRICAL_STRENGTH: LOW_TO_MODERATE; HISTORICAL_PROVENANCE_STRENGTH: HIGH; LAB_EXPERIMENT_STRENGTH: MODERATE; MEASUREMENT_VALIDITY_STRENGTH: LOW_TO_MODERATE; OVERALL: MIXED_CONTEXT_DEPENDENT; RATIONALE: Primary evidence: Evidence is promising but heterogeneous; many studies are short laboratory or simulation studies. ### ECSE-06 — Residual-task and workload-migration analysis

**Historical origin:** Bainbridge's ironies of automation and later human-automation coordination research

**Original form:** Examine what automation leaves, transforms, concentrates or creates for humans rather than equating machine execution with work removal.

**Development:** From task substitution accounting to joint-work and coordination-debt analysis.

**Evolved form in this historical corpus:** Accept automation only with an explicit residual-work account, peak-load evaluation and named owner for newly created cognitive work.

**Problem:** Automation often removes routine practice while leaving monitoring, exception diagnosis, coordination and rare recovery at the hardest moments.
