## Distinct predicates and within-lineage composition

An event occurrence, its recorded row, an interpreted activity and a chosen case/object unit are different. Reproducible extraction and valid syntax do not guarantee meaningful observations. Discovery depends on model bias and data assumptions. Conformance depends on trace, reference, mapping and cost semantics; minimum cost is not cause, legality or fairness. Fitness, precision, generalisation and simplicity are separate. Prediction, causal identification, feasible action, permission and observed benefit need their respective evidence.

The public composition joins event interpretation, case/object choice, model selection, validation and a real consumer. It permits source correction, participant challenge, descriptive/reference revision and post-intervention reassessment. Richer methods require a consequential distinction. A small query, relation table or narrower conclusion can be sufficient. An affected mapping can be re-examined while unrelated observations remain valid.

The public account emphasises meaningful event identity, traceable extraction, justified case/object units, explicit model/reference semantics, separate evaluation dimensions, valid experimental units, temporal inputs, the causal boundary, reproducibility, privacy and contestability. EPM-084 is a historical analytical handoff proposal whose complete field and evidence limits remain available; this reader does not independently validate a new protocol.

## Counterexamples, alternatives and evidence limits

Flattening shared events can change apparent rework or delay. A chosen low-cost alignment can explain a trace/model discrepancy without identifying its real-world cause. Retrospective prediction can leak future information. Secure computation can still disclose sensitive results in its outputs. These failures retain useful scoped methods while narrowing unsupported general claims.
