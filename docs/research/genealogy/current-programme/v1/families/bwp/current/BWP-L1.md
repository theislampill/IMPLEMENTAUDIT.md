## Genealogy and what the lineage actually contributes

BPM joins several histories. Process redesign and BPR made cross-functional outcomes and the relationship between information technology and work organisation prominent. Industrial engineering and quality management supplied related concerns with flows, measures and redesign. Office-information systems and workflow research contributed technical coordination and enactment. Organisational capability, governance, modelling, flexibility and newer responsible or sustainability-oriented work address further problems. The source genealogy distinguishes documented imports and transmission from shared ancestry, analogy and retrospective reconstruction. It does not tell a story in which one founder invented the whole field or BPM simply replaced an obsolete BPR stage. Its account is organised around three achievements: **warranted understanding; legitimate and feasible action; observed consequences**. None entails the next. ## The substantive predicates

**Purpose and consequence boundary.** EBPM-001 and EBPM-002 require the outcome, relevant beneficiaries and material boundary of the inquiry to be intelligible. EBPM-017 requires measures suited to that outcome. Faster internal handling can coexist with a worse end-to-end result or transferred burdens. The boundary is proportionate: the study does not demand knowledge of every conceivable consequence before any work can proceed. Material exclusions and uncertainty need an accountable treatment. Neither the word “customer” nor a metric resolves contested rights or values. [Ledger EBPM-001, 002, 017, 069.]

**Evidence of actual work.** EBPM-006 connects interviews, documents, observations and event evidence without pretending that they have identical evidential roles. A prescribed process and an account of situated work can both be useful. Informal coordination may explain an apparent deviation; an official record may preserve accountability even when it is incomplete as description. EBPM-037 therefore investigates a workaround’s causes, purpose and risks. A workaround can supply informative evidence while remaining unauthorised or harmful. Its existence neither warrants approval nor warrants automatic suppression. [Ledger EBPM-006–009, 037; source table and source-role limits.]

**Representation and execution.** EBPM-010 selects a representation that serves the actual consumer and decision. EBPM-014 distinguishes a communication or analytical model from the contract actually executed. A readable diagram can be inadequate for enactment; a precise formalism can be unnecessary for a small decision. The current correction to relation R006 makes purpose-fit representation a constraint on the choice of forms, not an alternative notation alongside them. The model preserves the historical erroneous relation in its ancestry. [Native relation R006; report additions.]

**Authority, feasibility and effects.** EBPM-031 asks whether an actor has effective powers, knowledge and resources. A title or a role box alone does not establish them. An intervention also needs a feasible mechanism and observation of the consequences relevant to its purpose. Organisational taxonomies and maturity assessments can support diagnosis but do not supply a universally best governance hierarchy or a causal improvement theorem. [Ledger EBPM-031, 033, 034, 044, 056.]

**Comparison and causal restraint.** EBPM-018 separates elapsed time from work effort. EBPM-022 separates a useful forecast, simulation or association from evidence that an intervention improves the relevant outcome. Case mix, selection, configuration, policy, baseline and exposure can change the interpretation of a comparison. The source’s longitudinal example retains 25 intended processes in ten organisations, seven implemented processes in five organisations, and five raw lead-time improvements versus two deteriorations. These nested populations do not support a universal benefit or failure percentage. [Ledger EBPM-018, 019, 022; source table’s empirical design and dependence records.]
