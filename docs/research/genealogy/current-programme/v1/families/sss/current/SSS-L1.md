## Architecture as consequential structure

The lineage's central object is a realised software system and the consequential relationships through which it works and changes. An architecture description is an account of that object. A design decision, evaluation method and observer's belief are further distinct objects. A faithful description can reveal a poor design; a well-run evaluation can miss a material condition; a valid model result can fail to transfer when its mapping to the realised system is false. Architectural significance therefore follows consequences for the relevant concern, rather than the size of a diagram, a component's name, or an organisational title. [Accepted report, scope and property interpretation](SOURCES.md#s-f1fa0d88a6701b1c).

This distinction gives the lineage a sharper predicate than “document the architecture.” A decision-useful claim names its bearer, concern, configuration and observer; identifies the relationships or assumptions that matter; and states what observation would establish or defeat it. The minimum adequate representation can be a small dependency account, a scenario or a direct inspection. An unused view earns no protection merely by existing. Conversely, the lack of a large architecture document is not evidence that the system lacks architecture. The accepted [68 per-property assessments](SOURCES.md#s-1e8fa529955c86e9) supply distinct applicability, observation, counter-control, cheaper-path, cost and reopening conditions.

A bounded context is not automatically a separately deployable service. The plural history matters because each proposal addresses a particular failure mode; treating every successor as a universally superior stage would erase the conditions under which an older, simpler form remains adequate.

## Decomposition, interfaces and quality claims

Information hiding targets anticipated change and the assumptions a module conceals. It does not select one universally correct partition. A uses relation concerns dependence needed for correctness and is not identical to a call graph. A module can call another without using it in the relevant sense, or depend on shared data, environment or ordering without the decisive relation appearing as a direct call. The meaningful boundary is the one that contains or exposes the actual change and correctness dependency. [Current property ledger, relevant decomposition and dependency records](SOURCES.md#s-ffbd77d9282c5374).

An interface contract can include data meaning and ownership, timing, order, failure, resource and environmental assumptions. A queue's acknowledgement can establish receipt while leaving downstream completion unestablished. A visible service boundary can still share a resource pool whose exhaustion defeats the intended isolation. These cases discriminate realised behavior from a diagram that looks modular. A modular monolith can be the better choice when independent deployment, isolation or organisational autonomy supplies insufficient benefit for the distributed arrangement's communication and operating costs.

A quality scenario identifies a source of stimulus, stimulus, environment, affected artifact, response and response measure. These are aspects needed to interpret the scenario, not a six-document requirement. SAAM and ATAM can reveal scenario-sensitive risks and tradeoffs without providing a precise quantitative prediction of all quality outcomes. CBAM's comparative benefit and cost reasoning depends on the preferences, uncertainties, horizon and cost bearers used; a numerical score does not confer decision authority or turn a violated constraint into an admissible option.

## Formal claims and realised conformance

The accepted Wright account requires the relevant connector assumptions, deadlock freedom and compatible ports for its bounded result. Compatibility alone is insufficient. Even a correct theorem remains about its model until the implementation and environment correspondence is justified. The qualification therefore preserves formal results while rejecting a lift from a local model property to unqualified realised quality or inevitable whole-system progress. [Report, formal analysis](SOURCES.md#s-f1fa0d88a6701b1c), [composition relations](SOURCES.md#s-4ed7c3424cf1e0fd).

Mapped architectural conformance compares actual extracted relations with a legitimate architectural baseline. It answers whether the mapped implementation agrees with that baseline at the inspected scope. It does not establish that the baseline is good, every relevant effect was observed, or the implementation must be changed to restore the baseline. A discrepancy can justify revising the model or baseline when the realised behavior is legitimate. Equally, a true conformance result can coexist with failure of an independent functional, safety or action criterion. That last separation becomes central in SSS-S r16; the lineage's own predicate must survive the synthesis rather than become a generic “passed check.”

## Adaptation, burden and finite discriminators

Dynamic adaptation depends on valid probes, an appropriate model, a policy and effective actions. A live loop can continue running while the observation used by a particular policy is stale or invalid. Changing a quantity's unit from milliseconds to seconds can defeat the policy that consumes that quantity without invalidating an unrelated policy. The appropriate response is selective invalidation or correction of the affected claim, not a universal stop on every architectural observation. Earlier binding, a fixed configuration, manual intervention or a narrower advisory use can be adequate alternatives. [Accepted assessments and report](SOURCES.md#s-1e8fa529955c86e9).

A second discriminator concerns joint burden. Two reviews can each fit a local estimate while their combined demands do not fit the same expert's capacity: two four-hour reviews plus half an hour of coordination exceed a six-hour budget. Local feasibility does not establish joint feasibility. Likewise, separate nonnegative balances can coexist with a failed global transfer-conservation requirement. These are finite constructions exposing an invalid inference, not measurements of general architecture-program effectiveness. They neither mandate a central review board nor prove that distributed design is inherently unworkable. The claim is to count the relevant shared constraint and retain a simpler adequate arrangement where available.

The 68-property ledger retains the original status distribution: nine strongly supported, fifteen evolved, thirteen contextual, eight assumption-sensitive, eight domain-specific, two easily gamed, eight rejected, two ceremonial, one with no general result, one superseded and one unresolved. These labels classify different kinds of claims. A rejected universal can leave a conditional method useful; an unresolved capability claim is not a claim of impossibility. The recurring adverse forms are architecture-by-diagram, prescribed views without a consumer, decomposition chosen by slogan, a formal result detached from correspondence, conformance mistaken for quality, and a local benefit that ignores joint operating burden. The remedies follow the native predicate: inspect the actual dependency, identify the concern, preserve an independently legitimate criterion, narrow the claim, or retain the adequate current structure. A new named practice is not required when direct reasoning or a smaller check supplies the function.
