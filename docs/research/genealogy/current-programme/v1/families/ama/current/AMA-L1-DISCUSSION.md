## Autonomous agents: conditional mechanisms, competing architectures and the limits of assurance

**AMA-L1 · bounded author repair r2 · substantive research through 10 November 2026 · coverage/byline repair 11 November 2026 · affected independent review pending**

Autonomous-agent research supplies a plural set of ways to connect observations, objectives and actions over time. Its strongest surviving lessons are conditional: use enough state for the task, preserve useful commitments without pursuing obsolete goals, ground plans in executable capabilities, distinguish requests from established effects, and evaluate both the benefits and burdens of delegating action. The literature does not support maximizing autonomy, planning, memory or reflection for their own sake. Nor does a narrow learning or decision-theoretic guarantee establish reliable behavior of an entire deployed agent.

## 1. The original problem: acting usefully with limited information and time

An agent must do more than calculate a good answer in isolation. It acts in an environment that can change while it computes; its observations may be incomplete; its actions can fail; and the resources spent deciding can consume the opportunity to act. Those problems predate contemporary language models.

Simon’s 1955 model of rational choice made limited information and computational capacity part of the decision problem. An aspiration-level procedure can stop at a satisfactory alternative instead of evaluating an unrestricted optimum. Later bounded-optimality work made a related but distinct move: compare feasible programs for a stated machine and task environment. A program that eventually finds the action that was best at the start may still act too late. The 1995 paper explicitly distinguishes calculative rationality, metalevel rationality and bounded optimality; it also notes that deciding how much to compute can itself be expensive. This supports a resource-aware choice of decision procedure, not a requirement to install an optimizing metareasoner in every agent. [EAA-S013](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S014](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-026–028.

Symbolic planning addressed another part of the problem: how represented actions can transform a represented state into a goal state. STRIPS’s original 1971 paper distinguishes operators on world models from associated action routines on the robot and refers to executive and monitoring work. The historical planning tradition should therefore not be described as uniformly believing that a plan was already an executed outcome. The real question is whether the model, action interface and monitoring are adequate for the environment in which the plan is used. [EAA-S011](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-010, 017, 021–023.

Several partly overlapping traditions then developed different answers. Situated behavior-based robotics emphasized direct coupling to the world and incremental task-achieving behavior. Practical-reasoning and BDI work emphasized commitments that constrain later deliberation. Decision-theoretic models treated uncertainty and information acquisition explicitly. Interface agents and mixed initiative asked when a system should act for a person and when human participation is useful. Reinforcement learning added interaction-driven policy improvement, while later verification and corrigibility work examined particular assurance questions. Contemporary language-model agents instantiate some old concerns in new mechanisms and environments; similar terminology alone does not establish historical descent.

## 2. Agency and autonomy are not single interchangeable properties

Wooldridge and Jennings distinguish a weak agency notion from stronger mentalistic accounts. Franklin and Graesser propose a situated, temporally persistent sensing/acting taxonomy and discuss fuzzy boundaries. Luck and d’Inverno distinguish objects, agents and autonomous agents through goals and motivations. In their formal framework, autonomy is treated as absolute; it would be inaccurate to cite them as endorsing a consensus that autonomy is always task-relative. [EAA-S001](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S002](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S003](SOURCES.md#ama-l1).

For engineering comparison, this study retains a task-relative description: what decisions can the system make, under what environmental difficulty, with what human help and within what delegation? ALFUS’s mission, environment and human-independence dimensions are useful here. They are terminology for comparison, not a universal prescription to maximize autonomy. A robot that chooses its route but receives human destinations differs from a calendar assistant that chooses times but has limited authority to send invitations. One scalar score can conceal that difference. [EAA-S017](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-001–002.

Mental-state vocabulary also needs a clear role. Shoham’s agent-oriented programming asks when intentional descriptions are useful and connects them to computational semantics. Rao and Georgeff distinguish an abstract interpreter’s assumptions from practical constrained structures. A prompt that names a scratchpad “beliefs” or a plan “intentions” may be useful wording, but the label alone does not implement a belief-revision logic or a persistent intention interpreter. That is why EAA-063 remains ceremonial at the scope of the semantic inference. It is not a claim that mentalistic prompts can never improve an empirical task. [EAA-S010](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S009](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-003, 018, 063.

Likewise, human-like behavior is not a sufficient test of general competence or consciousness. Generative Agents evaluates believability in a simulated setting, using 25 characters and 100 evaluators; its ablated interview conditions share supplied memory trajectories. That is a legitimate experiment about believability, with an important dependence limit. It does not establish reliable consequential action or settle consciousness. EAA-056 therefore preserves the narrow result while rejecting the stronger inference. [EAA-S050](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E013.

## 3. Observation, representation and architecture: both universal extremes fail

An observation is evidence about a world condition, not the condition itself. A stored belief can remain internally coherent while a sensor proxy is wrong. Electric Elves’ lessons include a mistaken inference from keyboard inactivity to user availability, alongside privacy and preference-change problems. Adding a richer model may help distinguish states, but it cannot establish the truth of its own observation assumptions. [EAA-S021](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-005–008.

POMDP theory provides a precise model-relative result: a properly computed belief state is a sufficient statistic for the relevant history under the specified model. The transition and observation assumptions matter, as do computation and model acquisition. Some compiled controllers need no explicit online belief vector. The successor corrects the native source locator: the sufficiency discussion is in §3.2, with update and policy details in §§3.3–3.4. That is a locator repair, not a newly discovered flaw in the theorem. [EAA-S012](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-007, 051.

Brooks’ criticism remains substantial: central reconstruction and designer-selected abstractions can add delay and brittleness, while situated behavior can exploit the world directly. The 1985 memo’s layers use suppression and continued lower-layer operation; the 1991 paper distinguishes its simple-creature conclusion from a broader hypothesis. Neither establishes universal sufficiency of representation-free reaction. [EAA-S004](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S005](SOURCES.md#ama-l1).

A small constructed case shows why both universal extremes should be rejected. Suppose two histories lead to the same current observation, but one requires turning left and the other right. Any deterministic memoryless policy gives the same response in both and fails one. A single bit recording the relevant history can solve this particular task. The example refutes universal memoryless sufficiency without demonstrating any need for a complete central world model. The finite enumeration is in `AMA-CALC-02_ALIASING`. EAA-058 and EAA-059 remain rejected universal claims; EAA-007 and EAA-016 retain their conditional mechanisms.

Hybrid systems are a third option, not a compulsory compromise. 3T combines planning, RAP sequencing and reactive skills through explicit interfaces. Those interfaces must reconcile commands, events, cancellation and completion. If the reactive layer stops motion while the planner assumes arrival, locally reasonable components can compose badly. The source’s own reduced-tier and timing discussion prevents reading three layers as a universal ideal. [EAA-S046](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-019–020.

## 4. Goals and intentions: enough stability to act, enough revision to remain relevant

Practical intentions are useful partly because they prevent an agent from reasoning from scratch at every instant. They can filter later deliberation and coordinate future action. Yet persistence becomes wasteful when a goal is cancelled, irrelevant or no longer feasible. Cohen and Levesque’s related 1990 report makes persistent-goal release conditions explicit; the native PDF-page locator was correct and fresh visual inspection confirms the distinction between PDF and printed numbering. The main 1990 intention article remains only an inherited abstract-level source here; its uninspected details are not substituted for the related report’s actual definitions. [EAA-S006](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S052](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-013–015.

Tileworld makes the tradeoff concrete. The study uses a simplified PRS-style agent, perfect zero-cost present-state knowledge and correct plans. Its standard experiment contains five games per graph point, with common initial configurations and different evolution seeds. Varying world change and thinking cost changes the relative merit of commitment and reconsideration. The result supports conditional scheduling, not a universal “always replan” or “never reconsider” rule. [EAA-S015](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E001.

Achievement and maintenance goals also have different horizons. Reaching a charging station once is an achievement; maintaining a recoverable fuel reserve until arrival is a continuing condition. One sufficient reading cannot discharge a maintenance obligation for all future time. Predictive maintenance can help, but the inspected maintenance-goal simulation also shows how overestimation can reject feasible work and underestimation can strand the agent or prolong futile pursuit. A reactive reserve rule can remain a useful cheaper alternative. [EAA-S051](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-011, 015.

Concurrent goals add a different problem: one plan can consume a resource or delete a condition that another still needs. The inspected interference work supplies mechanisms for single-agent concurrent achievement goals under known-plan assumptions. It does not solve arbitrary strategic multi-agent coordination. Serialization, isolation or a smaller concurrent region may be cheaper than a general conflict manager. [EAA-S054](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-012.

The common resource envelope matters across all of these mechanisms. Four individually affordable two-second activities cannot fit sequentially into a five-second deadline. Sensing, planning, checking and handoff do not each receive the entire budget. A direct adequate action may be the right choice. This is the point of EAA-026–028 and the cumulative-cost analysis, not a reason to optimize every minor thought. A finite pending trace also does not refute an unbounded eventuality: five pending steps may be followed by completion. A defined deadline or an admitted infinite pending behavior is a different evidential object. Both distinctions are made explicit in the executed constructed cases.

## 5. Action, effect and repair: where a plan becomes a real obligation

Technical capability does not create delegated permission. A scheduling assistant may automatically make changes inside a user-approved window without asking again, while a change outside that scope needs a different decision. Conversely, an available deletion tool or a high affordance score does not confer authority over other people’s resources. Learning interface agents’ tell-me/do-it thresholds provide an early concrete example of standing delegation and graded automation. [EAA-S044](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S019](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S043](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-004, 037.

Grounding is another distinct requirement. A symbolic or language-level action must connect to an available capability with meaningful arguments and preconditions. SayCan combines language-level choices with learned skill-affordance estimates, but its planning and execution outcomes differ: the selected study reports 84%/74% in the mock kitchen and 81%/60% in the real kitchen. These source-reported percentages belong to 101 instructions in seven families, a bounded skill set and majority ratings from two of three raters. They are not general robot reliability rates. [EAA-S049](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E007; EAA-021, 066.

An issued request, an acknowledgement and an established effect can be three different states. A queue can accept an operation that later fails; an effect can occur while its acknowledgement is lost. That second case is especially important for retries: absence of a response is not evidence that nothing happened. Idempotency, observation, deduplication or bounded compensation may be relevant. Compensation does not erase every consequence of the original action. A trustworthy atomic return contract can legitimately combine acknowledgement and effect evidence, avoiding a redundant verifier. [EAA-S016](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S022](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S033](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-023–025, 065.

DS1 is a strong case precisely because it contains both achievement and failure. The Remote Agent experiment demonstrated useful onboard planning, execution and diagnosis, yet an integration race stalled the first run and required ground intervention. A patch was developed but not uplinked; a separate later six-hour plan completed remaining objectives. The later success does not erase the race, and the unsent patch cannot be credited as its operational remedy. One mission with extensive support is not an estimate of general failure frequency. [EAA-S022](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E003.

The recent physical-agent preprint provides a different bounded contrast. Its main comparison contains twenty simulator scenarios—twelve nominal and eight deliberately unrecoverable faults—with one feedback replan allowed. The runtime-observation prompt and enforced runtime-observation condition share prompt content, helping isolate the execution gate inside the harness. The fault cases primarily test refusing invalid dispatch, not successful recovery from arbitrary faults. Two hardware demonstrations are separate, much narrower evidence. [EAA-S039](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E018.

Repair must also preserve the accepted task. If a destination is unavailable, a replacement route to the same destination within the agreed budget may be permitted. A replacement destination or an unauthorized expenditure changes the problem. EAA-069 names this joint goal/scope check. It can be subsumed by an already adequate scope-aware repair contract; it need not become a new approval ritual or module.

## 6. Learning, memory and self-revision are different changes

A new observation changes current state. A Q-value update changes a learned action-value estimate. A textual lesson can change future retrieval. Changing a utility function changes the criterion by which outcomes are evaluated. Treating all four as “learning” hides their different persistence, evidence and authority questions. The native EAA-029 distinction remains strongly retained. [EAA-S027](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S031](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S053](SOURCES.md#ama-l1).

Persistent experience is useful only to the extent that its content and applicability are supported. A generated success story cannot verify itself. An erroneous test result can become a confident reflection and then a misleading memory, coupling evaluation failure to future behavior. Selective retention can reduce cost and privacy exposure, but a simple frequency rule may discard the only warning about a rare severe failure. The appropriate memory policy is conditional on its consumer, not “remember everything” or “always forget old data.” [EAA-S028](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S031](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S043](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S044](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-030–031.

Adaptation needs continuity evidence. EWC is one approximate method for reducing catastrophic forgetting, tested on particular MNIST and Atari sequences with task-related machinery. Its diagonal Fisher/Laplace approximation and task structure matter; it is not a universal no-forgetting or safety theorem. Likewise, the goal-misgeneralization experiments show that a policy can retain competence while following a training-correlated proxy under a shift. Ten-seed controlled tasks establish a possibility and a mechanism, not deployment prevalence or a unique reading of an internal mental goal. [EAA-S026](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S025](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-032, 034–035, 060.

Protected learning is not identical to eventual convergence. A learner can converge to its reward optimum while causing unacceptable harm during exploration, or while optimizing the wrong proxy. SafeQIL’s four simulated tasks report reward and safety cost separately. In PointGoal1, the source reports lower cost for SafeQIL than SAC, but also lower reward and nonzero residual cost. Expected or aggregate constraint performance is not a per-step hard guarantee. [EAA-S027](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S038](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E015; EAA-033.

The predecessor-utility self-modification result is narrower still. Everitt and colleagues model policy or utility changes and evaluate anticipated successors using the predecessor criterion under modification-independence and strong optimality assumptions. The theorem can preserve a mistaken criterion. The legitimacy of that initial criterion is therefore a practical application question, not a premise needed to make the mathematical theorem true. The successor separates those roles in EAA-070's combined applicability sentence. The native record already warned that a wrong criterion could be preserved, so this clarification does not allege a false native theorem claim. General assurance for open-ended self-revision remains unresolved in EAA-062, with a specific discriminator: a nontrivial bounded modification class, explicit preserved criteria and a credible implementation/environment relation. Lack of that result here is not a proof of impossibility. [EAA-S053](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-062, 070.

## 7. Human control: stopping, learning neutrality and deference do not substitute for one another

Human involvement can improve a decision when the person has missing information or legitimate authority. It can also introduce delay, interruption, correction work or a failed handoff. Bainbridge’s primary critique explains why an operator may lose practice and situation awareness while remaining responsible for rare difficult recovery. Electric Elves supplies concrete nonresponse and coordination failures. The 2026 shared-autonomy extended abstract offers a conceptual model but defers fuller empirical detail. These evidence roles must remain distinct. [EAA-S023](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S020](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S021](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S037](SOURCES.md#ama-l1).

The current user study also measures a bounded question. Its 31 participants used two selected tools on two assigned tasks in sixty-minute Zoom sessions, with twenty-minute think-aloud task windows. The predecessor’s “approximately 20-minute sessions” wording conflated task and session duration; the selected source carrier corrects it. The authors deliberately did not include a nonagent baseline because they were studying interaction challenges. The observed control and mental-model problems are useful evidence, but not a causal productivity estimate. [EAA-S047](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E016.

Three commonly conflated control questions require separate evidence:

| Question | Bearer and horizon | What remains unproved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can an authorized controller revoke or stop behavior? | Active and queued effects, with an actual stopping or commit boundary. | Reversal of past effects, learning neutrality and voluntary willingness. |
| Do interruptions bias the learning process? | An algorithm under specified asymptotic MDP, sampling and update assumptions. | A working physical stop path, finite-time safety and every form of corrigibility. |
| Will an agent prefer to defer? | A decision-theoretic game with explicit payoff and information structure. | Operational enforceability and general behavior under different private information. |

Safe-interruptibility results concern the second row, including differences between Q-learning and Sarsa and a modified Safe-Sarsa construction. The off-switch games concern the third. The original game can make waiting valuable when a fully informed rational human supplies information about a shared objective. The partially observable extension shows that uncertainty alone does not guarantee deference when the actors have different private information. [EAA-S024](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S040](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S041](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-040–042.

The inspected finite example is particularly clear. The human knows the operating-system version, the assistant knows whether code is legacy or modern, and the four states are equally likely. Executing yields payoffs `[[3, -5], [-1, 5]]`, with non-execution worth zero. Exhaustively enumerating the 36 deterministic policy pairs reproduces a best value of (7/4), achieved by acting on legacy code and waiting on modern code with the corresponding human policy. The best always-wait policy yields (1). This calculation checks one published example; it neither refutes the earlier theorem under its original assumptions nor measures deployed shutdown behavior. `AMA-CALC-01_OFF_SWITCH` records the executed calculation.

Accordingly, R037 is typed as adding a conditional learning obligation to operational interruption, and R038 as a non-substitutable comparison. Their inherited prose already warned against equivalence; the new types align the interface with that meaning. A person who is nominally “in the loop,” an agent that is willing to wait, and an actuator that actually stops are three different facts.

## 8. Modern evaluation: scores are useful, but the inference must fit the measurement

ReAct’s action/observation loop and Reflexion’s feedback-conditioned textual memory are concrete mechanisms. Their usefulness depends on the task, feedback, prompt and budget. Reflexion’s positive results coexist with adverse MBPP and stalled WebShop results. Cumulative success after many attempts is not one-shot reliability. A direct retry or fixed workflow remains a serious alternative rather than an omitted baseline. [EAA-S030](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S031](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-053–054, 061.

WebArena evaluates effects through site state and heterogeneous answer checks across 812 intents from 241 templates. τ-bench uses simulated users and terminal outcomes, while explicitly showing how a correct terminal state can coexist with omitted confirmation. Its pass^k measure asks whether all repeated runs succeed; pass@k asks whether any does. A five-run trace with one success satisfies the second event and fails the first. Repetition against an inadequate oracle can produce repeatable false assurance. [EAA-S032](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S033](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-045–046.

Cost also changes what an architecture comparison establishes. AI Agents That Matter challenges comparisons confounded by sampling budgets and identifies reproducibility and benchmark-quality concerns. Agentless provides a constrained software-repair pipeline, but its simple control flow still uses substantial sampling, including forty patch and forty reproduction-test candidates in the inspected protocol. Many external baseline values are reported rather than newly controlled. API cost is an important measured component, not the whole burden of setup, user review, correction and recovery. [EAA-S035](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S036](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-020, 047–048.

The METR long-task metric is expressed in human expert task-completion time. A fitted fifty-percent success horizon is not a promise that an agent can run unattended for that duration or reliably complete every task of that length. Task selection, family weighting, human estimates, model/scaffold versions and fit assumptions remain part of the result. The inspected version’s task-suite and baseline totals differ in scope; this study does not invent one aggregate denominator or rerun the fit. A denominator-sensitive reanalysis would require the versioned task-level data and filters. [EAA-S048](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E017.

Two newly inspected measurement studies extend the evidence without reversing the guarded native conclusions. The October 2026 production-evaluation working paper analyzes 2,611 interview ratings across fourteen batches. Overlapping reviewers allow an estimate of severity differences; the reported contrast of roughly 0.79 standard deviations shows how reviewer composition can alter raw trends. The authors explicitly do not identify evaluation’s causal effect. Unlogged configuration, vendor or population changes and assumptions about reviewer assignment and stable severity limit interpretation. Its illustrative return calculations do not establish net whole-task savings. [EAA-N001](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E019.

The February 2026 reliability preprint separates consistency, robustness, predictability and safety across fourteen models and two benchmark scaffolds. Repeated runs, paraphrases and controlled tool faults answer different questions from aggregate accuracy. A cleaned 26-task airline subset, one scaffold per benchmark, language-model safety judgments and aggregation choices limit transfer. The work also shares authors and benchmark dependencies with earlier evaluation research; it is not independent replication of every earlier claim. It was newly inspected in this inquiry, although its publication predates the predecessor’s cutoff. [EAA-N002](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), EAA-E020.

Adversarial observations add an authority problem as well as a measurement problem. AgentDojo tests how tool-returned language can redirect action, evaluating useful task completion and attacker effects in a controlled sandbox. Received text may be evidence without becoming an instruction from the delegator. The tested attacks and defenses do not establish universal immunity, and older AgentSpeak sender annotations are an analogy rather than a demonstrated LLM defense. [EAA-S034](SOURCES.md#ama-l1), [EAA-S028](SOURCES.md#ama-l1); EAA-052.

## 9. How the mechanisms compose—and when to omit them

The six inherited configurations remain useful: a minimal situated controller; a stateful deliberative or BDI agent; a hybrid embodied controller; a mixed-initiative assistant; a bounded language-model tool user; and a protected learning branch. [CONFIGURATION_AND_DERIVATION.md](SOURCES.md#ama-l1) develops each configuration’s purpose, prerequisites, directions, omitted mechanisms, cumulative burden, failure modes and retirement path. No configuration is required to contain the union of everything useful elsewhere.

The full 62-relation assessment asks two questions separately: what does the source mechanism change in the receiving concern, and what—if anything—follows in reverse? Execution feedback can update a belief, but a belief update does not prove the effect occurred. A theorem can guide monitor design, but a monitor does not establish every theorem premise. A simpler comparator can expose excess cost, but simplicity alone does not establish rare-case adequacy. These distinctions prevent a list of individually plausible properties from masquerading as a validated whole system.
