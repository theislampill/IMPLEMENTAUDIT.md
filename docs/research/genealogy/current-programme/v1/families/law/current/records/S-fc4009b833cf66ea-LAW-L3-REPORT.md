## 4. History without the caricature or its inverse

### Earlier production discipline

Benington’s original account addresses integrated programs, scarce machine time, specifications, interfaces and successive testing. Its staged production depiction is real. Its testing discussion also allows specification correction and parallel preparation of tests. The 1983 foreword separately reports substantial prior experimental work and criticizes overly large implementation leaps. The foreword’s counterfactual savings estimate is recollection, not a controlled outcome. Neither layer supports reducing the history to “everything was rigid” or “the early authors were already modern Agile practitioners.” [S001]

The older study’s use of NATO discussion remains inherited and bounded: it is relevant context for software-engineering concerns, but a fresh full-text route failed. No direct Benington-to-Royce transmission is promoted merely because their mechanisms resemble one another. The genealogy distinguishes historical association from documentary influence.

### Royce and the naming hinge

Royce discusses a staged large-project process and the difficulty of late discovery, then adds feedback, preliminary design, pilot work, documentation, test discipline and customer involvement. The strong documentary discipline is part of the account and should not be erased when correcting the one-pass myth. Bell and Thayer explicitly cite Royce in their waterfall discussion; this supplies a precise naming/transmission hinge. It does not establish the entire later route by which diagrams, textbooks, contracts and organizational handoffs emphasized sequentiality. [S003–S004]

The successor retains real adverse experience of staged organizations. The Ericsson study is not dismissed as “not really Waterfall.” Its specific process and setting are evidence. At the same time, a single industrial case cannot rank every possible lifecycle or turn each observed problem into an inevitable consequence of any planning. Its findings include inadequate baselining as well as delayed feedback, which matters when choosing remedies. [S016]

### Risk-driven alternatives and plural V traditions

Boehm’s spiral model uses the dominant risks to choose the next work and commitment. It discusses failure modes of document-driven, evolutionary and other approaches. Its conditional possibilities include a waterfall-like development pattern when relevant requirements, user-interface and integration risks are low but schedule and budget risks matter. Thus a correct negative claim rejects universal one-pass necessity while retaining a well-guarded staged option. The paper’s example is useful mechanism evidence, not a causal proof of universal spiral superiority. [S008]

Three V traditions remain separate: software V/V charts that relate definition products to evaluation; the systems-engineering Vee and its incremental/evolutionary treatment; and the German V-Modell framework of products, roles, decisions and tailoring. The 1995 Vee record was newly verified at abstract/metadata level only. XT Bund 2.4’s inspected sections expressly allow iterations, parallelization and tailored execution strategies while retaining core responsibilities and workflow constraints. They are not permission to remove every required artifact. Shared geometry supplies an analogy, not sufficient evidence of common invention or direct descent. [S005, S010–S013]

## 6. Criticism, empirical scope and continued uncertainty

### Traceability

The freshly inspected 2012 experiment used 52 students, eight selected tasks on two Java systems, and supplied high-quality relevant links. Participants described changes rather than implementing them. Aggregate time and correctness improved, but correctness varied by task, including a task with a lower fully-correct result under traceability. Its setup-cost illustration explicitly excluded link maintenance. The 315 task observations are not 315 independent people. These details support P017’s selective, maintained, decision-useful trace rather than a universal exhaustive matrix. [S041]

The 2015 journal abstract reports a larger subject count, but the exact sample relationship was not resolved through the attempted full-text routes. It is conservatively grouped with the same research programme. The successor asserts neither proven duplicate data nor independently replicated benefits. Inherited observational studies and reviews remain useful bounded evidence, not newly replicated results. The open question is full lifetime benefit across contexts, not whether the experiment existed. [S042–S044]

### Independent V&V

The NIST report discusses several organizational placements for V&V and compares heterogeneous favorable and unfavorable studies. It identifies costs from interfaces, extra reporting, training and erroneous findings. Technical independence can counter shared assumptions and incentives, but a separate organization without competence or access is insufficient. Modern causal economic thresholds remain unresolved. A peer or embedded challenge can be legitimate where the risks and applicable obligations permit; an externally mandated independent assessment cannot be discarded solely because a cheaper arrangement is preferred. [S020, S045]

### Late change and reversibility

The Menzies study analyzes 171 TSP development projects after exclusions, using structured phase and effort records. Its validity discussion limits generalization, acknowledges recording and selection issues, and excludes post-delivery deployment costs. It challenges a universal exponential delayed-issue effect under those measures. It does not show that delaying safety, architecture or operational problems is harmless, nor that TSP caused the absence of the pattern. [S048]

The predecessor’s four costs remain useful analytical distinctions: editing, propagation, commitment unwinding, and failure/recovery. A code edit can be cheap while an installed asset, lost record, contractual commitment or accumulated exposure is expensive to undo. R0–R4 are authored heuristic profiles, not an empirically calibrated scale. The original P024 and P033 already included legal minima; the successor brings that constraint into the top-level summary rather than falsely claiming that it was absent from the original. [P024, P028, P033, P038; S030, S048]

### Safety cases, gates and automation

MASSC records criticism of document-heavy safety cases with inadequate operational engagement, then emphasizes a coherent argument tied to context and evidence. The response is documented; its successful implementation is not thereby established. Similarly, a real gate needs the power to stop or redirect, and a continuous-assurance process needs trustworthy observations and accountable consumption of results. The DoD cATO guide retains an existing ATO and continuous risk-management prerequisites. A green pipeline is not self-issued operating authority. [S033, S036]

The inherited forecasting, escalation-of-commitment and acquisition critiques remain in the full carrier. They are not erased as unfortunate implementation accidents. Their strong lesson is to challenge false precision, sunk-cost defense and milestone gaming while preserving the usefulness of coordination and forecast updates. No universally superior estimation method is selected. A combined package of documentation, models, traceability, independent review, safety case and pipeline may duplicate evidence or introduce shared failure and excessive delay. The total package must therefore be assessed, subject to applicable obligations.

These cases were reasoned through, not executed as software experiments or operational trials. They can expose a false universal inference—for example, code rollback does not necessarily reverse data loss—but do not estimate population effects.
