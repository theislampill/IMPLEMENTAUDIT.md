# Evolved Workflow Management

## A criticism-tested, independent tradition study

**Analytical label:** `EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT`  
**Frozen revision:** `EWM-R1-2026-09-06`  
**Research and access cut-off:** 6 September 2026  
**Population:** 77 candidates, all examined; one primary disposition per candidate.  
**Scope:** Workflow Management, independently of any particular host system or sibling synthesis.

“Evolved Workflow Management” is the name of the analytical composition developed here. It is not a claim that an academic school already exists under that compound name. The established subject is the representation, coordination and enactment of work through control, data, resource and interaction semantics. Its objects include human work and external applications, not only computer jobs. A workflow can contain loops, concurrent branches, cancellation, discretionary choices and changing cases. Restricting the entire tradition to a serial pipeline or a directed acyclic graph would exclude central mechanisms actually investigated in its literature. [EWM-S001–S002; EWM-S006; EWM-S013; EWM-S051]

### Principal judgement

The strongest surviving core is **explicit, bounded coordination with accountable interpretation of its consequences**. A usable system must distinguish the definition from the instance, determine what work is admissible under its selected semantics, connect data and resource conditions to that decision, communicate work and commitments to actual participants, and interpret the evidence by which it advances or closes a case. Failure, change and incomplete observation are part of that account rather than peripheral exceptions to an idealised pipeline. This is this report’s synthesis, supported by the mechanisms and contrasts below; it is not presented as the wording or consensus of any single source.

Three separations organise the composition. **Being enabled is not being authorised or able to act. Recording completion is not achieving the intended effect. Preserving execution history is not preserving the current validity of every assumption on which execution depended.** The appropriate response is not universally more machinery. It is a configuration whose actual consumer can use its distinctions, whose actor has the relevant authority and means, and whose evidence justifies the consequence being claimed. A short manual procedure can meet those conditions for a stable task. A long-lived inter-organisational workflow may need substantially richer state, recovery and interaction mechanisms.

The study retains 67 candidates in positive but differently qualified categories: 14 strongly retained, 19 retained in evolved form, 21 context-dependent, nine assumption-sensitive, three domain-specific and one useful but easily gamed. Of the remaining ten, four are rejected or disfavoured, one is ceremony rather than a general property, one is superseded by a stronger form, three do not establish a general property, and one is unresolved. “Strongly retained” identifies a robust distinction or bounded failure-control rationale, not a universal mandate to buy an engine or a claim of experimentally demonstrated benefit in all organisations.

## 1. The field’s plural problem situations

### Office work, application integration and practical coordination

One starting problem was how documents, information, people and applications move through office work. The WfMC reference model later gathered a heterogeneous collection of technologies and interfaces into a shared account of definitions and enactment. It should therefore be read as a consolidation and architectural reference, not the invention of all coordinated work. The original office-information and practical-action references were identified, but some original texts remained inaccessible; the report does not fabricate a detailed reconstruction of their methods from later citations. [EWM-S001–S003; EWM-S010–S012]

A different starting problem was that a formal route often leaves out the work required to make the route usable. Freeflow treats representation and action as things that need mediation; Bardram’s activity-theoretic study does not simply abolish plans, but asks how plans function within actual activity. These criticisms matter to the synthesis: participation, discretion and an observation boundary are retained, and rigid execution is not the default for every setting. They do not establish that all procedures are oppressive, all automation fails or no invariant can legitimately constrain human work. [EWM-S009; EWM-S034]

The language-action branch adds another distinction. A request, an undertaking to perform work, a declaration that it has been performed and acceptance of the result are not interchangeable events. ActionWorkflow’s material, information and business perspectives make it possible to ask what changes in an object, what message was exchanged and what commitment a participant undertook. This supports EWM-077 selectively: where these transitions have different consequences, collapsing them into one status hides coordination. It does not justify imposing four separate forms or meetings on every minor act. [EWM-S050, printed pp.281–282 and p.7]

These branches are not forced into one founding narrative. A reference model, a theory of situated work and an account of commitments answer related but different questions. Their composition is useful only when the resulting distinctions survive in real decisions, not merely in terminology.

### Formal control, patterns and process-aware execution

Workflow-net research addresses another concrete failure: an apparently plausible model can deadlock, finish with residual work or contain tasks that can never execute. Patterns address a different but connected problem: similar words in different languages and products may denote different coordination behaviour. YAWL is an explicit attempt to provide semantics and expressive mechanisms responsive to such concerns. The later data, resource and exception pattern work shows why control flow is not a complete model of enactment. [EWM-S004–S008; EWM-S013–S016]

The revised control-flow report has 43 patterns: the original twenty are revisited and twenty-three are added. This is a historically specific catalogue, not a universal property quota. Its distinctions help locate assumptions about multiple instances, cancellation and joins. They also expose why a product score based only on the number of supported patterns can be misleading: the chosen workflow may need only a few, and correctly implementing a relevant construct matters more than advertising many irrelevant ones. [EWM-S051, §§3.1–3.2; EWM-S023]

The strongest criticism of the pattern programme is not dismissed as resistance to formalisation. Questions about adequacy of specification, contexts, completeness and evaluative criteria affect what conclusions can be drawn from the catalogue. The original Börger article was available only at abstract/metadata level; the direct rejoinder and proponents’ response were inspected. Consequently, the technical dispute is not declared settled. What is independently supportable is narrower: patterns are useful discriminating vocabulary, but neither popularity nor catalogue coverage is a sufficient correctness or selection argument. [EWM-S023–S024; EWM-S032]

### Alternative forms, not one ladder of progress

Data-centric artifacts, case handling, adaptive change, worklets and declarative workflows answer different locations of uncertainty. Case handling centres relevant case information and authorised discretion. ADEPTflex concerns admissible transformations of live workflow state as well as structure. Worklets provide contextual selection of bounded subprocesses. Declarative approaches constrain the space of permitted behaviour rather than always fixing its sequence. The flexibility classification distinguishes design alternatives, deviation, underspecification and change rather than treating all of these as one technique. [EWM-S021–S022; EWM-S035–S039]

A mature response can therefore be narrower or cheaper. A well-understood recurring exception may become a small handler rather than a general adaptation service. A stable process with a few genuine constraints may use a structured route. A knowledge-intensive case may need a case view rather than a forced route. An existing instance may safely finish under an old version instead of being migrated. Those choices are alternatives with different assumptions; describing all of them does not authorise their simultaneous compulsory adoption.

## 2. What enactment must distinguish

For explanatory purposes, let a versioned definition be `D_v`. An instance `I` carries enough information about the case, governing version, current control/data state, work items and interactions to interpret its next transitions. A work item belongs to an enactment; a message attempt and an external business object may have independent lifetimes. An execution history is a set or sequence of recorded observations, not the instance, definition or external object itself. This tuple is an analytical interface, not a mandated storage schema. The object distinctions are grounded in the reference model, correlation specifications and implementation documentation. [EWM-S002; EWM-S018; EWM-S026]

The essential relation is not necessarily “take the next node”. It is closer to: given the current state and event, which actions are admissible, what is consumed or changed by an action, and under which conditions does a completion claim become justified? Several actions can be enabled together; none may be currently feasible; a later event can interrupt them; and an actor may be entitled to choose among permitted alternatives. A picture’s intended meaning, a formal definition of that meaning and the engine’s implemented behaviour are separate objects of assessment.

This distinction has practical consequences. A modeller can specify the intended join correctly while an engine implements an incomplete subset. An engine can implement the join correctly while a data binding refers to the wrong case. Every local transition can be valid while the external application changes the wrong business object. Conversely, a small non-engine procedure can be perfectly adequate because the distinctions that would otherwise require infrastructure are directly controlled by one performer and one stable record.

The contemporary lesson is not that standardisation failed. A standard can specify an interface or semantic obligation extremely usefully without certifying every implementation or integration. Historical engine tests found incomplete support and regressions across versions. That finding supports checking correspondence in the actual semantic subset used; it does not supply a present-day ranking or imply that every tested defect is equally consequential in every application. [EWM-S017–S019; EWM-S033]

## 3. Control flow, soundness and the quantifiers that matter

### Concurrency and convergence are semantic decisions

A parallel split activates work whose completion must later be accounted for. The required membership of a join can depend on which branches were activated, which iteration or invocation they belong to, whether additional branches can still arrive and what cancellation has changed. A simple AND-join and a general inclusive join do not ask the same question. The latter may require knowledge beyond its immediate incoming arcs. A single global topological order is therefore not a faithful universal model. [EWM-S006; EWM-S013; EWM-S017; EWM-S051]

Multiple-instance work adds a creation and completion policy: a known fixed population, dynamically created instances, or a threshold can require different accounting. Deferred choice adds a race among observations rather than a decision made entirely at an earlier split. Interleaving can permit several activities without permitting simultaneous access to a shared critical resource. Cancellation and termination introduce scope: ending one activity, region, subworkflow or case need not stop an external participant. These are different triggers and failure modes, so the ledger does not merge them under a slogan such as “support concurrency”.

A join with a cancelled branch is an especially useful probe. Continuing as though the branch succeeded can be wrong; waiting forever for a token that cancellation removed can also be wrong. The correction is not one universal alternative join. The correction is a coherent policy across activation, cancellation and convergence, tested in the selected semantics. If a case is allowed to close after a failed or withdrawn branch, its completion claim must state what happened to the protected obligation.

### Classical soundness is strong within a deliberately narrow claim

For the ordinary workflow-net setting with initial marking `[i]` and final marking `[o]`, the classical conditions include:

- For every reachable marking `M`, completion is still possible: `[i] →* M` implies `M →* [o]`.
- Completion is proper: a reachable marking containing the final token does not also retain unrelated work under the definition’s conditions.
- No task is dead: each transition can occur in some possible execution from the initial marking.

These are model predicates, not slogans. The first contains an existential continuation after each reachable state. It does **not** assert that every possible execution actually chooses a terminating continuation. The source literature also distinguishes different soundness variants and model classes rather than treating all completion properties as equivalent. Reduction, short-circuit and compositional results require their own assumptions. [EWM-S004–S005; EWM-S007]

The report’s analytical recursion probe makes this quantifier distinction concrete. Suppose another recursive call and a base-case exit are available at each level. An execution can keep selecting recursion. The availability of the exit is not by itself proof of inevitable termination. The revised patterns report contains stronger eventual-completion prose associated with this idea; this study narrows that prose instead of silently adopting it. A suitable progress assumption or well-founded decrease may establish a stronger result, but it must actually be stated and applicable. This counterexample is this researcher’s analysis, not an empirical engine incident. [EWM-S051, WCP-22, printed p.44; compare EWM-S007]

Adding cancellation or richer joins changes the formal problem. Undecidability for an unrestricted extended-net class is not a demonstration that every bounded workflow engine is unverifiable. Conversely, a result for ordinary nets cannot simply be carried into every reset, data, resource or OR-join extension. The composed system therefore treats verification as evidence for a named predicate, then separately asks what connects the model to the implementation and the environment. [EWM-S008; EWM-S013]

### A sound net can still fail to deliver

A sound resource-free net can wait indefinitely when its only authorised performer is absent. A satisfiable assignment model can lack a feasible timetable. A completed execution can produce an incorrect result because the work was badly specified or the external action failed. None of those observations automatically refutes the formal theorem: they reveal a mismatch between the proved claim and the practical claim being made.

The same discipline prevents an opposite error. Formal analysis is not worthless merely because it is bounded. Deadlock or residual-work defects inside its model may be both consequential and tractable to detect. The decision is whether its proved distinction is consumed by someone capable of correcting the relevant failure, and whether the unmodelled assumptions are separately exposed. The workflow satisfiability paper illustrates such a bound clearly: the assignment problem has a particular constraint formulation, and time ordering lies outside the studied problem. [EWM-S040]

## 4. Data, human work and the actual consumer

Control predicates depend on data scope, input/output bindings and the meaning of missing information. Shared data can also change while work is in flight. A guard evaluated against a stale record may be syntactically valid and semantically well-defined yet inappropriate for the real decision. The required consistency level is not automatically the strongest possible one; it depends on what stale or conflicting information can cause and on the cost of delaying work. Data patterns and data-centric accounts supply distinctions, not a universally optimal storage policy. [EWM-S014; EWM-S017; EWM-S039]

Correlation is an independent obligation. A case identifier, work-item identifier, message identifier, retry identifier and business key may coincide in a trivial setting but diverge under reuse, concurrency and recovery. The objective is not to assign an expensive global identifier service to every noun. It is to prevent an event or action from being attributed to the wrong lifecycle when that difference matters. A correct identifier still grants no authority, and a durably preserved incorrect correlation remains incorrect. [EWM-S018; EWM-S026]

Human work requires comparable distinctions. Offering a task to a group, allocating it to an individual, claiming it, starting it, suspending it and declaring it complete are not the same transition. Delegation may require re-evaluating eligibility and duty constraints. An authorised role may be unstaffed; a nominally available person may lack relevant context; a workload policy can discourage accurate reporting. The resource perspective cannot be reduced to an annotation on a control-flow node. [EWM-S015–S016]

The mature form of completion evidence is proportionate rather than omniscient. Where the promise is “prepare a label”, a declaration plus the label may suffice. Where the promise is “dispatch the parcel”, label creation alone does not establish dispatch. The consumer must know which claim is being made. That may require a confirmation, inspection, sampling rule or authorised acceptance, but it does not imply a universal requirement for surveillance, exhaustive documentation or a second approver. The language-action and situated-work sources motivate this separation; the examples and selection rule are analytical. [EWM-S034; EWM-S050]

## 5. Exceptions, recovery and interaction with the world

### Cancellation is not rollback, and silence is not an observed failure

Anticipated exceptions can be modelled with known handlers. Unanticipated deviations require a decision about repair, suspension, cancellation, migration or a different legitimate continuation. Even a well-developed exception taxonomy may assume that the exception is promptly detected and classified. Actual systems must ask how that observation becomes available and what remains uncertain when it does not. [EWM-S016]

Timeouts are a critical example. A remote action can commit while its response is lost. A timeout then supplies evidence about missing confirmation, not proof that the action did not occur. Cancellation is likewise a scoped request or control transition; an external worker may not yet have stopped, and cooperative activity cancellation can be ignored. Thus the handling decision must preserve uncertainty until a valid retry contract, query, reconciliation or accepted residual resolves the relevant claim. [EWM-S026]

Compensation is a further action. It may restore some economically or operationally relevant condition, but it need not recreate the original physical or social world. It may need different authority and may itself fail. This is not an objection invented only for modern workflows: the original Sagas discussion explicitly considers errors beyond the idealised compensation path. The evolved property is therefore remedial action with explicit limits, not compensation-as-magic-undo. [EWM-S020, §6]

### Exactly once requires a declared boundary

An engine can make a useful scoped exactly-once guarantee without guaranteeing every arbitrary outside consequence. The ExoFlow work is important precisely because the correct response to exaggerated marketing is not an equally exaggerated claim that exactly-once semantics are always impossible. Its result relies on its task, determinism, idempotence or rollback and persistence assumptions. Those assumptions must hold for the application at the chosen effect boundary. [EWM-S044, §§3.1–3.2 and §8]

The engineering question is consequently specific: what logical operation is repeated, which participant recognises it, what is committed atomically, how long does a deduplication key remain meaningful, and what must happen when the outside state cannot be queried? Idempotence, a transaction boundary, safe recomputation, compensating action and manual reconciliation are not interchangeable answers. They are alternatives or coupled mechanisms selected by the actual effect semantics.

### Durable history and live reality remain different

Persistent instance state can allow recovery after a crash. Replay-based systems impose an additional implementation contract: orchestrator behaviour must remain compatible with the durable history, while nondeterministic external work is handled through the appropriate activity/event boundary. Other systems can preserve continuity by checkpointing or different state-machine mechanisms. Deterministic replay is therefore domain-specific within Workflow Management, not a requirement on every engine. [EWM-S027–S029]

Retention should preserve what the particular recovery and evidence claim needs. Keeping every possible event forever is not automatically safer or more mature; discarding identifiers needed to recognise a repeated external action can be unsafe. The WfMC audit specification is evidence of a defined event interface, not a modern universal archival or legal-retention mandate. A later target must supply its actual record consumers and constraints. [EWM-S045]

Across organisational boundaries, a message being sent, delivered, understood, accepted and acted upon are different claims. Orchestration supplies one locus of control; choreography need not create a central controller at all. Locally sound participants can still compose badly when their interactions mismatch. Formal inter-organisational analysis is useful, but it cannot manufacture participant cooperation or authority outside its model. [EWM-S018; EWM-S038; EWM-S050]

## 6. Change without erasing an instance’s history

A definition can be valid while a partially executed instance has no valid direct image in it. Suppose the new process removes an activity that an old worker is currently performing, or introduces a data requirement that the old instance never established. The new graph’s soundness does not decide the disposition of the live activity, its inputs or its already-sent external request. ADEPTflex’s coupling of schema and state transformation supports the separate property of running-instance migration validity. [EWM-S021, printed pp.106–107]

There is no universal obligation to migrate. Keeping an old definition available for its current instances may be safer and cheaper, provided references, compatible workers and obligations remain manageable. Conversely, an urgent defect can make coexistence unacceptable. The discriminator is the actual compatibility and residual-risk relation, not “always latest” versus “never change”. Replay-based version constraints are one concrete implementation manifestation of the broader continuity question. [EWM-S021; EWM-S028]

Declarative and case approaches change what must be decided in advance, but not the need for correctness or accountability. A set of constraints can be inconsistent or leave activities dead; local permission is not assured global progress. Discretionary case planning needs relevant data, capable actors and completion criteria. CMMN’s lifecycle permits particular terminal states in its completion conditions; completion is not equivalent to every child having succeeded. The right response is to state the business acceptance interpretation, not silently rewrite the standard or prohibit all closure after failure. [EWM-S035; EWM-S019, §8.6.1]

Self-revision is supported only in a bounded sense: observations and justified exceptions can inform an authorised change to definitions or selected mechanisms, with continuity conditions for running work. A model, engine or agent does not acquire authority to revise its own obligations merely by generating a proposal. The new rule cannot be its own retrospective justification. This final constraint is an analytical boundary of the composition rather than a claimed universal theorem of the field.

## 7. What the empirical and current evidence actually establishes

The longitudinal deployment study is unusually important because it retains a difficult denominator. Ten organisations and 25 intended processes do not become 25 successful replications: five organisations implemented seven processes, and only five process comparisons supported validated simulation after two exclusions. Implementation failure and measurement/model limitations belong in the evidence. Most adjusted comparisons in implemented cases are favourable, while a material HRM lead/waiting-time exception remains. Abandonment was primarily attributed to organisational or programme changes, not demonstrated engine defects. The programme supports contextual benefit and adoption-risk assessment, not a universal causal improvement claim. [EWM-S025, §§3–5; Figures 4–5]

Scientific workflow evidence supplies another useful boundary. Provenance and execution validation can support reuse while leaving the expected or scientifically valid result unestablished. The Yevis discussion makes that limit explicit. The inspected Taverna account also contains iteration and streaming, so scientific workflows cannot all be used as evidence for a universal DAG-only ontology. These are domain translations with their own data, environment and output-oracle obligations. [EWM-S041–S042]

The 2025 PanDA presentation offers recent operational fault evidence, but its reported population and follow-on site selection must remain attached to the result. The slides were inspected, including the relevant diagrams; the full proceedings methods were not accessible. The report therefore does not turn the presentation’s fault percentage into a rate for workflow systems generally, does not infer an unreported observation window, and does not treat fault counts as the same thing as lost value. [EWM-S049, slides 6 and 9–15]

The current-year frontier is genuinely relevant but not mature by virtue of its date. The semantic-isolation preprint proposes making changing semantic dependencies explicit in durable AI work. Its static audit reports four exposure-positive projects among 54 classified durable within 100 eligible selected projects. This is not an observed incident rate or proof that its compatibility contracts are universally correct. Its separate controlled prototype experiments do show prevention of four tested semantic anomalies: static pinning suffices for the closed dependency set, while online extension handles the demonstrated late-discovered dependency. Stronger shared-write and visible-effect serialisability remains future work. The n8n study analyses more than 6,000 public templates and distinguishes execution from AI configuration, but static templates are not runtime traces. Exact denominator and hidden/custom-action limits are preserved. [EWM-S030, §§2–5.1; EWM-S031, §§3–5]

Accordingly, EWM-073 is an assumption-sensitive proposal-level concern, and EWM-074—generally safe autonomous workflow optimisation—remains **UNRESOLVED**. That is an examined conclusion. The supplied recent evidence is insufficient to establish the combined semantic preservation, authority, external-effect safety and practical benefit necessary for the general claim. More descriptions of the same proposals would not constitute independent validation.

## 8. The composed account in operation

The system begins logically with a purpose and an acceptance boundary, not temporally with a compulsory first phase. A case can receive data, requests and external events at many times. The selected definition or constraint model determines permissible state changes. Data conditions, resource capacity and authority qualify what can actually be undertaken. Participants then act through human work or application interfaces, changing real objects or commitments. The engine or shared record receives only the observations its interfaces provide.

Ordinary enactment is coupled to several loops. A missing or contradictory observation can trigger inquiry and reconciliation. A failure can suspend, cancel or redirect work. Cancellation changes the completion question and may create remedial obligations. A justified structural defect or contextual change can trigger a separate revision decision. None of those loops must run serially through a single pipeline, and none automatically grants the revising actor new authority.

Two composition-induced properties make this account stronger than the union of its sources. **EWM-075, closure accounting**, connects local completion, cancellation, human acceptance and residual external obligations. It asks whether remaining work has been settled, legitimately transferred or explicitly accepted—not whether every task ended in a success state. **EWM-076, continuity across version and external references**, connects identity, persistence, retry, migration and version coexistence. It asks whether a recovered or transformed instance can still interpret its governing semantics and outstanding outside actions. Neither invariant is claimed as a separately established academic school or a theorem proved jointly across all cited engines.

The minimal coherent form can be a named task or case, an intelligible next/completion rule, an accountable participant, proportionate result evidence and an explicit decision when uncertainty matters. Richer forms are triggered by consequential concurrency, complex data dependencies, scarce or constrained resources, long-lived failure exposure, autonomous participants, running-instance change or reuse across changing scientific/semantic environments. A later audit must establish that those triggers actually occur. It must also be allowed to conclude that existing arrangements are adequate, a simpler form dominates, a mechanism is inapplicable or evidence is insufficient.

The remainder of this report gives the full genealogy, denominator, models, criticism and selection conditions. The JSON model is a precise relational account: it distinguishes enabling, constraint, evidence, communication, feedback, alternatives and conflicts. It is not an implementation blueprint with one service, owner or artefact per property.

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_GENEALOGY

This is a plural graph. Influence is used only where the inspected source documents a relevant transmission or extension; related problems without that evidence are labelled convergence, shared ancestry, translation or analogy. A self-edge records criticism and revision within a programme rather than creating a fictitious new school.

### EWM-G01 — Office information systems, document routing and heterogeneous precursors

**Problem and mechanism.** Coordinate office information, documents, tasks and applications rather than only individual computations. Plural office/document, image, database, messaging and transaction technologies later converge in workflow enactment.

**Reception and limit.** WfMC’s later taxonomy documents a retrospective convergence; it does not settle the earliest invention or every influence edge. Ellis and Nutt 1980 was verified bibliographically only; detailed original mechanisms are not reconstructed from unread text. [EWM-S001; EWM-S002; EWM-S012]

### EWM-G02 — Situated and cooperative-work criticism

**Problem and mechanism.** Formal routes may omit the practical work through which people interpret plans and coordinate. Freeflow mediates representation/action; activity-theoretic design treats plans as resources used in situated work.

**Reception and limit.** Freeflow and Bardram explicitly engage the criticism; neither establishes that all formal models are harmful. Suchman 1983 and Bowers et al. 1995 originals are access-limited; direct evidence comes from inspected later work, not an invented reconstruction. [EWM-S009; EWM-S010; EWM-S011; EWM-S034]

### EWM-G03 — Long-lived transactions and Sagas

**Problem and mechanism.** Long-running work cannot always retain one atomic database transaction and its locks. Decompose work into committed components with compensating and forward-recovery actions.

**Reception and limit.** Later workflow exception/recovery accounts import this distinction, while specific engines implement different boundaries. Compensation is fallible and constrained by available inverse/remedial actions; physical equivalence is not guaranteed. [EWM-S020; EWM-S016]

### EWM-G04 — ActionWorkflow and the language-action branch

**Problem and mechanism.** Coordination requires commitments and acceptance, not only transmission of information or material steps. Distinguish proposal, agreement, performance declaration and satisfaction within an interaction.

**Reception and limit.** A native alternative account of workflow purpose; retained selectively rather than made a compulsory four-step ritual. Developer-origin account; its speech-act structure need not describe every kind of work or consent. [EWM-S050]

### EWM-G05 — WfMC Workflow Reference Model

**Problem and mechanism.** Heterogeneous workflow systems need shared terms, interfaces and separation of definition from enactment. Reference objects and interfaces connect build-time definitions, run-time services, worklists, applications and administration.

**Reception and limit.** The 2004 retrospective criticises continuing information-marshalling and integration limits. Interface standardisation does not prove runtime semantic equivalence, adoption or economic benefit. [EWM-S001; EWM-S002; EWM-S003]

### EWM-G06 — Workflow nets, Woflan and soundness variants

**Problem and mechanism.** Syntactically plausible models can deadlock, leave residual work or contain dead tasks. Petri-net analysis, short-circuit reasoning and reductions; later classification of distinct soundness predicates and decidability boundaries.

**Reception and limit.** Later YAWL and declarative work reuse and extend verification concerns without inheriting every theorem. Formal assumptions and implementation correspondence are mandatory; reset/cancellation extensions change analysis possibilities. [EWM-S004; EWM-S005; EWM-S007; EWM-S008]

### EWM-G07 — Workflow control-flow patterns

**Problem and mechanism.** Products and languages use similar words for materially different coordination behaviour. Pattern vocabulary and increasingly explicit contextual semantics distinguish choices, joins, concurrency, repetition and termination.

**Reception and limit.** YAWL explicitly responds to expressive gaps; the 2012 dispute remains partly access-limited. Context assumptions, completeness and evaluation claims are disputed; pattern support is not a full product-quality measure. [EWM-S006; EWM-S023; EWM-S024; EWM-S032; EWM-S051]

### EWM-G08 — Data, resource and exception perspectives

**Problem and mechanism.** Control-flow alone cannot capture information availability, human work allocation or recovery behaviour. Separate but coupled taxonomies of data scope/binding, work-item lifecycle/resource rules, and exception detection/handling.

**Reception and limit.** The multi-perspective view informs later process-aware systems and this synthesis’s joint-readiness model. Taxonomies and historical tool comparisons are not deployment frequencies; exception analysis may assume prompt detection/classification. [EWM-S014; EWM-S015; EWM-S016]

### EWM-G09 — YAWL

**Problem and mechanism.** Existing workflow languages inadequately express selected patterns and cancellation/join semantics. A formal workflow language combines Petri-net ancestry with multiple instances, cancellation regions and OR-joins.

**Reception and limit.** Used as a base for worklets and related research, not evidence that one engine unifies the whole field. Important composition results exclude unrestricted OR-join cases; expressiveness brings verification cost. [EWM-S013; EWM-S037; EWM-S051]

### EWM-G10 — ADEPTflex and adaptive running-instance change

**Problem and mechanism.** Unexpected change must not destroy the validity of already running cases. Admissible graph/schema operations are coupled to state and data transformations with preconditions.

**Reception and limit.** Later flexibility classifications preserve change as one mode among alternatives rather than a universal cure. Model and supported-operation bounds; prototype-era evidence, not arbitrary migration correctness. [EWM-S021; EWM-S036]

### EWM-G11 — Inter-organisational workflows

**Problem and mechanism.** Local process correctness does not ensure compatible exchange between autonomous participants. Loosely coupled local workflow models plus explicit communication places/contracts support composition analysis.

**Reception and limit.** Executable orchestration and choreography address related interfaces; a direct influence on each standard is not assumed. The analysed communication/reliability model bounds results; organisational agreement cannot be manufactured by a net. [EWM-S038; EWM-S018]

### EWM-G12 — Business artifacts and data-centric operational specification

**Problem and mechanism.** Activity-centric descriptions can obscure the business objects whose evolving data define useful work. Represent business artifacts, their relevant data and operational lifecycle.

**Reception and limit.** Shares concerns with case handling, but similarity alone is not documentary proof of derivation. Interacting artifacts still require correlation, access and global consistency obligations. [EWM-S039; EWM-S022]

### EWM-G13 — Case handling

**Problem and mechanism.** Knowledge-intensive cases require discretion and relevant information beyond one activity worklist. Make the case and its data central, allowing authorised performers to choose available work.

**Reception and limit.** Related case-management standardisation later exposes discretionary planning; strict historical derivation is not claimed. Information access does not create skill or legitimate authority; universal performance superiority is not shown. [EWM-S022; EWM-S019]

### EWM-G14 — Worklets

**Problem and mechanism.** Repeated contextual exceptions require controlled flexibility without redesigning the whole workflow every time. Select small context-sensitive subprocesses through a rule-based worklet service.

**Reception and limit.** Explicit YAWL implementation and documented use of ripple-down-rule ideas; not autonomous unconstrained self-revision. Rule proliferation, interface incompatibility and context drift remain possible. [EWM-S037]

### EWM-G15 — Declarative constraint-based workflows

**Problem and mechanism.** A complete prescribed order can be needlessly restrictive where only selected constraints matter. Describe allowed behaviour using constraints and verify their interactions through formal representations.

**Reception and limit.** The 2008 flexibility taxonomy places declarative methods among alternatives, not at the inevitable end of a progress line. Consistency, dead activities, pending obligations and intelligibility remain real burdens. [EWM-S035; EWM-S036]

### EWM-G16 — Executable service and process/case standards

**Problem and mechanism.** Shared interchange and execution notation must support service interaction, events, tasks and discretionary cases. BPEL specifies orchestration/correlation; BPMN combines model and executable constructs; CMMN specifies case planning/lifecycles.

**Reception and limit.** Historical engine tests show incomplete support and regressions; exact current version claims are limited to inspected pages. Conformance classes, extensions, underspecification and implementations limit portability; these edition dates are not field-origin dates. [EWM-S017; EWM-S018; EWM-S019; EWM-S033]

### EWM-G17 — Execution observations and practical evaluation

**Problem and mechanism.** Enactment needs meaningful histories, and practical benefit cannot be inferred from a design diagram. Audit event semantics, model/log comparison and contextual longitudinal observation.

**Reception and limit.** This is a documented intersection with process analysis/mining; no sibling Evolved output is used. Records omit outside work; conformance and adoption do not establish causal benefit. [EWM-S045; EWM-S043; EWM-S025]

### EWM-G18 — Scientific workflow translation

**Problem and mechanism.** Scientific work coordinates computations, services, data and environments across reuse and failure. Workflow engines combine iteration/dependency control, provenance, execution validation and distributed resource management.

**Reception and limit.** The inspected Taverna version has loops/streaming; scientific workflow is not uniformly DAG-only. Service/environment decay and successful-but-wrong outputs limit reproduction claims; accessed failure-study originals vary in depth. [EWM-S041; EWM-S042; EWM-S047; EWM-S048; EWM-S049]

### EWM-G19 — Durable execution and cloud orchestration

**Problem and mechanism.** Crashes, retries and long histories must not lose or duplicate consequential work. Persistent state/checkpoints, replay or speculative recovery operate under declared determinism and effect assumptions.

**Reception and limit.** A translation/convergence with prior workflow concerns, not proof that modern branding invented durability. Scoped exactly-once results do not encompass arbitrary external effects; replay and version compatibility are implementation-specific. [EWM-S020; EWM-S026; EWM-S027; EWM-S028; EWM-S029; EWM-S044]

### EWM-G20 — Low-code, agent/workflow hybrids and semantic-environment proposals

**Problem and mechanism.** Visual and learned orchestration can hide runtime actions and depend on changing semantic context. Static template analysis and proposed semantic-isolation contracts separate design wiring, runtime effects and compatibility assumptions. The semantic-isolation prototype also compares controlled anomaly prevention and a late-discovery case.

**Reception and limit.** Kept as assumption-sensitive frontier mechanisms and an unresolved autonomous-optimisation claim, not mature consensus. New preprints, selected static corpora and no independent broad safety/effectiveness demonstration. [EWM-S030; EWM-S031]

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_DOMAIN_MODELS

The following models give an explicit common vocabulary to the per-property domain profiles. They are analytical reconstructions of sourced distinctions, not a claim that every source shares one state model. A profile reference resolves to the named model field; a `PROPERTY_APPLICATION` adds the particular trigger, mechanism, authority or postcondition. A model field can explain why a concern is inapplicable in a cheap/manual configuration rather than requiring machinery for it.

### EWM-DM01 — Definition / instance / work-item semantics

A definition D_v supplies activities, constraints and a declared semantics at version v. An instance I=(case,v,control,data,work,interaction,recovery references) is one enactment, not D_v itself. An event e may induce I -> I_prime under D_v. A work item is an offered or undertaken unit within I; an external business object X has its own identity and state. This tuple is an analytical interface, not the exact storage schema of WfMC or any engine. Enabled(I,a), Authorised(actor,a), Able(actor,a) and EffectAchieved(X,a) are different predicates. Their distinctions may be implicit in a tiny manual procedure, but must not be conflated when consequences depend on them.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Definition version, case, work item, attempt and external object need distinct equality/lifetime meanings where they can diverge; separate databases or UUIDs are not mandatory.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** An enabling rule determines permitted transitions, not necessarily one predetermined next task; multiple actions and recurring interaction can remain enabled.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Inputs and incoming events must be bound to the right scope and case; an identifier alone does not validate its referent or value.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** An enactment model can offer work but cannot create availability, competence or external authority; a single known performer is a valid simple case.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Correctness first names the predicate and model. A correct transition or terminal state is not automatically a valid business result.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** The definition must say which work is stopped or continued by an exception; a non-engine procedure may use an explicit human decision instead.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** An external action changes X through the responsible participant. The model records a claim/observation; compensation, when available, is a further action.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Persist only facts/references needed for the chosen continuity claim. When recovery is irrelevant for an ephemeral action, no durable engine is implied.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** An instance must retain an intelligible governing definition; changing D_v does not automatically migrate I or revise past outside actions.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** The model observes its declared interface; request, declaration, acceptance and result can be different observations.

**Limit.** No universal state model or compulsory engine architecture is claimed. Purpose and acceptable consequence remain supplied by the actual context. [EWM-S001; EWM-S002; EWM-S004; EWM-S018; EWM-S026; EWM-S050]

### EWM-DM02 — Control-flow coordination

Let A be the semantic activation scope for a split, loop entry or subworkflow call. Branch membership B_A and a join predicate J_A(current marking, possible future arrivals) determine convergence. For a fixed AND-join, all required inputs must be present; for inclusive joins, whether another input can still arrive can be non-local. Cancellation changes the state on which J_A is evaluated but is not generally a success arrival. Multiple-instance thresholds, race arbitration and termination use separate predicates. A may be represented by net state rather than an explicit tagged identifier. Recursion has a parent-child continuation relation; an available base case alone does not force it to be selected.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Correlate branch arrivals to the intended case and activation. Recursion introduces distinct invocation instances, whether represented explicitly or by the formal semantics.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Sequence, choice, splits, synchronisation, merging, multiple instances, deferred choice, interleaving, loops and cancellation are alternative constructs with different enabling rules.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Guards need defined input and missing-value semantics; binding and concurrent update assumptions cannot be inferred from graph edges.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Token readiness does not establish worker/resource readiness; critical sections only exclude actors in their enforced scope.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Pattern satisfaction is a local/contextual behavioural claim. General joins may need non-local analysis and do not inherit every ordinary-net composition theorem.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Specify cancelled region, interrupted activity and downstream join effects; termination may be local while external work continues.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** Control cancellation and merging do not undo outside effects; successful control convergence can coexist with unsuccessful task consequences.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Recovery must not double-count arrivals or mix activation scopes. Pure in-memory calculations may instead be safely recomputed.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Changing a join or loop while a case runs requires compatibility with its current arrivals and active work, not only a valid new graph.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** A displayed token or branch status reflects model state; hidden worker activity and delayed events may differ.

**Limit.** Block structure, unrestricted graphs and DAGs are selected by required behaviours and analysis cost; no universal preference is imposed. [EWM-S006; EWM-S013; EWM-S017; EWM-S051]

### EWM-DM03 — Soundness and model limits

For an ordinary WF-net N=(P,T,F) with source i and sink o and each node on an i-to-o path, start with marking [i]. Classical soundness requires: (1) for every reachable M, there exists a firing sequence to [o]; (2) if a reachable M contains an o token, then M=[o]; (3) every transition can occur in some execution from [i]. This does not say every run eventually finishes. The short-circuited-net characterisation connects soundness to liveness/boundedness under the stated ordinary-net model. Other soundness variants change populations or quantifiers. Reset arcs, data, time and resource extensions change what can be proved and how. Model-to-engine correspondence is a separate argument.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** The theorem’s population and initial marking are part of the claim; one case does not automatically establish a many-case property.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Transition firing follows the selected net semantics. A possible completing continuation is not an observed scheduling policy or inevitable progress.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Ordinary control-flow abstractions omit data values unless explicitly extended; false or missing business inputs remain outside the proof.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Unconstrained transitions abstract human availability and authority. Resource/time feasibility needs a separate model or operational evidence.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** The guarantee is the exact soundness variant for the exact net class. Reduction and composition must preserve that property; general reset-net undecidability does not forbid all bounded analysis.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Cancellation needs an appropriate extended model or bounded analysis; adding it can invalidate a proof over the original ordinary net.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** Final marking is not proof of material success, accurate output or a successfully executed compensation.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** A structural theorem does not by itself specify crash persistence or external retry handling. Add those only when the claimed scope includes them.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** A theorem about a new definition is not a proof that a current marking/data state can migrate into it.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** A model-checker result proves an abstract property if its encoding is faithful, not unmodelled organisational facts.

**Limit.** Formal validity is often strong while comparative deployment benefit is weak or absent. No theorem is refuted merely by an implementation violating its assumptions. [EWM-S004; EWM-S005; EWM-S007; EWM-S008; EWM-S013; EWM-S033]

### EWM-DM04 — Data and correlation

An input binding names a producer/value, scope and binding time. A guard can evaluate true, false, unresolved or error according to the selected semantics; missing values are not silently assigned a universal interpretation. Correlation maps an incoming message to zero, one or more candidate cases; ambiguous/no-match outcomes require an explicit rule. Business-object identity is distinct from case, work item and message identity. Data-centric models use lifecycle state and available information to permit work. Concurrent mutation adds a snapshot/conflict/isolation choice that must fit the decision being made.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Case, document/object, task and message identities have separate lifecycles; interacting objects need not fit one artificial one-to-one case key.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Data availability and value predicates can enable or constrain work independently of control tokens; data-dependent errors require more than syntactic graph validity.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Declare scope, copy/reference/shared semantics, ownership, binding time, correlation rule and freshness required for each consequential input.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Data access policy and action authority require a basis beyond case membership; a visible input is not necessarily permitted for every use.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Availability, type and correlation correctness do not establish truth of the underlying data or every business invariant.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Skips, cancellation and repairs can remove a producer or change the input expected by a later task; check those paths.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** An external source may commit a change while the workflow holds stale data; neither copying nor logging restores the original world.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Retries need stable operation keys and a policy for re-reading or preserving inputs; both blind reuse and blind refresh can be wrong.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Migration must preserve required input history and pending correlation meanings or deliberately transform them with a justified rule.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** The engine sees the values supplied to it, not automatically the current or correct state of external business objects.

**Limit.** This packet does not prescribe a universal isolation level, global shared store, access policy or legal permission model. [EWM-S014; EWM-S017; EWM-S018; EWM-S021; EWM-S022; EWM-S039]

### EWM-DM05 — Resources and human work

The work-item lifecycle distinguishes the needed states among enabled/offered, allocated, started, suspended, resumed, completed, failed and withdrawn. These are a vocabulary, not a mandatory universal sequence. An authorisation relation U x T and cross-task constraints define permissible assignments; satisfying them does not solve time ordering or provide available workers. Delegation transfers a declared state and responsibility only under applicable constraints. A person’s declaration and a recipient’s acceptance are evidence-bearing interactions, distinct from the material result.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Work item and performer identity must survive handover; the person occupying a role can change during a long case.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Enabling, offering, claiming and starting have different consumers and need only be separated where their distinction changes coordination.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Provide the performer with necessary data and handover context; prior external actions must not vanish during reassignment.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Role eligibility, permission, competence, calendar availability and capacity are different assumptions; duty constraints may make an assignment globally infeasible.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Workflow satisfiability addresses assignment under a specific constraint language; the inspected WSP model explicitly does not solve time ordering.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Withdrawal, suspension and delegation require treatment of still-running work; a reassignment label does not physically stop the old performer.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** A human click or report is evidence of a declaration, not automatic verification of the intended external effect. Risk may justify trusting that declaration.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Recovery must identify the current responsible party and any abandoned or continuing work; a simple acknowledged handover may suffice.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Changes to roles, rules or task meaning can invalidate an assignment mid-case and need a relevant recheck, not necessarily universal migration.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** Informal coordination and work outside the system may be material. More instrumentation can be intrusive and still incomplete.

**Limit.** No compulsory surveillance, independence-of-witness rule or new authority is inferred. Fairness and workload goals require actual participants and context. [EWM-S015; EWM-S019; EWM-S034; EWM-S040; EWM-S050]

### EWM-DM06 — Exceptions, cancellation and compensation

An exception response is analysed along work-item, case and recovery dimensions. Interrupting stops or requests stopping the affected scope; non-interrupting creates additional handling while original work may continue. Requested, acknowledged and observed cessation may differ. A timeout establishes missing expected response/progress. If action f has changed external state X, compensation c is a further relation from the then-current state, not generally f inverse. Residual obligations may be fulfilled, legitimately waived, transferred or explicitly accepted as unresolved; this classification is an analytical closure interface, not a vendor lifecycle.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Identify the affected item, containing scope, action attempt and external operation before cancellation or repair.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Define what remains enabled after handler activation; cancellation is not universally a success input to a later join.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Handlers need current context and prior-effect identity; incomplete detection/classification is itself a limit.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** The handler or operator needs authority for each remedy, which may differ from the authority for the original action.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Correct handler control-flow does not prove complete exception detection, successful stopping or restored business state.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Interrupt, cancellation, termination and suspension differ; specify propagation and independently continuing work.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** Compensation is a fallible forward action with restricted scope. Irreversible effects and new intervening facts can prevent exact restoration.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Timeouts and lost responses require safe retry or reconciliation; failed remedies cannot be silently treated as completed.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** A repair can be case-specific; changing a handler for future instances does not retroactively repair an already interrupted state.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** An engine may record cancellation before a worker stops; external success/failure can remain unknown.

**Limit.** No universal rollback or infallible compensator exists. Closure may be legitimate with accepted residual work, but cannot misstate it as fulfilled. [EWM-S016; EWM-S017; EWM-S018; EWM-S020; EWM-S026; EWM-S051]

### EWM-DM07 — Interorganisational interaction and commitment

Participants P_i control local transition systems. A shared protocol constrains observable messages and commitments without necessarily exposing private states or installing a central controller. Sent, received, accepted, declared performed and accepted-as-satisfactory distinguish information transport from operative commitments; only distinctions needed by the relationship must be materialised. Local soundness plus channel composition can fail globally when participants’ waits or order assumptions disagree. The inspected formal model assumes predefined asynchronous partners and abstracts resources/data, whereas actual participant autonomy requires additional evidence.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Message, partner, business object and case identity must be matched across organisational boundaries; local IDs may require mappings.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Local orchestration and shared protocol/choreography constrain different transition systems; global compatibility is not automatic from local validity.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Message contracts specify fields, correlation, ordering and error interpretation; schema validity alone does not establish shared meaning.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Each participant retains its effective authority and capacity. An agreed protocol cannot make an unwilling or unavailable participant act.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Compatibility/completion claims are bounded by partner and channel models. Open strategic participants are not validated by a closed cooperative proof.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** One party’s timeout or cancellation request may not stop another party’s work; negotiate continuation and rejection semantics.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** External action belongs to its performer; compensation or waiver may require new agreement from affected participants.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Lost/duplicate/reordered messages and externally committed actions need an explicitly chosen retry/reconciliation protocol.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Protocol and definition versions can coexist only if pending messages and commitments remain interpretable.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** Central monitoring sees exchanged signals, not every partner’s private state or actual satisfaction.

**Limit.** The language-action cycle is useful for some cooperative work, not an exhaustive ontology or automatic proof of informed consent. [EWM-S018; EWM-S038; EWM-S045; EWM-S050]

### EWM-DM08 — Persistence, replay and recovery

Let H be persisted coordination evidence, I its reconstructed state and X the external state that may differ. Recovery must justify the next action from H plus any necessary observation of X. Replay-based runtimes constrain the command sequence produced from recorded history; nondeterministic I/O is isolated/recorded through the runtime contract. Other workflow implementations use different persistence mechanisms and need not obey the same programming rule. Exactly-once output claims require a declared cooperative boundary: ExoFlow explicitly depends on determinism classification and idempotency or idempotent rollback, among other persistence conditions.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Retain case, version, work-item, attempt and intended business-operation identity at the granularity needed for recovery.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Recovery restores a justified enabled set rather than blindly restarting the last visible activity; command replay is a specific implementation discipline.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Know which inputs are frozen and which must be re-read; preserve correlation and deduplication keys for the relevant horizon.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Work-item ownership after crash must be resolved; technical replay cannot make an unavailable person or service act.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Durable/replay correctness and business-effect count are separate claims. A scoped exactly-once theorem is not a universal effect guarantee.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Cancellation may be cooperative and delayed; recovery must consider live workers and remedies, not only terminal engine status.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** An outside action may have committed without a recorded acknowledgement. Idempotency, transactions or reconciliation must cover that boundary.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Choose sufficient checkpoint/history/intent evidence and test crash points. Retention is based on consumers and obligations, not forever by default.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Compatible code and governing definitions must remain identifiable. Pinning, coexistence or checked migration are alternative continuity strategies.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** Persisted history is authoritative about recorded decisions only under its integrity assumptions, not automatically about current external reality.

**Limit.** No exact storage design, fixed retention time or universal replay engine is prescribed. External uncertainty may force bounded manual adjudication. [EWM-S020; EWM-S026; EWM-S027; EWM-S028; EWM-S044; EWM-S045]

### EWM-DM09 — Dynamic change and flexible/case execution

A migration is a guarded relation mu from an instance governed by D_v to an instance governed by D_w. The guard examines current control/data state, completed/pending work and relevant interaction obligations. Sound(D_w) alone does not imply mu is valid. Flexibility by design selects an anticipated branch; deviation permits bounded departure; underspecification leaves parts to later choice; change revises the governing definition/state. Declarative rules constrain allowed finite traces and pending obligations. Case handling makes available data and actor judgement central. Worklets late-bind a subworkflow using contextual rules. These remain alternative or combined configurations, not one compulsory ladder.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Keep the current case and its historical/version context intelligible across change; new cases may simply start under a new definition.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Prescribed paths, allowed-trace constraints and discretionary plan items have different enabling/completion rules that require explicit coupling.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Changes must preserve or deliberately remap required inputs, past data and pending message bindings.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Authorised planners and reviewers determine which changes or discretionary actions are permitted; technical edit capability is insufficient.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Check current-state migration validity, rule consistency and relevant progress assumptions. A sound target or individually sensible constraints are insufficient.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** An exception can justify case repair without becoming a general rule; new definitions do not automatically resolve existing exceptions.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** Past outside actions remain facts to reconcile, even if the model is revised; state edit or model undo is not physical compensation.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Recovery and version continuity must still work after change; pinning or coexistence may be cheaper than automatic migration.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Declare change population, compatible state mapping, deployment selection and retirement conditions for old definitions.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** History of formal changes may omit informal adaptations; participant feedback is needed where that affects continuation or interpretation.

**Limit.** Greater freedom is not always better. Choose by uncertainty, skill, required constraints and verification/communication cost. [EWM-S021; EWM-S022; EWM-S019; EWM-S035; EWM-S036; EWM-S037]

### EWM-DM10 — Monitoring and external correspondence

An observation function O maps some aspects of an actual execution/world history into recorded events. O is generally not injective: two worlds can produce the same completion click while differing in whether work occurred. Conformance evaluates a model against that chosen log abstraction, not every fact about the world. Monitoring needs a consumer who can distinguish explanations and take an authorised action. Scientific rerun success and output validity are separate; static low-code/agent definitions and observed runtime performance are separate evidence objects. Semantic-environment compatibility is a current proposal whose adequacy needs evaluation.

**DEFINITION_INSTANCE_AND_WORK_ITEM_IDENTITY.** Logs require declared case/work-item/attempt/object mapping; a single-case projection may omit many-object relationships.

**CONTROL_FLOW_AND_ENABLING_SEMANTICS.** Observed ordering must fit the model’s semantics; timestamps or display order do not automatically establish causality.

**DATA_AND_CORRELATION_ASSUMPTIONS.** Events may omit payload, provenance, freshness or downstream acknowledgement needed for a stronger claim.

**RESOURCE_AVAILABILITY_AND_AUTHORITY.** Human work outside the engine may be material; monitoring creates no automatic authority to intervene or surveil.

**SOUNDNESS_OR_CORRECTNESS_MODEL.** Conformance, test execution and performance metrics support distinct questions. Neither fitness nor a successful exit validates the business/scientific result.

**EXCEPTION_INTERRUPT_AND_CANCELLATION_SCOPE.** Error and cancellation records describe detected/reported events; undetected failures and overlapping fault categories limit diagnosis.

**EXTERNAL_EFFECT_AND_COMPENSATION_BOUNDARY.** An event-to-world claim needs an appropriate observation or acceptance source; repeated copies of one record are not independent corroboration.

**PERSISTENCE_RETRY_AND_RECOVERY_OBLIGATION.** Keep the evidence needed for actual recovery/review consumers and know what compaction removes; complete logging is not a universal obligation.

**VERSION_AND_RUNNING_INSTANCE_MIGRATION.** Definition, engine, data and semantic-environment versions affect interpretation; static template exports may hide code and configuration.

**ENGINE_OR_MODEL_OBSERVABILITY_LIMIT.** Every monitored claim is bounded by the recorder’s observation and the study’s sampling. Current preprints/static corpora do not establish general causal benefit.

**Limit.** The formal observation-function account is this report’s explanatory abstraction, not a new empirical result. Its purpose is to prevent stronger claims than the evidence allows. [EWM-S025; EWM-S031; EWM-S041; EWM-S042; EWM-S043; EWM-S045; EWM-S049]

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_INTERNAL_TENSIONS

A tension is not resolved by saying “balance” or “be adaptive”. Each entry identifies conditions favouring each side, the actual hybrid and a discriminator; a hybrid can itself fail.

### EWM-T001 — Predictable coordination versus Situated discretion

`EWM-002`, `EWM-031`, `EWM-050`, `EWM-051`, `EWM-052`. **Costs:** Prescription costs exceptions; discretion costs explanation and consistent constraint enforcement.

**First side:** Stable repeated work with known dependencies and high consequence of omission. **Second side:** Cases whose next valid action depends on information not yet available.

**Supported hybrid:** Prescribed routine fragments with explicit case/constraint boundaries and a repair path. **Hybrid failure:** Nominal discretion that silently bypasses constraints, or a rigid model with hidden manual work.

**Discriminator:** Can meaningful alternatives be specified without prohibitive branching, and can workers explain a permissible next action? **Uncertainty:** No universal crossover threshold or comparative comprehension effect is established.

### EWM-T002 — Expressiveness versus Affordable verification

`EWM-007`, `EWM-012`, `EWM-015`, `EWM-016`, `EWM-017`, `EWM-034`. **Costs:** General joins, reset arcs and unbounded state increase analysis cost; restrictions can distort the work.

**First side:** A restricted/structured model expresses every consequential case. **Second side:** Real loops, cancellations or unstructured dependencies would be lost by restriction.

**Supported hybrid:** Restricted core plus explicitly delimited exceptional submodels and appropriate bounded analysis. **Hybrid failure:** The exception interface violates the assumptions of the core proof.

**Discriminator:** Which required counterexample cannot be expressed or verified in the simpler model? **Uncertainty:** Finite tests and abstractions may not cover all interactions.

### EWM-T003 — Central visibility versus Participant autonomy

`EWM-022`, `EWM-038`, `EWM-039`, `EWM-040`, `EWM-077`. **Costs:** Global information helps diagnosis but may expose private state and imply unavailable control.

**First side:** One accountable organisation actually controls the relevant resources and actions. **Second side:** Independent participants retain private policies and external authority.

**Supported hybrid:** Local orchestration with agreed public message/commitment contracts. **Hybrid failure:** A global model silently assumes internal details or cooperation absent from local contracts.

**Discriminator:** What is the minimum public behaviour necessary to establish the shared coordination claim? **Uncertainty:** Open strategic participation exceeds inspected formal models.

### EWM-T004 — Fast automatic recovery versus Honest external-effect uncertainty

`EWM-035`, `EWM-036`, `EWM-037`, `EWM-042`, `EWM-043`, `EWM-044`. **Costs:** Waiting and manual repair cost time; blind retry/compensation can cause duplicate or irreversible damage.

**First side:** Stable operation keys and cooperative idempotent/transactional participants make repetition safe. **Second side:** Opaque or irreversible effects cannot be queried or safely repeated.

**Supported hybrid:** Automatic recovery inside a verified boundary, explicit adjudication at ambiguous external edges. **Hybrid failure:** An internal timeout is treated as evidence about an outside commit.

**Discriminator:** Does the recovery mechanism have evidence or a protocol covering each possibly repeated effect? **Uncertainty:** Some outcomes may remain permanently uncertain.

### EWM-T005 — Adaptability versus Comprehensibility and continuity

`EWM-048`, `EWM-049`, `EWM-050`, `EWM-051`, `EWM-054`. **Costs:** Change/rule accretion burdens operators; freezing may make legitimate work impossible.

**First side:** Instances are short-lived and old semantics remain acceptable. **Second side:** Changed obligations or case information make the old route unusable.

**Supported hybrid:** Version coexistence plus checked migration for cases that actually need it; selective worklet reuse. **Hybrid failure:** Current state or pending messages do not fit the new version despite a sound target.

**Discriminator:** Can existing cases complete acceptably without migration, and what exact state transformation would be required? **Uncertainty:** Business validity can remain outside formal migration proofs.

### EWM-T006 — Durable evidence versus Retention cost and exposure

`EWM-043`, `EWM-046`, `EWM-055`, `EWM-059`, `EWM-072`. **Costs:** More history costs storage/interpretation and may expose unnecessary data; less history can break recovery.

**First side:** Long retry, review or scientific reproducibility claims need the retained facts. **Second side:** Consumers and obligations have expired or a sufficient compact representation exists.

**Supported hybrid:** Snapshots plus required provenance and operation references, with a tested retirement rule. **Hybrid failure:** Compaction removes deduplication or migration facts still needed by a late event.

**Discriminator:** Which future legitimate claim fails without each retained record? **Uncertainty:** Legal and domain-specific retention duties are not determined here.

### EWM-T007 — Throughput versus Fairness and valid resource use

`EWM-010`, `EWM-019`, `EWM-025`, `EWM-028`, `EWM-029`, `EWM-057`. **Costs:** Priority optimisation may starve cases or overload staff; strict equality may waste scarce expertise.

**First side:** Urgent, high-consequence or strongly constrained tasks have a justified precedence. **Second side:** Workload distribution and starvation undermine service or participation.

**Discriminator:** Who waits longer or works harder under the policy, and is that distribution justified? **Uncertainty:** Fairness has no universal metric in the inspected corpus.

### EWM-T008 — Assurance of consequences versus Proportionate trust and privacy

`EWM-030`, `EWM-055`, `EWM-057`, `EWM-058`, `EWM-077`. **Costs:** Independent observation can be intrusive and costly; unexamined declarations can conceal failure.

**First side:** High-consequence or disputed results make unsupported declaration inadequate. **Second side:** Low-consequence trusted work and directly accountable participants make reports sufficient.

**Supported hybrid:** Claim-relative evidence and selective checks, with explicit acceptance meaning. **Hybrid failure:** The check merely repeats the same source, or formal acceptance masks coercion.

**Discriminator:** What error would this extra observation detect that the existing evidence would miss? **Uncertainty:** Suitable observation and consent require the actual setting.

### EWM-T009 — Portable semantics versus Useful specialised capability

`EWM-004`, `EWM-018`, `EWM-041`, `EWM-061`, `EWM-070`. **Costs:** Portable subsets may be weak; extensions increase exit and testing costs.

**First side:** Switching/interoperability risk is concrete and the shared subset is adequate. **Second side:** Specialised constructs offer demonstrated value exceeding the cost of dependency.

**Discriminator:** Can required behaviour and in-flight state actually move at an acceptable cost? **Uncertainty:** Historical conformance studies do not rank current offerings.

### EWM-T010 — Stable interpretation versus Improvement of learned components

`EWM-024`, `EWM-047`, `EWM-062`, `EWM-073`, `EWM-074`. **Costs:** Pinning preserves context but can retain defects; updates can invalidate old decisions.

**First side:** Reproducibility or long-lived commitments depend on a stable environment. **Second side:** New evidence or defects make the old environment unacceptable.

**Supported hybrid:** Bounded reviewed updates with explicit compatibility and current-instance adjudication. **Hybrid failure:** The same optimiser authors both the change and the sole evidence that it is safe.

**Discriminator:** What externally assessable consequence test would reject an incompatible update? **Uncertainty:** Current preprints do not establish a generally reliable semantic compatibility oracle.


## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_COMPOSED_SYSTEM

The composed system is a conditional network of decision and action relations. It is not a super-engine, forced serial pipeline or checklist whose every entry must be instantiated. Its purpose is to keep a work claim intelligible from definition through action, observation, exception and change. The environment may contain autonomous participants and unobservable effects, so the system must be able to communicate qualified claims rather than manufacture certainty.

### Objects, participants and effect-bearing operation

The domain models distinguish definition/version, instance state, data/object identity, work-item ownership, messages and commitments, outside effects and retained evidence. A modeller or custodian defines admissibility within their authority; a worker or service executes an action; a requester or other legitimate consumer interprets acceptance; an exception/recovery actor decides how to proceed when observations or assumptions fail. These roles may coincide in a small case. The composition does not require a separate owner for each property.

Requests and work offers communicate what is sought. Claims and commitments identify who undertakes what. Applications and people change material objects, information or relationships. Declarations and observations feed back into the case state and acceptance decision. A model can constrain a step without causing it; a communication can be received without accepted commitment; acceptance can legitimately settle an obligation without establishing that every earlier task succeeded. These distinctions are supported across the reference model, resource/exception work, case standards and language-action branch. [EWM-S002; EWM-S015–S019; EWM-S050]

### Ordinary, exceptional and revision loops

Ordinary enactment repeatedly evaluates relevant constraints, makes permitted work available and incorporates observed results. Parallel branches may remain active together. An exception can interrupt one scope while another continues. Missing confirmation can cause reconciliation rather than a fresh task. A closure decision examines live work and residual commitments. A revision loop consumes observed defects or justified new needs, but changes the definition only through an independently authorised decision; live instances require compatible coexistence or a valid transformation. [EWM-S013; EWM-S016; EWM-S021; EWM-S027–S028; EWM-S035–S038]

EWM-075 adds explicit closure accounting across those loops; EWM-076 adds continuity of meaning and external references across recovery/version change. They are researcher-derived composition invariants, with no claim of joint formal proof or controlled validation. They have costs: stronger closure can add approval burden, and continuity can prolong legacy interpretation. An authorised transfer or sufficiently informative checkpoint may be preferable to keeping a case or its entire history alive indefinitely.

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_HYBRIDISATION_AND_EXTERNAL_RELATIONS

Documented imports and translations are preserved rather than suppressed because neighbouring disciplines also study them. Petri-net verification supplies bounded behavioural reasoning. Long-lived transaction work supplies compensation/recovery distinctions. The worklet mechanism explicitly combines a workflow setting with contextual rule selection. Scientific workflows translate enactment into data, environment and reproducibility concerns. Durable execution combines workflow state with distributed recovery contracts. [EWM-S004–S008; EWM-S020; EWM-S037; EWM-S041–S044]

The relationship to Business Process Management is one of scope/interface: organisational purposes, value and redesign decisions constrain useful enactment, but a workflow engine cannot establish them by executing a model. The relationship to Process Mining is an evidence interface: execution observations can support discovery or checking only under their identity, semantics and completeness assumptions. The inspected conformance source is a legitimate intersection, not access to a sibling corpus. [EWM-S001–S003; EWM-S025; EWM-S043]

The language-action branch and current learned agents must not be conflated because both use agent terminology. Similarly, workflow assignment “patterns” in the satisfiability literature are not simply the control-flow pattern catalogue. Shared names establish neither influence nor semantic equivalence. [EWM-S040; EWM-S050–S051]

`SIBLING_CORPORA_NOT_CONSULTED` and `CROSS_TRIFECTA_SYNTHESIS_NOT_PERFORMED` remain true. Later reconciliation must compare semantic levels, inputs, outputs, assumptions, costs and common evidence, not count one new mechanism per source property.

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_STRONGEST_SURVIVING_PROPERTIES

The strongest retained distinctions are robust because violating them creates a clear category error or uncontrolled consequential failure. Their operative implementations remain proportional and contextual. They are not all supported by independent field effect estimates.

**EWM-001 — Identity separation: definition, instance, work item and external object.** Strongly retain distinction of lifecycles, not a compulsory UUID for every noun. Minimal form: A single stable task may use a named record rather than an identifier service; omit separate identifiers when their lifecycles genuinely coincide. [EWM-S001; EWM-S002; EWM-S018; EWM-S026]

**EWM-002 — Explicit executable enabling and transition semantics.** The invariant is explicit meaning when meaning matters, not compulsory automation. Minimal form: For stable one-person work, a short conditional procedure with known next/completion conditions may suffice. [EWM-S002; EWM-S004; EWM-S017; EWM-S027]

**EWM-004 — Proportional choice of enactment infrastructure.** Strong as a selection principle; no empirical claim that the smallest tool always has the best outcome. Minimal form: No engine, a shared record, direct agreement or a short procedure can be adequate where concurrency, duration and recovery risk are small. [EWM-S002; EWM-S009; EWM-S025]

**EWM-006 — Decision guards with defined ambiguity and absence behaviour.** The strongly retained property is declared branch meaning, not universal exclusive choice. Minimal form: A human resolves an infrequent ambiguous choice explicitly rather than maintaining a general rules engine. [EWM-S006; EWM-S014; EWM-S017]

**EWM-018 — Model-to-engine semantic correspondence.** Strongly retain the distinction and checking obligation where a model verdict is relied upon; no current engine ranking is inferred. Minimal form: A small conformance test set for the used subset is often enough; exhaustive language support is unnecessary. [EWM-S017; EWM-S027; EWM-S033]

**EWM-022 — Case, message and business-object correlation.** Strongly retain correct association whenever asynchronous or multi-case work exists. Minimal form: A verified human lookup is sufficient at low volume when ambiguity is explicitly resolved. [EWM-S018; EWM-S026; EWM-S039; EWM-S045]

**EWM-026 — Work-item lifecycle and accountable ownership.** Strongly retain accountable ownership when responsibility may otherwise be ambiguous. Minimal form: One accountable performer and a clear ready/done exchange can suffice when no claiming or handover race exists. [EWM-S015; EWM-S026; EWM-S045]

**EWM-030 — Human task completion distinguished from achieved effect.** Strongly retain the distinction, not universal distrust of human reports. Minimal form: For low-consequence trusted work, a declaration may be adequate evidence; do not mandate independent witnessing of everything. [EWM-S009; EWM-S019; EWM-S041; EWM-S050]

**EWM-034 — Interrupt versus non-interrupt semantics and cancellation scope.** Strong distinction whenever interruption exists; no universal cancellation implementation is prescribed. Minimal form: A direct stop/continue agreement may suffice for an immediately observable human task. [EWM-S016; EWM-S017; EWM-S026; EWM-S051]

**EWM-035 — Timeout interpreted as uncertainty rather than external failure.** Strongly retain epistemic distinction; no blanket rule against retries. Minimal form: Wait or query the authoritative service rather than immediately retrying when delay is safer than duplication. [EWM-S026; EWM-S020; EWM-S044]

**EWM-036 — Compensation distinguished from rollback.** Strongly retain rollback/compensation distinction; no compulsory compensator for actions with no meaningful inverse. Minimal form: Use a real atomic transaction inside a suitable boundary, or a case-specific remedial action, when those are cheaper and more faithful. [EWM-S020; EWM-S016; EWM-S018]

**EWM-043 — Retries and duplicate external-effect control.** Strongly retain duplicate-effect control wherever repetition is possible; no single technique is universally necessary. Minimal form: Disable automatic retry and adjudicate the uncommon ambiguous case when the action has no safe repeat protocol. [EWM-S020; EWM-S026; EWM-S044]

**EWM-048 — State-sensitive running-instance migration.** Strongly retain the distinction when migration is attempted; checked migration is not compulsory for every release. Minimal form: Leave the old case on its original definition or explicitly close/restart it with consequence accounting when migration adds no value. [EWM-S021; EWM-S035; EWM-S036]

**EWM-058 — Observation completeness and event-to-world correspondence.** Strongly retain the distinction; no general demand for omniscient observation. Minimal form: A trustworthy participant report is sufficient when the consequence and risk justify it; independent sensing is not universal. [EWM-S009; EWM-S041; EWM-S045; EWM-S050]

The two composition-induced invariants, EWM-075 and EWM-076, are retained in evolved form rather than being elevated to established independent theory. Their value is in connecting already differentiated obligations without erasing their model and evidence boundaries.

## EVOLVED_WORKFLOW_MANAGEMENT_ADVERSARIAL_SYNTHESIS_VERDICT

**Verdict: retain a conditional core with alternatives; reject universal engine doctrine.** The most defensible core consists of identity and semantic clarity, coupled control/data/resource conditions, accountable work and interaction, scoped failure/recovery treatment, valid continuity through change and qualified evidence of consequence. Its strongest logical insight is that a representation, permission, action and result are different claims, each with a consumer and boundary.

The strongest objection to unification is that the cited schools do not share one complete semantics or empirical validation setting. Ordinary workflow nets, declarative traces, case lifecycles, human commitments and durable DAG recovery cannot have all their guarantees added together as though their assumptions were identical. The model therefore preserves alternatives and labels its integrative invariants analytical. A later actual configuration must discharge the compatibility obligations instead of inheriting a fictional universal theorem.

The strongest objection to practical deployment is that formal precision and implementation capability can cost more than the coordination problem warrants, displace work outside the system, increase maintenance burden or concentrate inappropriate authority. Those objections alter the packet: EWM-004 makes proportionality part of the core; EWM-031 preserves participation; EWM-058 limits observation claims; EWM-063 requires contextual benefit evidence; and nine unconditional overclaims receive adverse dispositions. No measured universal benefit or target adoption is asserted.

What would defeat a retained payoff? A mechanism that does not change a relevant decision, whose cheap alternative controls the same failure more adequately, whose required observation/authority is unavailable, or whose side effects dominate the avoided risk should be omitted, narrowed or retired. A formal claim is defeated by a counterexample within its actual assumptions, not merely by an outside failure; a transfer claim is defeated when its environment violates those assumptions. The test is mechanism- and claim-specific, not loyalty to a school.

The unresolved agent-optimisation claim and access-bounded original critique remain visible. The 13 analytical probes demonstrate internal discriminating capacity, not empirical validation. The strong core is usable for a later neutral audit, but it does not authorise changes to any actual system.
