### EPM-006 — Ordering proportional to temporal evidence

**Disposition / kind:** ASSUMPTION_SENSITIVE / COUPLED_CRITERION_AND_MECHANISM. **Examination:** EXAMINED.

**Mature form:** Use the strongest ordering supported by the evidence: a total order where justified, an explicit partial order or bounded alternatives otherwise. Keep tie-breaking separate from observed chronology and test sensitivity when an algorithm requires a sequence.

**Controlled failure:** Coarse clocks and recording delays induce an arbitrary total order, creating false directly-follows, concurrency or conformance claims.

**Trigger:** Timestamp ties, mixed granularity, asynchronous systems, uncertain timestamps or consequential order-sensitive findings. **Non-trigger / cheap path:** Ignore order for an order-insensitive question, or flag the few ambiguous pairs instead of enumerating every possible trace.

**Prerequisites and required local evidence:** Knowledge of clocks, recording delay and lifecycle; an algorithm able to express uncertainty or a declared approximation. Evidence must be sufficient to establish this observable postcondition: A conclusion is either invariant across plausible orders or qualified by the orders on which it depends. A document saying it exists is not by itself that evidence.

**Domain profile:** EPM-DM01; full ten-dimensional profile in the property record. Specific limitation to test: Coarse clocks and recording delays induce an arbitrary total order, creating false directly-follows, concurrency or conformance claims.

**Consumer and authority:** Analyst exposes ordering alternatives to the consumer; system timestamps alone do not settle the real chronology. Analytical competence does not itself supply additional organisational authority.

**Criticism and transfer:** Enumerating realisations can be expensive; asserted uncertainty sets can omit the true order. No probability follows merely from listing possibilities. Scalable partial-order analysis across uncertain interacting objects remains a transfer-sensitive frontier.
