## Introduction to the established tradition

Business Process Management (BPM) is an interdisciplinary tradition concerned with understanding, organising, improving, enacting and monitoring work that produces outcomes across activities, people and organisational boundaries. Its organisational approaches emphasise purpose, process ownership, capabilities, people and culture; its information-systems approaches emphasise representation, analysis and enactment of process-aware work. These are overlapping perspectives rather than a single definition tied to a particular notation or software suite. [S002-L2; S003-L1–L2; S004-L1; S043-L1–L2]

Its genealogy is plural. Inspected 1990 formulations by Michael Hammer and by Thomas Davenport and James Short made radical redesign, cross-functional outcomes and the relationship between information technology and work organisation prominent. Davenport and Short also explicitly discussed industrial-engineering and quality-management antecedents, multiple objectives and work-life concerns. A technical branch has retrospectively documented office-information-system and workflow antecedents. Later developments include process modelling, redesign heuristics, organisational capabilities, declarative and case-oriented flexibility, process intelligence and responsible or sustainability-oriented approaches. This is not a simple progression in which BPR disappeared and BPM replaced it. [S001-L1–L2; S002-L1–L4; S003-L1; S004-L3; S014; S037; S043]

## What “Evolved” means here

**Evolved Business Process Management** is the analytical label of this independent, criticism-tested synthesis. It is not an assertion that an established academic school uses that compound name. The study preserves useful mechanisms, narrows them where assumptions or evidence require, rejects universalised claims and leaves examined open questions unresolved.

The frozen population contains 73 candidates: 58 conditionally usable, 14 non-retained and one unresolved. All ten declared domain families and all registered candidates were examined, with material access or transfer limits identified where appropriate. These counts concern this bounded research corpus, not the total number of ideas in the field. The full denominator remains in `EVOLVED_BUSINESS_PROCESS_MANAGEMENT_PROPERTY_LEDGER.json`; a public introduction should not present a few attractive principles as though they were the complete study.

The source corpus comprises 48 works. It includes primary papers, standards, conceptual arguments, empirical/design studies, reviews, publisher records and research agendas. Their number is not a count of independent effectiveness experiments. The source table preserves exact works, dates, access levels, claim locators, empirical units and common datasets or programmes.

## Evolution under criticism

### From local optimisation to received outcomes

Early process arguments challenged arrangements in which a department could improve its own measure without improving the whole result. The surviving property is to identify the recipient’s outcome, the material process boundary and the burdens imposed on other actors. A locally faster operation is not automatically an end-to-end improvement. The analytical synthesis makes the acceptance boundary explicit: a change should be evaluated against its declared outcomes and constraints, not merely the easiest recorded metric. [S002-L2; S016-L1–L2; EBPM-001, EBPM-002, EBPM-017, EBPM-069]

This does not mean that every process must include every conceivable social consequence. An unbounded scope can make inquiry and accountability impossible. The requirement is proportional examination of material effects, explicit limits and a route for challenging omissions. Nor does identifying a customer automatically settle how to treat employees, non-paying recipients, partners or other affected parties. That is partly a governance and value question, not a fact a process diagram can decide. [S028; S030; analytical refinement]

### From process pictures to warranted and usable representations

A model may serve communication, analysis, training or execution, and these purposes can require different detail. Syntactic correctness is not the same as accurately representing the relevant work, and neither is the same as being understood by its consumer. BPMN and other standards establish specified semantics and conformance arrangements within their scope; they do not establish organisational truth or successful outcomes. A narrative or table may be adequate when it serves the actual decision. [S004-L3; S009-L1–L2; S011-L1–L2; EBPM-010–EBPM-014]

Modelling recommendations remain defeasible. A later experiment involving 21 students and 42 models did not find a statistically significant benefit on the tested outcomes from the examined guidelines. Eight models were excluded from the model-issues analysis because of serious syntax errors after incomplete tasks. The small educational setting, exclusions and task-order design limit interpretation. It is not proof that guidelines never help, but it does not support treating their use as sufficient evidence of quality. The study therefore retains behaviour-preserving complexity management while superseding a universal numerical decomposition threshold. [S009-L1–L2; S010-L1–L2; EBPM-012, EBPM-063]

### From official accounts to evidence of actual work

Documents, interviews, observation and event logs provide different evidence. Official accounts may omit informal coordination; logs may omit activity outside an instrumented system; interviews may report intended rather than performed work. Conversely, a formal record can be valuable for accountability even when it is incomplete as a description. Dourish’s dual-use analysis is important because it prevents both naïve model realism and indiscriminate removal of useful records. [S035-L2; S041-L1–L2; EBPM-006–EBPM-009]

The 2026 workaround study supports investigating departures as clues to a mismatch between a process and its context. A workaround can prevent a problem, but can also evade a necessary control or transfer risk elsewhere. Its existence warrants inquiry, not automatic approval or condemnation. The paper’s development and demonstration cases are not counted as independent replications of every earlier case on which it builds. [S025-L1–L2; EBPM-037]

### From redesign slogans to conditional mechanisms

Removing, combining, parallelising, centralising and standardising work are not interchangeable prescriptions. Each changes a different dependency or responsibility. Task elimination can remove a control; parallelisation can add synchronisation risks; centralisation can create queues; task combination can require additional expertise or conflict with segregation of duties. The 25-practitioner heuristic survey provides relevant experience and objections, not controlled evidence that every recommendation works in every process. [S015-L1; S016-L1–L2; EBPM-023–EBPM-030]

The mature form is a warranted intervention hypothesis, comparison with feasible alternatives and outcome evaluation. No intervention is a legitimate alternative when the expected gain is unsupported or the existing arrangement is adequate. Radical change remains an option when conditions warrant it, not the default meaning of improvement.

### From ownership labels and maturity scores to functioning governance

A process owner’s title is not equivalent to effective authority, resources or knowledge. Governance configurations vary, and the inspected 2026 taxonomy is a descriptive/design contribution rather than a controlled comparison demonstrating one best hierarchy. A capable existing manager may supply the necessary function; another setting may require more explicit cross-functional or inter-organisational arrangements. [S020-L1–L2; S021-L1–L3; EBPM-031, EBPM-033]

Capability and maturity models can organise diagnosis, but their scores are proxies. The systematic review and later capability/maturity discussion identify limited validation and weak support for some prescriptive paths. A positive association between maturity and a reported outcome does not establish that raising a score causes the outcome. The retained question is what consequential capability is present or absent and how that judgement changes a real decision. [S022-L1; S023-L1–L2; S040-L1; EBPM-034, EBPM-056]

### From deployment to demonstrated consequences

The longitudinal workflow study preserved 25 intended processes in ten organisations, of which seven processes in five organisations were implemented. Five of the seven had lower raw lead time and two had higher raw lead time. The study’s historical setting, changing conditions and partly modelled comparisons restrict transfer. It shows why planned deployment, implemented use and improved outcome must remain separate claims; it supplies neither a universal benefit estimate nor a general failure percentage. [S027-L1–L3]

Automation and low-code delivery are therefore conditional options. Ease of implementing an interaction does not establish that the business decision is sound, that its author is authorised, or that someone can maintain its dependencies. The low-code and citizen-development literature inspected here supports attention to scope, competence, support and governance, with limited evidence for representative outcome effects. [S029-L1; S044-L1–L2; S045-L1–L2; S046-L1–L2; S047-L1; EBPM-041, EBPM-072]

## Composition: how the surviving parts work together

The central analytical synthesis connects three distinguishable achievements: **warranted understanding**, **legitimate and feasible action**, and **observed consequences**. One does not establish the next automatically. A diagnosis can be sound but lack an actor who can act; an authorised intervention can rest on mistaken information; a deployed change can fail to achieve its purpose.

The smallest coherent arrangement contains a meaningful purpose and boundary, enough evidence about actual work, an intelligible shared account, an actor with appropriate knowledge and powers, proportionate action or justified non-action, and observation with a correction route. It requires neither a mandatory diagram nor an automation platform. More elaborate techniques need separate triggers: interacting constraints may justify formal checking, consequential resource uncertainty may justify simulation, and a complex network may need explicit negotiation and governance.

The system is not a compulsory serial lifecycle. New evidence can reopen discovery; an exception can interrupt enactment; a poor outcome can reopen the process boundary or redesign hypothesis; and appropriately independent tasks can proceed concurrently. The model preserves five alternative configurations: lightweight stewardship, structured repeatable work, goal- and case-oriented professional work, negotiated inter-organisational work, and evidence-intensive decision support.

Four analytical properties arise from joining the parts rather than merely listing them. EBPM-068 connects evidence and distinctions to authorised effects. EBPM-069 evaluates improvement within an explicit consequence boundary. EBPM-070 selects the smallest adequate portfolio of methods and controls. EBPM-071 distinguishes and connects revision of representations, actual work, objectives and governance. These are source-grounded analytical constructions, not a claim that this exact bundle has been empirically validated as a universal programme.

## Strongest surviving properties

The nine candidates classified `STRONGLY_RETAINED` are end-to-end purposes and beneficiaries (EBPM-001), triangulated discovery of performed work (006), purpose-fit representation (010), an explicit modelling-versus-execution contract (014), outcome-valid plural measures (017), elapsed time distinguished from work effort (018), causal restraint in intervention selection (022), ownership with effective authority (031), and decision-linked monitoring (044).

“Strongly retained” expresses the synthesis’s bounded judgement of the criterion or mechanism. It does not mean each has a strong randomised evidence base for universal economic benefit. Their dependencies, costs, exceptions and cheaper adequate alternatives remain binding. Other useful mechanisms are deliberately conditional, assumption-sensitive, vulnerable to gaming or specialised—for example simulation, maturity assessment, AI assistance and formal declarative checking.

## Ceremonies and caricatures to avoid

A process map is useful when it supports a consumer’s understanding or a defined analytical/execution task; its existence alone is not success. A workshop is useful when it exposes consequential disagreement, not because holding one is a ritual. A maturity assessment is useful when it changes a justified capability decision, not because the score rises. A dashboard is useful when observations inform action or reasoned non-action, not because its indicators refresh. An owner designation is useful when it connects responsibility to exercised powers.

Simplification must not destroy a real protective function. Removing a document that coordinates a difficult handoff, records an external obligation or supports accountability is not automatically mature. The question is whether the same function has become unnecessary or is supplied adequately by a cheaper alternative. The corpus rejects empty observance, not documents, meetings, formal methods or automation as categories. [S005; S020; S041; EBPM-056, EBPM-060, EBPM-061, EBPM-070]

## Current frontiers without hype

The recent corpus includes more precise short-term simulation, causal-dependency discovery, governance taxonomies, problem-focused workaround inquiry, responsible and Green BPM, LLM-assisted modelling, low-code participation and agentic proposals. These sources have different evidential roles. A simulation that predicts selected recorded outcomes is not a proven intervention policy; a taxonomy is not evidence of a universally superior structure; a sustainability survey is not a lifecycle impact measurement. [S018; S019; S021; S025; S030–S034; S040; S044–S047]

The 2026 current-state simulation study includes counter-results and uncertainty limitations; these narrow its transfer claims. The LLM benchmark evaluates 16 models against 20 process descriptions in a specific evaluation framework, not autonomous operation of a new organisation. The 2026 agentic BPM manifesto is a research agenda. Autonomous agentic self-revision therefore remains `UNRESOLVED` in the property ledger, while independently checked modelling assistance is retained only under explicit assumptions. [S018-L1–L3; S033-L1–L2; S034-L1–L2; EBPM-052, EBPM-053]

At the research cut-off, the advertised BPM 2026 conference in late September/early October was still in the future. Its event notice establishes venue continuity, not evidence from future presentations. Publication, online issue and repository-deposit dates should not be silently substituted for dates of experiments or deployment. [S039-L1; S033; S044]

## Suggested public page outline

1. **What BPM studies:** end-to-end work, received outcomes and organisational/technical plurality.
2. **Where it came from:** documented antecedents and parallel branches, with attribution limits.
3. **What survived criticism:** discovery, modelling, analysis, governance, discretion and consequence-sensitive improvement.
4. **How the parts operate together:** the conditional core, five configurations and separate evidence/action/outcome claims.
5. **Where machinery is unnecessary:** cheap paths, non-triggers, substitution and retirement.
6. **Evidence and current questions:** representative studies, access limits, negative findings and unresolved agentic revision.
7. **Research corpus:** full ledger, source table, composition, audit and synthesis intakes, coverage and manifest.

This outline is a documentation proposal, not a claim that a host already implements the described mechanisms.

## Claims not to make

Do not claim that “Evolved BPM” is an established school, that this report invented process thinking, or that all BPM descends from one founding text. Do not describe a neat BPR-to-BPM replacement where the evidence instead shows coexistence and divergent emphases.

Do not claim that a notation-conforming or formally verified model is therefore an accurate account of actual work, that a completed workflow is necessarily a completed external obligation, or that a named owner has effective authority. Do not claim that documentation, a maturity score or a refreshed dashboard demonstrates improved outcomes.

Do not claim a universal BPM benefit or BPR failure percentage from selected cases. Do not treat the 48 sources as independent experiments, repeated datasets as replications, or a later issue date as another study. Do not convert a cross-sectional association into an intervention effect or a prediction benchmark into proof of causal understanding.

Do not claim that automation is intrinsically improvement, that maximum utilisation is universally desirable, that every workaround is legitimate, or that every deviation should be suppressed. Do not present lower cost, resilience, fairness, privacy and sustainability as objectives the corpus proves can always be maximised together.

Do not present the exact composed system as a field-validated universal method. Its four composition-induced properties are analytical claims whose transfer requires new evidence. Do not state that a particular host has adopted, implemented or benefited from them without separate evidence about that host.

Do not claim that the unread Workflow Management or Process Mining synthesis agrees with this one. The provisional grouping is not a completed cross-tradition reconciliation. Preserve globally unique property keys and the frozen denominator in any later integration.

## Public evidence and attribution boundary

For factual claims, cite the original work or issuing-body specification appropriate to the claim. Cite this analytical synthesis for its selection, rejection and composition judgements, not as a substitute for original empirical evidence. Abstract-only access supports only the inspected abstract. Normative proposals concerning responsibility or sustainability should be introduced as arguments or criteria rather than empirical consensus. Formal claims must retain their assumptions, and operational claims must retain their setting and comparison.

The corpus supports an independently useful introduction to BPM even if no later trifecta is assembled. It establishes neither host-system adoption nor cross-tradition agreement.
