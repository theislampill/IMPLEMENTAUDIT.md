## Executive judgement

The most defensible BPM core is not a notation, lifecycle picture, automation suite or maturity ladder. It is a set of coupled responsibilities: identify a meaningful end-to-end purpose and boundary; establish enough about work as performed to support a decision; communicate that understanding in a representation suitable for its consumer; connect decisions to actors with legitimate and effective powers; carry out proportionate interventions; and examine received consequences in ways that can expose mistakes in both the work and its representation. The historical sources establish several parts of this account; their exact reconciliation here is the researcher’s analytical contribution. [S001–S005; S020; S027; S041; S043]

The strongest correction is to separate **making a warranted distinction**, **being authorised and able to act on it**, and **achieving the intended consequence**. A process map can support understanding without establishing execution semantics. A valid executable model can still implement the wrong decision. A named process owner can lack cross-functional influence. A deployed system can fail to be used, and used software can fail to improve the received result. These are different deficiencies and require different evidence. The synthesis therefore does not turn all BPM activities into one compulsory sequence or make every source property a new organisational role. [S011-L1–L2; S020-L2; S027-L1–L3; S041-L1–L2; analytical composition EBPM-068]

The complete candidate population is **73**. All are examined: 53 without a designated material evidence restriction at the property level, and 20 with explicit evidence limits. There are **58 conditionally usable candidates**, **14 non-retained candidates**, and **one unresolved candidate**, autonomous agentic process self-revision. A useful candidate is not an unconditional requirement. Retention includes context-dependent, assumption-sensitive, easily gamed and domain-specific mechanisms. The eight payloads preserve the denominator, source roles, criticism, alternatives and remaining uncertainty rather than presenting only favourable properties.

Four properties are explicitly composition-induced: coupling warranted distinctions to authorised effects (EBPM-068), boundary-sensitive improvement acceptance (EBPM-069), the minimal sufficient portfolio of BPM machinery (EBPM-070), and coupled revision of representations and work (EBPM-071). They expose gaps between otherwise individually useful components. They are not claimed as newly discovered empirical laws or as academic consensus. The complete composed bundle has not been field-validated as a single intervention.

## 1. What BPM is, and what it is not

BPM concerns work that produces outcomes across activities, people, resources and organisational boundaries. Its units are processes, cases or other work objects, dependencies, decisions and received results—not merely departments or software functions. Organisational formulations emphasise sustained process orientation, strategic alignment, governance, people and culture. Information-systems formulations emphasise modelling, analysis, enactment and diagnosis of process-aware systems. These overlap, but neither viewpoint can silently replace the other. A useful definition for this report is therefore **the coordinated identification, understanding, organisation, intervention and revision of end-to-end work**, with the actual meaning of “end-to-end” itself subject to examination. [S002-L2; S003-L1–L2; S004-L1; S005-L2; S043-L1–L2]

Business process re-engineering is a related intervention orientation, especially associated with radical reconsideration of inherited arrangements. BPM is not simply BPR after it became less radical: radical redesign, incremental improvement, information-systems engineering and organisational capability approaches coexist. Workflow management provides important mechanisms for coordinating or enacting work; BPM additionally asks whether the resulting work serves a warranted purpose and whether organisational powers, participation and consequences support it. Process mining contributes event-based evidence and models; it does not by itself settle what is unlogged, whose outcomes matter, who may intervene or whether an intervention caused improvement. [S001; S002; S003; S035; S036; S037]

Business analysis can investigate requirements and decisions without taking the whole recurring process as its organising unit. Enterprise architecture can situate capabilities, information and systems at broader abstraction levels. Project management organises a change endeavour, whereas BPM can concern continuing operational work before and after that project. These are working boundary distinctions, not claims of mutually exclusive professional jurisdictions. RPA and low-code platforms are technological options that may serve selected process needs; neither defines the tradition. No claim that BPM invented or universally subsumes these neighbouring activities is made. [S008; S029; S043; S045; boundary interpretation]

A process boundary is an intervention hypothesis. An organisation chart shows responsibility or reporting relationships, but does not establish the sequence, dependencies, waiting or result received across those relationships. Conversely, expanding a process boundary to include every possible consequence makes it unusable. The mature task is to identify the effects material to the decision, state what is excluded, and retain a route for challenging that exclusion. For some knowledge work, goals, obligations and case facts are stable enough to organise inquiry while the activity sequence is not. Heavy standardisation then needs a separate justification. [S002-L2; S008-L1–L2; S014-L1–L2; EBPM-002, EBPM-003, EBPM-067]

## 2. A plural genealogy rather than a progress myth

The inspected 1990 Davenport and Short paper explicitly connects process thinking with industrial engineering and the quality movement. It challenges local task optimisation and argues for attention to business processes enabled by information technology. This is documentary support for their stated intellectual engagement, not a claim that the present study has independently reconstructed all of Taylorism, Deming’s work or quality-management history. Earlier works cited by that paper remain secondary historical leads unless separately inspected. [S002-L1, S002-L4]

The same early paper already discusses multiple redesign objectives, including cost, time, quality, work-life quality, learning and empowerment. It recognises that objectives cannot ordinarily all be optimised together and that information technology can support different arrangements of control. It would therefore be a caricature to portray every early formulation as interested only in headcount reduction, or to claim that concern for people was invented by twenty-first-century responsible BPM. Later work changes emphasis, concepts and evidence; it does not create every concern ex nihilo. [S002-L4; S030-L1–L2]

Hammer’s 1990 argument supplies a prominent radical formulation: inherited work should be reconsidered rather than merely accelerated by new technology. His cases and principles make outcome organisation and coordination around whole cases salient. They are practice arguments with selected cases, not randomised evidence or a valid population denominator for a universal success/failure rate. Davenport and Short’s simultaneous formulation couples process redesign and IT, including prototype/pilot learning and inter-organisational processes. The two contributions overlap but are not treated as one identical doctrine or as a proven genealogy of every later school. [S001-L1–L2; S002-L2–L3]

A technical branch has earlier office-information-system and workflow antecedents. The inspected 2013 survey retrospectively discusses work by Ellis, Holt and Zisman, information-control and Petri-net-related models, and later workflow management. Those earlier original works were not independently read in this run. The genealogy therefore marks this branch as a retrospectively documented antecedent with a primary-access limit. The 2003 BPM survey provides a later explicit formulation connecting process modelling, analysis, enactment and diagnosis, with BPM broader than the traditional execution-centred workflow scope. [S003-L1–L2; S004-L3]

The 2000s do not supply one successor to BPR. They contain modelling and semantic work, redesign heuristics, simulation, process-aware information systems, declarative flexibility, organisational ownership and capability models. Later standards such as BPMN and CMMN codify particular representation and interchange arrangements. They establish specified meanings and conformance obligations within their scope; they do not establish that a notation is universally understood, that a conforming model is socially legitimate, or that an implementation improves a business result. [S009; S011; S012; S015–S017; S020; S042; S043]

The organisational capability branch is especially important because it prevents an engine-centred reconstruction. Rosemann and vom Brocke’s six-core-elements account includes strategic alignment, governance, methods, information technology, people and culture. The ten-principles paper later emphasises context, continuity, enablement, holism and other constraints on good BPM. These are frameworks and expert-informed propositions; they are not a guarantee that a checklist or maturity score causes success. Their useful function is to expose missing capacities and connections. [S005-L1–L2; S043-L1–L2]

Contemporary developments include process intelligence, workarounds as evidence for problem-focused improvement, context-sensitive governance, sustainability, AI-assisted modelling and agentic proposals. Some extend existing mechanisms; some import capabilities from adjacent fields; some remain agendas. The genealogy below classifies these relations explicitly instead of inferring influence from similar vocabulary or treating a newer label as a demonstrated improvement. [S021; S025; S030–S034; S036; S045–S047]

## 3. How work becomes knowable without becoming a fiction

An as-is model is a claim about selected work, for a selected purpose, based on selected observations. Interviews reveal interpretations and tacit knowledge but can idealise practice. Workshops expose disagreements but can suppress lower-status voices. Documents reveal prescriptions and accountability requirements but need not describe enactment. Observation captures situated activity but is sampled and can affect behaviour. Event data can reveal recurring recorded behaviour while excluding informal coordination, uninstrumented work and facts not expressible through its case and activity schema. None is automatically the final court of truth. [S035-L2; S041-L1–L2; S048-L1; EBPM-006–EBPM-009]

Triangulation here does not mean mechanically requiring three channels. It means using materially different evidence when a consequential uncertainty cannot be resolved by one channel, and preserving contradictions rather than averaging them away. A simple familiar service may need a few end-to-end walkthroughs with the people doing and receiving the work. A contested cross-organisational process may require observation, case samples, event provenance and a forum in which conflicting accounts can be challenged. The required intensity depends on the decision and the cost of being wrong; this selection rule is analytical rather than a claimed universal formula in the literature. [S014; S025; S035; S041; EBPM-006–EBPM-008]

Dourish’s analysis is a crucial warning against two opposite mistakes. The formal workflow description in the discussed print-shop setting did not simply mirror ongoing practice: work could begin before the required identifier existed, and coordination crossed the formal categories. Yet formal accounts also performed organisational and contractual functions. Consequently, “the model is inaccurate” does not entail “the record is useless”, and “the record is required” does not entail “the record is a complete account of work”. This paper reanalyses earlier reported field material; it is not counted as another independent deployment of that earlier case. [S041-L1–L2]

The 2026 workaround study develops an approach across three settings and demonstrates it more fully in a fourth, building on earlier case work. It supports treating workaround reports as clues to process–context misalignment. A workaround may protect a recipient or help workers complete an otherwise impossible task, but may also displace risk or undermine a necessary control. The surviving mechanism is inquiry into the problem, purpose and consequences of the departure—not unconditional adoption or suppression of deviations. Reused case material does not create independent replication merely because it appears in a later paper. [S025-L1–L2]

Representation must then be selected for its consumer. BPMN, EPCs, decision models, declarative constraints, case-oriented representations, narratives and tables have different strengths. Communication, analysis, training and execution require overlapping but non-identical detail. Syntactic correctness concerns the representation’s rules; semantic adequacy concerns whether relevant domain meaning is captured; pragmatic quality concerns whether actual consumers can use it. Agreement on a picture can conceal disagreement about a decision’s authority or a rare exception. [S004-L3; S009-L1–L2; S011-L1–L2; S012-L1–L2; S014; EBPM-010–EBPM-016]

The modelling-guidelines evidence illustrates why advice must remain defeasible. The seven-guidelines paper connects recommendations to empirical foundations, including complexity and understandability. A later experiment with 21 students and 42 models did not find a statistically significant benefit for the tested outcomes. Eight models were excluded from the model-issues analysis because serious syntax errors followed unfinished tasks. The design was small and educational; the guideline task came second, so selection, order and learning effects matter. This result neither proves equivalence nor establishes that guidelines never help. It does remove the warrant for treating compliance with guidelines, or an exact universal decomposition threshold, as sufficient evidence of model quality. [S009-L1–L2; S010-L1–L2; EBPM-012, EBPM-063]

## 4. From diagnosis to intervention: what the evidence can and cannot establish

Performance analysis must distinguish work effort from elapsed time. Waiting, batching, queues, resource calendars, handoffs, missing information and rework can dominate the time a recipient experiences. A local activity becoming faster is not enough when downstream work grows or difficult cases are excluded. Measures need a stated outcome, a defined population and relevant distributions or variants; a single favourable average can hide a worse tail or a shifted burden. These principles connect early cross-functional arguments, redesign work and later empirical process analysis. [S002-L2; S015–S018; S027; EBPM-017–EBPM-020]

Queueing and simulation are documented analytical imports, not BPM inventions. Their usefulness depends on assumptions about arrivals, resources, routing, durations and dependencies. An attractive simulation is not an observed counterfactual. Calibration to historical data may establish selected predictive adequacy, while leaving a redesign scenario unsupported because the intervention changes behaviour or demand. Causal discovery adds further assumptions: temporal or statistical dependence alone is not causal identification. The 2025 causal-dependency work is therefore retained with its model assumptions, synthetic evaluation and limited open benchmark setting, not as a general warrant for intervention from event logs. [S004-L2; S017-L1–L2; S019-L1–L2; EBPM-021–EBPM-022]

The 2026 short-term simulation study improves the treatment of an operating process’s current state and quantifies uncertainty. Its evaluations include two synthetic process models under three workload patterns and several real event-log datasets. The results are not uniform: a warm-up comparator performs better on remaining time for BPIC17 where unmodelled extraneous delays matter; uncertainty intervals can under-cover for fine-grained activity claims. The approach assumes sufficiently complete and consistent event information and uses restricted modelling constructs. Its retrospective predictive evaluation does not establish causal effects of arbitrary future redesigns. This current negative result narrows the claim rather than being relegated to an obsolete-practice footnote. [S018-L1–L3]

Redesign heuristics are options with different prerequisites. Eliminating a task can remove waste or an important control. Combining tasks can reduce handoffs while requiring broader expertise or violating segregation of duties. Parallelising work can shorten a critical path while increasing synchronisation, resource contention or inconsistent decisions. Centralising resources can reduce duplication while adding queueing and losing local knowledge. Segmenting cases can improve fit while creating unfair service tiers or misclassification costs. There is no evidence here for a universally dominant combination. [S015-L1; S016-L1–L2; EBPM-023–EBPM-030]

The 2007 use-and-impact paper reports a 25-practitioner survey and discusses qualitative objections. Its reported cost dimension concerns operational cost, not a universal accounting of implementation expense. A practitioner’s assessment of a heuristic is relevant field evidence, but is not a randomised test of that heuristic across representative organisations. The mature form is therefore an explicit hypothesis: identify the mechanism expected to change, compare plausible alternatives—including better information, organisational change, selective automation and no intervention—and evaluate the result under the actual constraints. [S016-L1–L2]

The longitudinal workflow study is especially valuable because it preserves non-implementation. Across a Dutch programme running from 2001 to 2012, 25 intended processes in ten organisations yielded seven implemented processes in five organisations. Five of those seven showed lower raw lead time, while two showed higher raw lead time. The study also uses simulation and counterfactual reasoning, with incomplete validation and changing organisational conditions. This is neither proof that workflow technology always pays nor a universal failure-rate estimate. It shows why the intervention denominator, adoption conditions, changing workload and outcome definitions must remain visible. Earlier publications from the same programme are not additional independent replications. [S027-L1–L3]

## 5. Governance and human work are part of the mechanism

A process owner matters only to the extent that the role connects knowledge, responsibility, resources and influence across the relevant work. Naming a person is not the mechanism. The descriptive ownership study finds variation in how ownership operates in practice, and the later governance work explicitly reconstructs contextual alternatives. The 2026 governance taxonomy uses development interviews, classification, evaluation interviews and additional cases; some evaluation participants overlap earlier stages. It is evidence for distinctions and configurations, not a controlled proof that one central, decentralised or federated structure is best. [S020-L1–L2; S021-L1–L3]

Effective governance also involves functional managers, sponsors, professionals, technical staff and affected recipients. Their powers need not be identical. An analyst may be competent to diagnose an issue without authority to change a service commitment; a manager may hold authority without sufficient knowledge to recognise a dangerous exception. Communication is therefore not decorative documentation: it is how differences in knowledge and power become actionable. A lighter arrangement may combine roles in one capable person; a complex network may need explicit negotiation and escalation. The necessary function, rather than a mandatory office or hierarchy, is retained. [S005; S020; S021; S028; EBPM-031–EBPM-035]

Maturity models are diagnostic hypotheses. The systematic review of 61 studies and the later capability/maturity chapter identify limits in validation, prescriptive advice, assumptions about stages and evidence of performance implications. A 2026 Polish study reports positive associations between BPM maturity and ESG readiness in a sample of 287 enterprises, but the inspected abstract does not establish an intervention effect or measured environmental improvement. A high score can organise reflection while still reward paperwork, key-informant optimism or conformity to a framework. The evolved form asks what actual capability and outcome evidence the assessment changes. [S022-L1; S023-L1–L2; S040-L1; EBPM-034, EBPM-056]

Knowledge-intensive work often cannot be reduced to a fixed exhaustive sequence without losing the judgement that makes it effective. Case and declarative approaches can preserve goals, facts and constraints while permitting qualified actors to select a path. This is not the absence of control. Constraints can conflict; people can misunderstand them; incomplete histories can invalidate change assumptions. The declarative-workflow paper supplies formal and prototype mechanisms for consistency, possible activity and history-compatible changes under its encoded assumptions. These are strong model-level results with limited deployment generality, not proofs that the constraints are ethically adequate or faithfully represent real work. [S012; S014; S042-L1–L2; EBPM-016, EBPM-036, EBPM-073]

Participation, fairness and privacy qualify process design because the process changes people’s work, opportunities and exposure—not only its throughput. The public-administration culture study is a cross-sectional association study, not a causal intervention. The social-work criticism was inspected at abstract level and cannot support invented case detail. Responsible AI-BPM contributes an explicit normative and design challenge, not an established universal evaluation metric. These limits do not make the concerns irrelevant; they make their evidential role clear. Whether a monitoring practice is legitimate remains partly a governance and affected-party question, not a question resolved by more accurate prediction. [S024; S026; S030; EBPM-035, EBPM-038–EBPM-039]

## 6. Enactment, monitoring and contemporary extensions

Enactment is a socio-technical transition, not the saving of a to-be file. It requires systems and people to perform the intended work, with feasible resource arrangements, training where needed, usable information and support for exceptions. BPM suites, service integration, RPA and low-code tools may implement parts of that transition. Their documentation or demonstrable execution semantics cannot alone establish informed participation, responsibility or successful external completion. A case marked complete internally may still leave a recipient waiting or a partner obligation unresolved. [S011-L2; S027; S029; S044–S047; EBPM-040–EBPM-043]

RPA is retained as a selective automation option, particularly for suitable repeatable interaction with existing systems. The inspected editorial establishes positioning and research challenges, not comparative economic superiority. Low-code work adds an important distinction: reducing the programming barrier changes who may create applications, but not automatically who may authorise consequential business decisions or maintain integrations. The 2025-deposited usability study involves developers, a single platform context and a small set of diagrams; the 2024 citizen-development work studies 24 organisations, while the 2026 maturity paper is abstract-only here. These support a domain-specific governance/support property, not the claim that any employee can safely automate any process. [S029-L1; S044-L1–L2; S045-L1–L2; S046-L1–L2; S047-L1; EBPM-072]

Monitoring is useful only through a consumer and a possible decision. Updating a dashboard is not demonstrated improvement. A process variant may deserve investigation rather than immediate suppression; changed demand may invalidate an earlier model; recurring exceptions may reveal a missing activity or a harmful rule. Feedback must be able to challenge the observation scheme itself. Otherwise the model defines what is logged, the log confirms the model and omitted work remains invisible. The synthesis consequently distinguishes model revision, operational revision, objective revision and revision of the BPM apparatus. Each has different authority and continuity obligations. [S025; S035; S036; S041; EBPM-044–EBPM-047, EBPM-071]

Across organisations, no single process owner necessarily has command over the whole network. Process interfaces, data meanings, service commitments and recovery responsibilities may require negotiation among parties with different incentives. The inter-organisational governance study develops and evaluates a model through a design-science process; it does not establish that every network will cooperate or that an interface standard resolves contractual disagreement. The relevant BPM contribution is to preserve shared outcome and coordination concerns while recognising autonomous powers and disclosure limits. [S028-L1–L2; EBPM-048–EBPM-049]

Sustainability extends the outcomes under examination. The Green BPM survey of 403 senior respondents reports self-assessed adoption and performance relationships, not independently measured lifecycle emissions or experimental treatment effects. The 2025 doctoral work is a research programme, not a completed outcome evaluation. The 2026 ESG study adds a contemporary association, bounded by its abstract-only access and Polish setting. Environmental or societal criteria can therefore be legitimate design constraints without claiming that generic BPM maturity necessarily improves them. The boundary must include material displacement to partners, infrastructure or users where relevant, while acknowledging measurement cost and unresolved value conflicts. [S031-L1–L2; S032-L1; S040-L1; EBPM-050]

AI-assisted modelling is better supported as bounded assistance than as autonomous organisational control. The inspected benchmark evaluates 16 language models against 20 process descriptions using a specific framework, reference artefacts and constrained evaluation conditions. It does not demonstrate that generated models are true accounts of a new organisation’s actual work or that autonomous deployment improves outcomes. The surviving property requires independent checking of content, semantics and use authority proportionate to consequence. A plausible diagram is not evidence of a real process. [S033-L1–L2; EBPM-052]

The 2026 agentic BPM manifesto proposes a research agenda for increasingly agentic process management, including modelling, execution and adaptation challenges. Its proposals are not already replicated field findings. Autonomous self-revision remains `UNRESOLVED` in this corpus: a system would need to connect evidence, delegated authority, intervention boundaries, ongoing obligations and independently assessed outcomes while objectives may change. This is an examined open question, not an unread mandatory family and not a recommendation for deployment. [S034-L1–L2; EBPM-053]

## 7. Why the surviving account is one conditional system

A mere union of properties would permit several contradictions: extensive modelling without an actor who can use it; powerful intervention without adequate evidence; efficiency improvement that shifts harm outside its measured boundary; and a simplified apparatus that destroys the accountability function it was intended to remove. The composed account rejects those combinations through explicit relations. Its 67 relations include requirements, enabling and constraining links, evidence and communication links, feedback, alternatives, conflicts, refinement and supersession.

The system’s smallest coherent form is deliberately modest: a meaningful purpose and boundary, enough actual-work evidence, an intelligible shared account, capable legitimate authority, proportionate action or reasoned non-action, and observation with a route for correction. A short narrative and existing management arrangement may satisfy it. Greater scale, interacting constraints, uncertainty or consequence can justify richer modelling, formal checking, simulation, dedicated governance or automation. Removing machinery is appropriate only when its consumer and protected function are absent or adequately replaced. [S005; S011; S020; S041; analytical EBPM-068–EBPM-071]

Five configurations preserve genuine alternatives: lightweight stewardship; structured repeatable work; goal- and case-oriented professional work; negotiated inter-organisational work; and evidence-intensive analytical intervention. They share a conditional core but do not require the simultaneous use of every technique. Discovery can recur during monitoring, an exception can interrupt enactment, a failed result can reopen the purpose boundary, and authorised work can proceed concurrently where dependencies permit. These are relationships among real activities and decisions, not simply movements through an internal information model.

The resulting judgement is a **conditional analytical synthesis with bounded source support**, not a universal optimisation theorem. Its value would be falsified in a proposed application if a cheaper arrangement supplies the protected functions equally well, if its observation and authority assumptions do not hold, or if its coordination burden outweighs the relevant benefit. The case tests below expose these possibilities; invented tests are explicitly not counted as empirical validation.

## EVOLVED_BUSINESS_PROCESS_MANAGEMENT_DOMAIN_MODELS

These ten models are the common interpretative frame for the complete ten-field `DOMAIN_PROFILE` in every property record. They specify real objects, actors, evidence, decisions, operations and feedback—not merely stage names.

### B1 — Process identity and purposes

**Question.** What received result makes this work a meaningful unit of intervention, and where does responsibility end?

**Objects.** Initiating needs, cases or work objects, activities, dependencies, recipients, completion claims and material external burdens.

**Actors.** Performers and recipients supply distinct accounts; sponsors and authorised owners decide scope and priorities; partners retain autonomous commitments.

**Evidence.** Complete-case examples, receiving-side outcomes, unmet needs and interfaces. An organisation chart contributes authority information but does not establish process identity.

**Decisions.** Identify a meaningful case/episode and completion rule; select which process merits attention; include or explicitly bound material effects; resolve or preserve competing purposes.

**Operation.** Change work allocation, commitments or the selected intervention scope. A portfolio or architecture only informs these acts; it does not perform them.

**Feedback.** Complaints, displaced delay, ambiguous closure and changed strategy can reopen purpose or boundary decisions.

**Limits.** Not all work has one stable repeated case. Open-ended inquiry may be managed through goals and responsibilities without heavy process standardisation. Expanding the boundary indefinitely is also a cost.

**Coupling.** A purpose criterion becomes operative when those receiving the outcome can inform an actor able to choose or change the work; a diagram alone cannot supply either condition.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-001, EBPM-002, EBPM-003, EBPM-004, EBPM-005, EBPM-032, EBPM-048, EBPM-067, EBPM-069

**Sources.** S001, S002, S005, S008, S028

### B2 — Discovery evidence and performed work

**Question.** Which aspects of actual work are warranted by which observations, and what remains unknown?

**Objects.** Prescribed procedures, remembered accounts, observed cases, event records, variants, informal coordination and disagreements.

**Actors.** Analysts elicit and compare accounts; practitioners and recipients validate or challenge them; data custodians explain recording conditions.

**Evidence.** Interviews expose reasons but can idealise practice; workshops reveal disagreement but can suppress weaker voices; observation exposes action but samples selectively; logs scale observation but inherit system boundaries.

**Decisions.** Choose evidence channels by the uncertainty that matters; sample relevant variants and missing participants; record unresolved disagreements rather than manufacture a single consensual as-is truth.

**Operation.** Conduct walkthroughs, observe cases, inspect documents and data generation, and return the representation to relevant participants. Discovery changes inquiry and shared understanding, not necessarily work itself.

**Feedback.** A workaround, recipient complaint or failed prediction can trigger new observation and boundary revision.

**Limits.** No channel is automatically ground truth. The inspected textbook discovery chapter was abstract-only, so detailed methodological claims here rely on primary cases and the other inspected sources.

**Coupling.** Evidence has a consumer: modellers, analysts and redesign decision-makers. The consumer must know whether a statement describes a prescription, a sample observation or an unresolved inference.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-006, EBPM-007, EBPM-008, EBPM-009, EBPM-037, EBPM-047, EBPM-062

**Sources.** S002, S025, S035, S041, S048

### B3 — Representation and communication

**Question.** What must these users distinguish, and which representation supports that purpose at acceptable cost?

**Objects.** Activities, events, decisions, control/data/resource relations, goals, cases, constraints and abstraction levels.

**Actors.** Domain participants judge meaning; modellers maintain representations; technical specialists check defined syntax and execution semantics.

**Evidence.** Comprehension and modelling experiments, domain walkthroughs and formal/specification checks establish different kinds of quality. A notation specification does not establish real-world accuracy.

**Decisions.** Choose a narrative, table, procedural model, decision representation or case/declarative form; decide whether a representation is communicative, analytical or intended for execution.

**Operation.** Participants explain and challenge model meanings; tools may validate syntax or encoded behaviour; separately authorised execution changes real work.

**Feedback.** User misunderstanding, semantic discrepancy, constraint conflict and changed work trigger selective revision.

**Limits.** The 7PMG null-result experiment does not prove guidelines useless, but defeats automatic sufficiency. Declarative satisfiability does not validate omitted obligations. DMN 1.5 was verified at version-record level only; detailed clause compliance is not claimed.

**Coupling.** Representation supports communication only when users can interpret consequential distinctions. Agreement on the picture may leave decision authority or exceptions unresolved.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-010, EBPM-011, EBPM-012, EBPM-013, EBPM-014, EBPM-015, EBPM-016, EBPM-052, EBPM-057, EBPM-063, EBPM-073

**Sources.** S004, S009, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014, S042, S044

### B4 — Performance and causal analysis

**Question.** Which mechanism produces the observed outcome, and how much can the available evidence justify changing it?

**Objects.** Elapsed time, processing effort, queues, resources, arrivals, case mix, rework, handoffs, costs and outcome distributions.

**Actors.** Analysts define measurements and comparisons; performers explain hidden work; owners decide resource and redesign interventions.

**Evidence.** Separate elapsed time from active work, description from prediction and prediction from causal effect. Simulation requires resource, arrival, routing and state assumptions; repeated simulation runs do not remove model error.

**Decisions.** Locate plausible bottlenecks or error mechanisms; compare alternative explanations; decide whether a cheap observation, calibrated model or experiment is needed.

**Operation.** Measure complete cases, inspect rework causes, build and check quantitative models where warranted, then make a separately authorised intervention.

**Feedback.** Unexpected outcomes, case-mix changes, resource changes or poor predictive calibration reopen the diagnosis.

**Limits.** High utilisation is not a universal objective. The inspected causal-discovery method uses a specific model and benchmark setting; real interventions need their own assumption checks and comparisons.

**Coupling.** A correct performance distinction only matters when its implications reach an actor able to change capacity, routing or working conditions; measured outcome change remains a separate claim.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-017, EBPM-018, EBPM-019, EBPM-020, EBPM-021, EBPM-022, EBPM-039, EBPM-059, EBPM-066

**Sources.** S015, S016, S017, S018, S019, S027

### B5 — Redesign and alternatives

**Question.** Which intervention changes the actual failure mechanism at acceptable transition and ongoing cost?

**Objects.** Current work, alternative arrangements, protective functions, dependencies, case classes, resources and transition commitments.

**Actors.** Owners, performers, specialists and affected recipients compare alternatives; sponsors authorise resources and disruption.

**Evidence.** An as-is baseline, explicit mechanism hypothesis and relevant comparison. Practitioner support for a heuristic supplies plausibility and use experience, not a universal causal effect.

**Decisions.** Choose among removal, resequencing, parallel work, coordination, pooling, segmentation, better information, automation, organisational repair and no intervention.

**Operation.** Actually alter tasks, responsibilities, information or resources; preserve necessary controls and manage transition.

**Feedback.** Adverse cases, failed adoption or absent outcome improvement can require reversal, a different alternative or retention of the original work.

**Limits.** Elimination can remove a control; parallelisation adds synchronisation; pooling creates queues and larger failure domains; segmentation can disadvantage hard cases. Radical and incremental change remain alternatives.

**Coupling.** The proposed mechanism, authorised change and its effect must all be evidenced. A more elegant to-be diagram is not an implemented improvement.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-023, EBPM-024, EBPM-025, EBPM-026, EBPM-027, EBPM-028, EBPM-029, EBPM-030, EBPM-055, EBPM-064, EBPM-065

**Sources.** S001, S002, S015, S016, S027, S030

### B6 — Organisational governance and capability

**Question.** Who can make consequential process decisions, with what competence, resources and accountability?

**Objects.** Decision rights, sponsorship, process ownership, functional responsibilities, support capabilities, incentives and escalation relations.

**Actors.** Sponsors allocate commitments; process owners coordinate outcomes; functional and professional managers retain explicitly assigned powers; BPM specialists enable or govern within their mandate.

**Evidence.** Actual decisions, resolved conflicts, resource commitments and demonstrated skills. Policies, titles and self-rated stages are supporting artefacts, not sufficient evidence of capability.

**Decisions.** Choose a governance configuration and support level; decide which capability gaps warrant investment; resolve cross-functional conflicts.

**Operation.** Delegate powers, supply resources and training, negotiate commitments and act on escalations.

**Feedback.** Persistent blocked decisions, local bypass, poor outcomes despite high ratings or changing dependencies reopen governance choices.

**Limits.** The six-element framework describes strategic alignment, governance, methods, IT, people and culture; it does not prove that all must grow through identical stages. Central, local and federated arrangements have different failure modes.

**Coupling.** Responsibility becomes operative through exercised authority and practical ability. Participation requires influence on decisions, not merely attendance.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-031, EBPM-032, EBPM-033, EBPM-034, EBPM-035, EBPM-056, EBPM-060, EBPM-070, EBPM-068

**Sources.** S005, S020, S021, S022, S023, S024, S043

### B7 — Human discretion, exceptions and responsibility

**Question.** When should a person follow, vary, challenge or stop the ordinary process, and who judges that departure?

**Objects.** Case circumstances, professional knowledge, binding constraints, optional work, workarounds, reasons and affected-party consequences.

**Actors.** Practitioners exercise bounded expertise; authorised reviewers adjudicate consequential departures; recipients and workers can challenge harmful designs or interpretations.

**Evidence.** Case narratives, observed exceptions, recipient experience and analysis of the protected function of both the rule and its workaround.

**Decisions.** Permit a justified departure, correct a misfit, preserve a necessary standard or reject unsafe circumvention.

**Operation.** Change the case path, supply expert judgement, stop unsafe action or resolve an exception while retaining outstanding responsibilities.

**Feedback.** Repeated justified exceptions can change a process; recurrent harm or unequal discretion can justify stronger constraints or training.

**Limits.** Neither universal conformance nor universal celebration of workarounds is retained. Visibility can enable accountability and also surveillance. The social-work criticism is abstract-only, limiting claims about its detailed empirical design.

**Coupling.** The system must let people communicate exceptional facts to someone able to act. An override button without authority, competence or actual control is not meaningful discretion.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-035, EBPM-036, EBPM-037, EBPM-038, EBPM-039, EBPM-054

**Sources.** S012, S014, S025, S026, S030, S041, S042

### B8 — Enactment, automation and external effects

**Question.** How does a design become functioning work, and what remains the organisation’s responsibility outside software?

**Objects.** Operational roles, training, systems, interfaces, deployments, actual cases, external effects and partial failures.

**Actors.** Owners coordinate enactment; technical teams implement bounded systems; performers and support teams make them usable; recipients and partners establish received consequences.

**Evidence.** Readiness and real-use observations, adoption, receiving-side completion and unresolved exceptions. Technical availability does not establish any of these by itself.

**Decisions.** Select a suitable technical or non-technical implementation, authorise external operations and determine how interrupted work will be resolved.

**Operation.** Perform work, communicate commitments, transfer objects or alter records. This is where real objects and relations change, not merely internal representations.

**Feedback.** Non-adoption, manual repair, interface changes, failed delivery or harmful automated decisions reopen design and governance.

**Limits.** BPM-level composition identifies recovery obligations without supplying the full workflow-engine algorithmic lane. Low-code usability evidence is not proof of non-specialist competence or safe deployment.

**Coupling.** A deployable workflow is a technical artefact. A functioning organisational process additionally needs skills, authority, resources, coordination and outcome evidence.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-014, EBPM-040, EBPM-041, EBPM-042, EBPM-043, EBPM-058, EBPM-072

**Sources.** S002, S011, S012, S027, S029, S044, S045, S046, S047

### B9 — Monitoring, adaptation and differentiated revision

**Question.** What observations can change which decisions, and how are descriptions, work and outcomes kept distinct?

**Objects.** Indicators, baselines, assumptions, model versions, actual practices, complaints, interventions and evaluation judgements.

**Actors.** Analysts supply signals; authorised owners or professionals decide action; performers and recipients supply evidence missing from system logs.

**Evidence.** Decision-linked measures, comparison with the intervention hypothesis and independent routes for omitted work to challenge the model.

**Decisions.** Investigate, act, refrain, retire a control, revise a model, change work or reconsider the objective. These are distinct decisions with different authority requirements.

**Operation.** Change resources, tasks or commitments where authorised; a representational correction alone does not alter operations.

**Feedback.** The revision itself is evaluated. Criteria may be reconsidered openly but not silently rewritten to declare the previous intervention successful.

**Limits.** Feedback does not prove convergence or self-authorisation. A dashboard with no capable consumer is not an adaptive process, while indiscriminate alerts can consume the capacity needed to act.

**Coupling.** Learning is a two-way relation: observations inform decisions and consequences revise the evidence model. Exceptions may interrupt any ordinary phase rather than waiting for a lifecycle stage.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-044, EBPM-045, EBPM-046, EBPM-047, EBPM-061, EBPM-071, EBPM-068

**Sources.** S004, S005, S018, S025, S027, S035, S036, S041

### B10 — Cross-organisational and contemporary consequences

**Question.** How can process coordination and innovation remain accountable across autonomous actors and new technical capabilities?

**Objects.** Shared outcomes, interface meanings, commitments, disclosure boundaries, environmental/social effects, analytical models and delegated automation.

**Actors.** Autonomous organisations negotiate commitments; affected people supply consequences; analysts and tool builders do not acquire network authority by integration.

**Evidence.** Field and design-science cases, surveys, technical benchmarks and research agendas are kept distinct. Reused datasets and case programmes do not create independent replications.

**Decisions.** Negotiate governance, determine necessary information sharing, include material impacts and select bounded use of intelligence or AI.

**Operation.** Coordinate real interorganisational commitments, test interventions and deliver consequences under separately justified authority.

**Feedback.** Partner disputes, displaced harm, data-semantic changes or failed AI/analytics assumptions trigger review.

**Limits.** The 2026 agentic manifesto is an agenda, not operational assurance. Sustainability readiness is not measured environmental benefit. The September 28–October 2, 2026 conference is future relative to the cut-off and is not treated as completed evidence.

**Coupling.** Interoperability, intelligence and visibility are enabling capabilities only. They do not by themselves settle interests, grant authority or establish benefit.

**Candidate links.** EBPM-048, EBPM-049, EBPM-050, EBPM-051, EBPM-052, EBPM-053, EBPM-069

**Sources.** S018, S019, S028, S030, S031, S032, S033, S034, S036, S039, S040

### Operational state, communication and consequential acts

The system distinguishes six kinds of state: the work and its external obligations; representations of that work; observations and their provenance; purposes and evaluation criteria; authorities and capacities; and proposed or implemented changes. These are analytical categories, not a prescribed software schema. Updating one does not silently update the others. A received complaint changes available evidence; an authorised resource allocation changes feasible work; an edited model changes a representation; a delivered service changes an external relation. A report of success establishes none of these automatically. [S002; S020; S027; S041; EBPM-068]

Performers and recipients communicate actual-case evidence to analysts and accountable actors. Analysts communicate uncertainty and alternative explanations, not just a recommended design. Functional managers, process owners, professionals or partner representatives commit resources and authorise changes only within their powers. Technical actors enact supported changes and report execution limits. Recipients, workers and partners can supply evidence missing from system logs. ### Four properties induced by composition

#### EBPM-068 — Coupling warranted distinctions to authorised effects

**Mechanism.** Keep three linked but non-equivalent claims explicit: evidence supports a distinction about work; an identified actor is authorised and able to intervene; observed consequences support an outcome judgement. **Gap prevented.** Analysis without agency; authority without warrant; action without benefit; excessive documentation of trivial links.

**Observable result.** A claim of improvement can be traced through warranted observation, an actual authorised intervention and its observed result, with missing links explicit.

**Adversarial limit.** The component distinctions have source support, but this exact composed condition has not been independently validated as a management programme.

#### EBPM-069 — Boundary-sensitive improvement acceptance

**Mechanism.** Accept an improvement claim only over its declared consequence boundary and protected outcomes. A local gain remains qualified when material receiving-side burdens are unknown; do not aggregate away a harmed group or silently change the boundary to rescue the claim.

**Gap prevented.** Infinite scope expansion, strategic nondisclosure, metric aggregation masking harm and excessive evaluation cost.

**Observable result.** The benefit claim is accepted, rejected or bounded to what the evidence supports, including unknown external effects.

**Adversarial limit.** This is an analytical acceptance condition, not evidence that all costs can be measured or all values reconciled.

#### EBPM-070 — Minimal sufficient portfolio of BPM machinery

**Mechanism.** Select the smallest set of BPM mechanisms that jointly covers the actual purpose, evidence, authority and feedback needs. Evaluate shared functions and dependencies before adding artefacts, roles or tools; omit or retire machinery whose function is already adequately supplied or no longer needed.

**Gap prevented.** False economy removes needed evidence; simplicity becomes dogma; overlapping controls are removed without understanding defence or accountability functions.

**Observable result.** The chosen configuration is coherent and justified, with omitted functions or unresolved dependencies explicit.

**Adversarial limit.** S041 shows apparently cumbersome documentation may serve a contractual consumer; S010 shows recommended form alone does not establish quality. Minimality is conditional, not a universal size target.

#### EBPM-071 — Coupled revision of representations and work

**Mechanism.** Allow feedback to change a representation, the real work, the measure or the intervention hypothesis, but distinguish these acts. **Adversarial limit.** This synthesis is not a claim that BPM methods are automatically self-authorising or that feedback converges to a globally optimal process.

### Loops, interrupts, concurrency and non-action

Discovery is revisited when monitoring or exceptions contradict the account of work. Performance analysis can send a proposed redesign back for more evidence rather than forward to implementation. A safety-relevant or otherwise consequential exception can interrupt normal enactment while an authorised person investigates; an interruption does not automatically entitle the interrupter to redesign the whole process. Dependencies determine where work may proceed concurrently. Beneficiary or partner evidence can reopen the process boundary itself. A no-change judgement is a valid output when the expected benefit is unsupported or a cheaper existing mechanism already suffices. These are analytical relations grounded in the cited constituent mechanisms, not a claim that a particular source specifies this exact network.

### CFG-LIGHT — Lightweight process stewardship

**Trigger.** Small, understandable work with limited interdependence and reversible consequences.

**Mechanism.** Use case examples and a short purpose/responsibility account, existing management authority, proportionate change or non-action and direct outcome review.

**Omitted By Default.** Formal architecture, simulation, mining, maturity assessment, BPM office, execution engine and low-code platform.

**Escalation Trigger.** Material hidden dependencies, repeated misunderstanding, consequential constraints or scale exceeds the cheap path.

**Retirement Condition.** No recurring decision need or the protected function is supplied adequately elsewhere.

**Cost.** Time for shared understanding and evidence remains; lightweight does not mean undocumented harm.

### Revision boundaries

#### REPRESENTATION

**Authority.** Relevant modeller/domain steward within delegated remit.

**Required Evidence.** A meaningful discrepancy or changed communication/analysis purpose.

**Must Not Infer.** Editing the model changes actual work or grants execution authority.

**Continuity.** Inform affected consumers where meaning changes.

#### OPERATIONS

**Authority.** Responsible process/functional/professional actors and partners within their powers.

**Required Evidence.** A warranted intervention hypothesis, feasibility and protected consequences.

**Must Not Infer.** A desirable diagram or model-generated recommendation is sufficient authorisation.

**Continuity.** Preserve or resolve ongoing cases and external obligations.

#### OBJECTIVES_OR_EVALUATION

**Authority.** Legitimate sponsors and affected-party governance, not the measured process alone.

**Required Evidence.** Reasons for changing purposes or protected constraints and explicit treatment of prior evaluation claims.

**Must Not Infer.** Changing a metric retroactively proves an earlier intervention successful.

**Continuity.** Keep old and new claim scopes distinguishable.

#### METHOD_OR_GOVERNANCE

**Authority.** Actors accountable for the relevant management arrangement.

**Required Evidence.** Observed burden, inadequacy or changed dependencies, with alternatives considered.

**Must Not Infer.** BPM is automatically self-authorising or converges to an optimum.

**Continuity.** Ensure necessary decisions and accountability remain supported while machinery is simplified or retired.

### Discriminating tests of the composed account

Nine tests are explicitly analytical hypotheticals. Five are examinations of published study/case findings. The latter retain the original studies’ validity limits; neither group validates the complete Evolved system as a field intervention.

#### A01 — ANALYTICAL_HYPOTHETICAL_FAVOURABLE_AND_CONFLICT

**Property Ids.** EBPM-001, EBPM-002, EBPM-017, EBPM-018, EBPM-028, EBPM-048, EBPM-069

**Scenario.** A department halves its recorded handling time by releasing incomplete cases to the next team; the recipient waits longer.

**Discriminating Test.** Compare complete-case elapsed time, downstream repair and received result rather than the first department’s stopwatch.

**Composition Judgement.** Reject an unqualified end-to-end improvement claim; consider better handoff information or retaining local work instead of rewarding displaced effort.

**Falsifier Or Revision Trigger.** Receiving-side evidence shows no extra burden and a better final outcome, in which case the local change may be accepted within that boundary.

**Evidence Limit.** Invented case, not a published experiment or effect-size estimate.

**Source Ids.** S002, S028

#### A02 — ANALYTICAL_HYPOTHETICAL_FAILED_OBSERVATION

**Property Ids.** EBPM-006, EBPM-007, EBPM-008, EBPM-009, EBPM-037, EBPM-047, EBPM-071

**Scenario.** An official model omits informal coordination that resolves conflicting customer information before a case ID exists.

**Discriminating Test.** Walk through actual cases with performers and recipients; compare activity before registration with system records.

**Composition Judgement.** Revise the discovery account and investigate the informal function before automating or suppressing it. The log alone cannot disprove the work.

**Falsifier Or Revision Trigger.** Evidence shows the coordination has no useful function or causes greater harm; then redesign or remove it rather than canonise the workaround.

**Evidence Limit.** Invented test inspired by, but not equated with, the PF2 literature.

**Source Ids.** S025, S041

#### A03 — ANALYTICAL_HYPOTHETICAL_EXCEPTION

**Property Ids.** EBPM-024, EBPM-029, EBPM-036, EBPM-038, EBPM-042, EBPM-043

**Scenario.** A practitioner pauses a standard route because new case facts show that the routine decision would harm the recipient.

**Discriminating Test.** Assess competence, authority, the protected constraint and actual consequence of the pause; compare both rule compliance and departure.

**Composition Judgement.** A justified manual exception can be a successful process response, not a failure merely because it reduces throughput.

**Falsifier Or Revision Trigger.** The pause lacks a defensible purpose, exceeds authority or produces greater avoidable harm; revise the exception policy or support.

**Evidence Limit.** No medical, legal or safety protocol is prescribed; this is a neutral hypothetical test.

## EVOLVED_BUSINESS_PROCESS_MANAGEMENT_CURRENT_STATE_AND_RESEARCH_FRONTIER

The recent evidence is not treated as a single wave of confirmed progress. The 2024–2026 corpus includes original studies, conceptual work, surveys, a doctoral agenda and a manifesto. Governance taxonomies describe viable distinctions; current-state simulation improves selected predictive tasks but has counter-results; workaround inquiry develops a practical method without establishing representative effect sizes; LLM benchmarks establish bounded task performance; sustainability studies chiefly supply associations; agentic BPM remains a research proposition. [S018; S021; S025; S030–S034; S040; S044–S047]

The frontier questions are therefore concrete: how to identify intervention effects rather than merely predict variants; how to make incomplete observations and unlogged work visible; how to preserve professional judgement while enforcing necessary constraints; how to evaluate governance without rewarding paperwork; how to allocate network costs and authority; and how to constrain learned assistance or autonomous adaptation with independently evaluated consequences. Evidence that only establishes syntax, adoption, intention, prediction or a survey association does not settle these questions.

At the cut-off, the advertised 2026 BPM conference remained future. This report makes no claim to have inspected its future proceedings. Later publication or operational developments can revise the corpus; freezing fixes this revision’s evidence and judgements, not the future of the field. [S039-L1]

## EVOLVED_BUSINESS_PROCESS_MANAGEMENT_ADVERSARIAL_SYNTHESIS_VERDICT

**Verdict: retain a conditional core with alternative configurations; reject universal machinery and guaranteed improvement.** The strongest core joins purpose, actual-work evidence, communicable understanding, effective authority and consequence-sensitive revision. Its parts have historical, conceptual, formal or field support of different kinds. The exact network is an analytical synthesis and lacks direct comparative deployment validation as a whole.

The strongest objection to unification is that “BPM” covers diverse objects and powers: a stable routine, an evolving professional case and a strategic network cannot be made equivalent by one lifecycle drawing. The response is not to require every technique but to retain explicit guards and alternatives. A second objection is bureaucratic accretion: the study’s many distinctions could generate needless apparatus. EBPM-070 makes omission, substitution and retirement part of the system itself. A third is managerial capture: a sponsor’s definition of value may exclude those bearing the cost. EBPM-002, EBPM-038 and EBPM-069 preserve the boundary and legitimacy question rather than solving it with a single metric.

The system is unnecessary where a simpler existing practice already supports the consequential decisions and corrections. Its claimed payoff is defeated where it produces no better outcome or uncertainty reduction than that alternative, where its actors cannot exercise the required powers, where its model or data omit decisive conditions, or where its overhead and harms dominate. It remains unable to guarantee agreement among legitimate interests, reliable causal identification from insufficient observations, correct encoding of all human obligations or autonomous convergence to better objectives. These are not defects concealed by a favourable retention rate.

The null guideline experiment changes EBPM-012 and supersedes EBPM-063; the implementation denominator and mixed workflow outcomes reject EBPM-058 as a sufficient claim; maturity criticism rejects EBPM-056 and limits EBPM-034; enacted-work evidence rejects EBPM-062 as unqualified truth; formal constraint checking is separated as domain-specific EBPM-073; the 2026 manifesto leaves EBPM-053 unresolved. Adverse evidence therefore has observable effects on the ledger.
