## Native concepts and within-tradition reconstruction

BPM contributes process purpose and boundary, end-to-end received outcome, work-as-performed discovery, process architecture, purpose-fit representations, performance and redesign hypotheses, effective ownership, organisational capabilities, discretion and exception handling, enactment, outcome monitoring and revision. Its organisational and information-systems branches overlap without a single linear succession. Queueing, simulation, event-based mining, RPA and low-code have documented external relationships; their presence is not evidence that BPM invented them. [S001–S005; S008; S017; S020; S029; S035; S043; S045]

**Label Status.** RESEARCHER_ANALYTICAL_SYNTHESIS_NOT_AN_ESTABLISHED_SCHOOL_NAME

**Purpose.** Make the identification, understanding, organisation, intervention and revision of end-to-end work coherent while preserving distinctions between warranted knowledge, legitimate/effective action and observed consequences.

**Environment.** Organisations and cooperating or partly autonomous partners, with variable work, incomplete observation, resource constraints, human judgement and competing legitimate interests.

**Objects And State.** Needs, cases/work objects, obligations, real work, resources, responsibilities, process representations, observations, hypotheses, interventions and received consequences. Their states are related but not interchangeable.

**Actors.** Recipients and affected people; performers and professionals; analysts/modellers; owners/functional managers; sponsors; technical/support actors; autonomous partners. Roles may be combined where competence and authority permit.

**Operational Meaning.** People and systems perform tasks, commit resources, make decisions, exchange messages and alter real records/objects/obligations. Models support these acts but do not create authority or establish their success.

**Control Form.** A conditional relation network with recurring inquiry, alternative representations and interventions, concurrent work where justified, and exception/unsafe-continuation interrupts. No universal fixed stage order is prescribed.

**Minimal Coherent Form.** A meaningful purpose and boundary; enough evidence of actual work to support the decision; an intelligible shared account; an existing or explicitly agreed capable authority; proportionate action or reasoned non-action; observation of consequences and a route for correction. No mandatory diagram, engine, mining system, maturity assessment or BPM office.

**Induced Properties.** EBPM-068, EBPM-069, EBPM-070, EBPM-071

**Validation Status.** The components have distinct source support; this exact composition is an analytical construction tested by counterexamples, not an empirically validated universal BPM programme.

### CFG-LIGHT — Lightweight process stewardship

**Trigger.** Small, understandable work with limited interdependence and reversible consequences.

**Mechanism.** Use case examples and a short purpose/responsibility account, existing management authority, proportionate change or non-action and direct outcome review.

**Omitted By Default.** Formal architecture, simulation, mining, maturity assessment, BPM office, execution engine and low-code platform.

**Escalation Trigger.** Material hidden dependencies, repeated misunderstanding, consequential constraints or scale exceeds the cheap path.

**Retirement Condition.** No recurring decision need or the protected function is supplied adequately elsewhere.

**Cost.** Time for shared understanding and evidence remains; lightweight does not mean undocumented harm.

### CFG-ROUTINE — Structured repeatable process

**Trigger.** Stable case identity, repeated tasks and a credible benefit from shared representation or automation.

### Reconciliation tests for later integration

Semantic equivalence requires the same controlled failure, trigger, postcondition and assumption boundary—not a shared label such as “monitoring”. Complementarity requires an actual evidence, communication or action connection. Redundancy concerns mechanisms, not automatic deletion of source histories. Abstraction differences must distinguish organisational outcome criteria from technical trace properties. Incompatibility must preserve concrete guards, such as fixed-case versus evolving-case assumptions or central authority versus autonomous partners. Shared mechanisms may satisfy several candidates, but their new integration and maintenance costs must be examined.

For Workflow Management, determine which execution, exception, history and completion claims concern a formal model, an implemented engine or actual organisational work. Ask whether an existing mechanism supplies the needed guarantee rather than assuming a new one. For Process Mining, determine what event/case assumptions support a finding, how missing work could challenge it, and what additional causal, authority and outcome conditions are necessary before intervention. These questions are not answered for the unread sibling corpora.
