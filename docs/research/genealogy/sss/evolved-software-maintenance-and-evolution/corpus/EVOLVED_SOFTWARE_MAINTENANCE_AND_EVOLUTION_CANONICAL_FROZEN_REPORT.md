## C. Reconciliation method and authority of the records

A canonical property is an independently discriminable criterion: a bearer, failure, mechanism, trigger, supported observation, consumer and authority boundary. Different titles do not create different criteria; similar titles do not establish equivalence. The reconciliation ledger compares the original problem, failure, historical/original/mature form, mechanism, trigger, cheaper path, prerequisites, domain, payoff, consumer, authority, interaction, postcondition, failures, criticisms, assumptions, evidence partitions, sources, supersession, omission and open questions. Exact original values remain inspectable. The canonical rationale states why overlap was merged, a bundle split, a residual retained or an analytical claim absorbed.

A broader input may contribute different facets to multiple rows. For example, A's maintenance-preparation row contributes both consequential non-code scope and readiness; its partial-oracle row contributes differential and metamorphic mechanisms; its data-transition row contributes authority and meaning-preserving mapping. B separately bundles slicing/comprehension, documentation/rationale, concurrent integration/conflict detection, learned dependencies/drift, and evidence binding/invalidation. Eight input rows therefore have two canonical targets. A split does not license applying all original source claims indiscriminately to each facet.

Several inputs converge on one criterion. Initial handover and later knowledge succession share demonstrated recipient capability under different temporal guards. Historical/API-specific impact accounts support one typed impact boundary without making all inference methods identical. Several refactoring-benefit rows converge on task-specific outcome and total cost, while engine validation remains distinct from checking the actual transformed candidate. A checked output is not evidence that every operation of an engine is sound; an engine theorem or test suite does not establish that a particular candidate meets every supported observer.

Disposition is adjudicated separately from semantic identity. The ledger records the losing or narrowed judgement rather than averaging labels. “Retained” does not override an assumption-sensitive empirical claim, nor does an adverse example prove universal uselessness. There are 33 canonical groups with differing contributing input dispositions and 42 input rows with at least one changed canonical disposition. Only 3 input rows change the binary conditional-eligibility judgment. These are overlapping counts, not additive causes of the denominator.

The canonical interface controls. Original variants are scoped evidence and alternative realisations, not an implicit conjunction of all practices. Source ancestry likewise is not a proof blanket: claim locators and access limits remain attached to the exact A or B record. No new original-source access was undertaken. ### D2. Evolution is an empirical programme, not a growth command

Belady and Lehman's historical programme, later system categories and feedback formulations concern changing software in changing use and environments. The categorical and empirical scope matters: a finding about a population, measure and time window is not an instruction that every useful system must grow, be rewritten or consume a fixed work rate. Linux, FreeBSD and glibc studies and later reviews add counterpressure through different measures and populations. Multiple analyses of Linux do not become independent software populations; a plateau does not refute every formulation, and rapid growth does not create a new universal law. C008–C012 separate feedback, scoped empirical claims, capability and rejected universalisations. [ESME-CS008 (A:ESME-S008); ESME-CS009 (A:ESME-S009); ESME-CS010 (A:ESME-S010); ESME-CS011 (A:ESME-S011); ESME-CS012 (A:ESME-S012); ESME-CS079 (B:ESME-S029); ESME-CS100 (B:ESME-S060); ESME-CS101 (B:ESME-S061)]

The staged lifecycle account distinguishes evolution, restricted servicing, phaseout and closedown. Its strong irreversibility implication is preserved as contested C082 rather than accepted as a necessary law or dismissed without argument. Loss of knowledge and practical capacity is consequential; this corpus does not prove that capability can never be restored or that restoration is generally affordable. Continued useful non-change and deliberately restricted servicing remain legitimate configurations. [ESME-CS013 (A:ESME-S013)]

### D6. Evidence is partial, action-relative and subject to invalidation

The oracle survey, partial-oracle techniques and repair-quality studies explain why passing tests can be useful yet insufficient. Differential agreement may share an error; a metamorphic relation must be justified; mutation results depend on the fault and test setting. These are alternative evidence mechanisms, not a mandatory stack. [ESME-CS025 (A:ESME-S025); ESME-CS099 (B:ESME-S059); ESME-CS102 (B:ESME-S063); ESME-CS103 (B:ESME-S064); ESME-CS049 (A:ESME-S048); ESME-CS034 (A:ESME-S034)]

Safe-selection claims remain model- and suite-relative, and analysis overhead may make a full run preferable. The 1999 prioritisation paper and 2001 journal record are not one access record; A's abstract-level account cannot certify unread detailed findings. CI evidence must also account for the actual decision, time and cost. [ESME-CS026 (A:ESME-S026); ESME-CS047 (A:ESME-S046); ESME-CS048 (A:ESME-S047); ESME-CS105 (B:ESME-S066); ESME-CS098 (B:ESME-S058)]

## H. Composition-induced reconciliation and the six required overlap tests

Four induced criteria survive. Their premises are existing source-grounded or already analytical mechanisms, and their residual-failure witnesses are analytical counterexamples. They are not attributed historically to a source or advertised as integrated comparative evidence.

**C102 — Cross-stage change–evidence–action coherence.** A-093 and the binding facet of B-072 share a failure: individually legitimate intent, correct impact evidence and passing checks can describe different changes or subjects at their handoff. Obligation alignment and action identity become one coherent intervention-binding criterion; the input fields are not discarded. It is discharged by an adequate existing local binding, not necessarily a new contract document. Parents include C001, C005, C022, C030, C038, C033, C039 and C094; relations CR057, CR058 and CR002 express the coupling.

**C103 — Assumption-indexed evidence invalidation.** A-094 and the validity facet of B-072 match. B-072 explicitly includes changed assumptions, not just a candidate identifier. Keep the identifier unchanged but change the relied-on environment, observer or tool: C102 can hold while evidence no longer applies. Conversely, current evidence can concern the wrong intervention. This discriminates C103 from C102. Invalidation follows consequential dependencies selectively; it does not require global revalidation. Parents and dependency edges are recorded in CR059, CR055 and CR049.

**C104 — Obligation continuity across reachable joint states.** B-073 retains a distinct residual only where existing checks cover separate projections or endpoints rather than their consequential combinations. An edit pair checked under an old schema and a schema path checked with the old edits can join into a new failure. Existing adequate joint checks, proven disjointness or quiescence can already discharge the requirement. It is not a mandate for exhaustive state modelling. Its parents are C028, C057, C053, C055 and C056, with CR060, CR061 and CR036.

**A-095 versus B-073:** recovery optionality is not identical to acceptable joint operation.
