## CEREMONIES_TO_NOT_BLINDLY_ADOPT

- Human approval clicks, nominal override buttons or passive monitoring without capability-backed intervention.
- A situation-awareness dashboard, common operating picture or mode annunciator treated as proof of understanding.
- NASA-TLX, SAGAT, trust scores or team-cognition scores treated as direct safety/performance controls.
- Named CWA, EID, CRM, I-PASS or checklist packages copied without the triggering work-system failure and implementation conditions.
- Second signatures, agent votes or repeated prompts treated as independent verification without source/method independence.
- Explanation panels, saliency maps or chain-of-thought displays treated as faithful/actionable transparency by default.
- More alerts, higher sensitivity or acknowledgement counts treated as awareness or resolution.
- Manual-fallback documentation treated as evidence of takeover readiness.
- Levels-of-automation labels treated as allocation or coordination design.
- Human-error labels, procedure-compliance counts or training attendance treated as causal closure.

## CONTEXTS_WHERE_PROPERTY_SHOULD_NOT_TRIGGER

- Transparent, reversible, low-consequence, single-actor operations with immediate trustworthy world-effect feedback.
- Stateless or single-mode deterministic functions for which mode/intent infrastructure would add no decision value.
- Same-context handoffs where sender and receiver share directly visible live state and responsibility never becomes ambiguous.
- Decisions where a second reviewer or explanation cannot add independent discriminating evidence before the deadline.
- Alerts for conditions with no live capable recipient or action; use passive status, logging or architectural protection instead.
- Human takeover claims that cannot meet orientation and recovery timing; the correct response is to remove the human fallback claim, not add more procedure.
- Full CWA/EID programmes where a small state model, invariant list or direct constraint cue closes the material failure mode.
- Independence protocols that withhold authoritative facts, known hazards or constraints needed for competent review.

## HUMAN_CAPABILITY_PRECONDITIONS

- Current role-relevant competence, including rare recovery skills where retained.
- Independent or sufficiently discriminating evidence rather than only the automation output being approved.
- Attention and workload reserve at the moment intervention or verification is required.
- Accessible and usable controls, stop paths, undo/compensation and escalation.
- Authority and organisational protection to challenge, defer, stop or deviate within governed boundaries.
- Enough time for detection, state/model reacquisition, judgement, action and stabilisation.
- Current knowledge of the deployed version, mode, limits, degradation and changed policies.
- Recurrent demonstrated readiness when natural task exposure is too rare to maintain capability.

## STATE_MODE_LEGIBILITY_PRECONDITIONS

- Identity and scope of the system, action, datum and authority state.
- Current versus pending, armed, committed, executing, completed, failed, reverted and degraded states where relevant.
- Mode consequences, active intent/target, constraints, likely near-term behaviour and available interventions.
- Freshness/as-of time, latency and update semantics tied to the actual claim, not merely display refresh.
- Provenance/source/transform lineage and distinction among measured, inferred, reported and model-generated state.
- Uncertainty, missingness and degraded capability at the granularity that changes action.
- Command receipt separated from execution and intended world-state confirmation.
- Role-specific access before the decision or recovery deadline, with an explicit cheap path where direct perception suffices.

## AUTHORITY_HANDOFF_PROPERTIES

- ECSE-11 aligns authority, responsibility, observability and capability.
- ECSE-12 rejects oversight without real intervention capability.
- ECSE-13 transfers state, authority and pending obligations with acceptance and verification.
- ECSE-14 validates takeover/re-entry as a timed capability, not a button.
- ECSE-30 provides mutual observability, predictability and directability for interdependent work.
- ECSE-36 makes timing, expiry and pending obligations explicit.
- ECSE-37 verifies degraded-mode authority and fallback independence.
- ECSE-38 requires discriminating evidence and blocking authority for independent checks.

## OUT_OF_LOOP_AND_TAKEOVER_RISKS

- Vigilance decrement and passive monitoring burden.
- Loss of manual or diagnostic skill and stale process models.
- Startle, surprise and attentional tunnelling at transfer.
- Time required to discover why automation is handing back control.
- Reacquisition of mode, trajectory, pending actions, degraded capabilities and environmental state.
- Authority ambiguity and automation behaviour during the handback interval.
- Fallback dependence on the same failed sensor, data, model or organisational resource.
- Recovery-quality and stabilisation time omitted by simple takeover-time metrics.
- Rare plans and training scenarios not exercised on the current deployed configuration.

## Evidence-bound audit questions

- Does the target system make current mode, pending action, authority, uncertainty and freshness legible before consequential action rather than only after it?
- Is a human approval or override step backed by actual timely observability and intervention capability, or is it ceremonial human-in-the-loop theatre?
- When automation performs nominal work, what preserves human readiness for rare takeover or proves takeover is not required?
- Does a handoff distinguish transmitted claims from independently verified live state?
- Are repeated reviewers or agents independent sources of discriminating evidence, or were they anchored to one favoured hypothesis?
- Does another dashboard, alert, report or explanation have a live decision consumer, or would it only add cognitive load?
- Which residual monitoring, verification, exception and recovery tasks remain after automation, and are they staffed and timed realistically?
- Does authority follow actual capability and state access, or only documented responsibility?
- Can the system demonstrate command-to-world effect closure rather than merely command acceptance?
- Are stale procedures, alerts, models and displays retired when their state, system version or decision consumer changes?
