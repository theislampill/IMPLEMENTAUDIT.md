# Evolved Multi-Agent Systems — public-documentation intake

Revision `EMAS-R1`; research cut-off 2026-09-06. “Evolved Multi-Agent Systems” is an analytical synthesis, not a claimed historical school. This document supports accurate public explanation of the external tradition; it does not assert adoption by any host system.

## Introduction and genealogy

Multi-Agent Systems studies interactions among agents whose information, capabilities, control or objectives can be distributed. It includes cooperative, competitive and mixed-motive settings, and does not require human-like cognition or natural-language conversation. Its plural ancestry includes distributed problem solving, communication and teamwork theories, organisational/normative systems, and documented imports from economics and AI logic. [EMAS-S001, weak/strong agency and social aspects; EMAS-S005, task/result sharing; EMAS-S023, foundational taxonomy]

Canonical examined developments include Smith’s 1980 Contract Net task-connection protocol, Durfee and Lesser’s 1987 Partial Global Planning, joint-intention and SharedPlans accounts of collaboration, operational teamwork, and public-commitment approaches to communication. Distributed constraint optimisation, multi-agent learning and contemporary human/LLM-agent experiments extend different branches rather than forming one inevitable progress sequence. The community’s AAMAS conference began in 2002 through the merger of AGENTS, ICMAS and ATAL. [EMAS-S002, community history; EMAS-S004; EMAS-S006–010; EMAS-S013]

## Evolution under criticism

Criticism has exposed mismatches between message syntax and meaning, allocation and performance, private intention and public accountability, norm declaration and enforcement, and local learning gains and collective outcomes. Cost and validity limits also matter: a larger team or a distributed optimiser can be worse than a feasible simpler alternative. Modern evidence must distinguish independent information from repeated messages, training cost from inference cost, and a field trial from deployment or causal benefit. [EMAS-S013; EMAS-S020–021; EMAS-S028; EMAS-S035–043]

Maturity can therefore mean a narrower protocol, a partial plan, selective communication, a bounded failure model or an omitted mechanism. It does not necessarily mean more agents, paperwork, monitoring or formalism. This is the present report’s synthesis of the evidence, not a claim that the field unanimously endorses one methodology.

## The composed account

The proposed within-tradition composition starts with a real interdependence and chooses compatible mechanisms under explicit information, incentive, authority and failure assumptions. It keeps private plans, public obligations, material actions and observed consequences distinct, while linking them where actual operation requires it. Planning, acting, communicating and monitoring can occur concurrently; failure or changed assumptions can cause repair, renegotiation, human decision or termination.

Its smallest form may be a direct authorised interaction with a clear outcome and observation path. Richer cooperative-team, strategic-market, distributed-optimisation, adaptive or organisational configurations are alternatives, not mandatory layers. Four additional analytical properties describe the composition burden: observation–obligation–action closure, regime-change handoff, minimum adequate coordination, and dependency-coupled adaptation. No experiment has validated this entire composed account as a single system.

## Strongest properties and their limits

The robust core makes boundaries, objectives, information and real powers explicit; distinguishes syntactic/protocol success from completed work; tests collective consequences rather than local appearance; preserves assumptions when members or policies change; and compares against credible cheaper alternatives. Formal guarantees remain within their models. A norm model does not confer external legitimacy, an equilibrium does not imply a desirable outcome, and a majority does not provide independent ground truth.

The most misleading ceremonies are proliferating named agents, compulsory debate or negotiation, unconsumed role charts, norm text without enforceable effects, and approval or explanation displays that do not change a real decision. Their presence can be useful, but presence alone is not the property. ## Suggested page outline

A public page can first define the established field and its plural history; then explain the motivating interdependence; introduce communication, commitments, strategic and organisational alternatives; show how operations and observations connect; explain criticism and cheaper forms; and finish with evidence limits, current research questions and exact source references. A compact example should be labelled analytical unless it is a sourced published case.

## Claims not to make

Do not invent a historical origin or consensus for the “Evolved” label. Do not claim that all MAS is decentralised, human-like or LLM-based. Do not imply that more agents, messages or roles guarantee capability, resilience or independence. Do not describe protocol legality, agreement, norm text or task awards as proof of real successful action. Do not detach formal guarantees from their assumptions or treat simulated institutional powers as legitimate external authority.

Do not promote isolated examples into universal economic or safety benefits, count repeated programme reports as independent replications, equate popularity with maturity, or turn exploratory/null findings into confirmed effects or equivalence. Do not claim host-system adoption, full cross-tradition reconciliation or evaluated end-to-end effectiveness on the basis of this intake.

## Current frontier without hype

The inspected frontier includes unseen-partner coordination, communication/attention trade-offs, correlated failure, adversarial incentives, bounded fault tolerance, dynamic organisations and norms, and better comparisons of language-agent collectives. The packet preserves positive, negative and null findings. It also challenges an overly strong information-loss argument for universal single-agent superiority, using an explicitly labelled researcher counterexample rather than presenting it as a published correction. The result is a conditional engineering account, not a forecast that one architecture will dominate every task.
