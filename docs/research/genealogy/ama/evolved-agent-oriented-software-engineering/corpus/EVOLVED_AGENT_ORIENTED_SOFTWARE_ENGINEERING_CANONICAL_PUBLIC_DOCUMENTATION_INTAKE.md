# Evolved Agent-Oriented Software Engineering — public-documentation intake

Agent-Oriented Software Engineering (AOSE) is an established research tradition and family of development approaches using agents and their interactions as requirements, analysis, design and implementation abstractions. Its genealogy includes intentional programming and BDI foundations, actor/goal/dependency requirements, role/organisation design, interaction/commitment engineering, named methods such as Gaia, Tropos, MaSE/O-MaSE and Prometheus, and model, language and tool support. AOSE is broader than an agent runtime and different from merely asking a software-generating agent to write code. [EAOSE-CS018; EAOSE-CS023; EAOSE-CS049; EAOSE-CS022; EAOSE-CS032; EAOSE-CS024; EAOSE-CS055; EAOSE-CS029]

“Evolved AOSE” is the analytical synthesis in this canonical research packet, not the historical name of a universally accepted school. The synthesis preserves criticism, alternative configurations and bounded evidence. It connects legitimate stakeholder needs to selected abstractions, roles, public interactions, executable behaviour, observed consequences and accountable change. Conventional software remains a legitimate configuration; agent-oriented analysis does not require an agent runtime. No host-system adoption is established. [EAOSE-C012; EAOSE-C013; canonical composition]

## Evolution under criticism

Requirements diagrams can miss affected stakeholders. Generated artefacts can preserve syntax while changing meaning. Formal checks depend on models and environmental/implementation assumptions. Public compliance cannot generally be established from inaccessible private mental states. Rich methods can impose burdens without usable prescriptions, and design coverage does not automatically supply operations. Contemporary role prompts and generated plans therefore retain external authority, interface, evidence and lifecycle obligations rather than escaping them. [EAOSE-CS044; EAOSE-CS014; EAOSE-CS068; EAOSE-CS072; EAOSE-CS047; EAOSE-CS034; EAOSE-CS079; EAOSE-CS096]

## Composition and evidence

The canonical synthesis has 132 examined properties, including negative and uncertain candidates, and 124 bibliographic work/edition records. These are accounting denominators, not effect sizes or numbers of independent experiments. The canonical model offers 10 guarded configurations rather than a compulsory serial methodology. No distinct induced property survives: the proposed decision-level coupling is retained as an analytical relation bundle over inherited compatibility, acceptance, authority, implementation and effect criteria. Where a direct contract/result check already preserves the relation, additional machinery is unnecessary.

Whole-method superiority remains unresolved in two scopes: broad complete AOSE methodologies, and this newly constructed Evolved-AOSE configuration family. Local application or benchmark success does not settle either question. Absence of superiority evidence is not evidence that every mechanism is inferior. Publication, platform popularity and adoption are not interchangeable with engineering benefit. [EAOSE-C120; EAOSE-C119; EAOSE-C115; EAOSE-C118]

## Suggested page structure

Start with AOSE's established subject and boundaries. Explain its plural genealogy and major methods. Present how criticisms change the mechanisms and conditions. Show the conventional and specialised configuration alternatives. State the evidence/whole-method limits and the analytical status of induced claims. Link the complete candidate ledger, not merely the strongest properties.

## Claims not to make

Do not invent a single origin, universal consensus or complete inheritance across all named methods. Do not call role prompts formal contracts, model generation requirements validation, local checks global acceptance, beneficial effects legitimate authority, or self-revision automatic improvement. Do not turn many sources, agents, diagrams or repeated reports into independent evidence. Do not describe this packet as proof that any particular system has adopted, needs, or benefits from the synthesis.
